=== TOPIC indian-economy:15 | External sector - BoP, forex and exchange rate

## Balance of Payments | from: 25, 52-2
# 1. BoP vs BoT
| Basis | Balance of Trade | Balance of Payments |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | **Goods only** — **visible trade**; **records only goods** | **All international economic transactions** — **goods**, **services**, **transfers** and **capital transactions** |
| Formula / scope | **Exports of goods** **minus** **imports of goods** | The **broader external account** |
| Place | **Part** of the **Current Account** | **Current Account** and **Capital/financial Account** transactions |
- **Balance of Payments:** A **systematic** and **complete record** of **all economic transactions** between the **residents** of a **country** and the **rest of the world** during a **specified period** — **normally one year**
- **BoT surplus:** **Goods exports** **greater** than **goods imports**; **BoT deficit:** **goods imports** **greater** than **goods exports**
# 2. Current Account
- **Includes** **goods**, **services**, **income** and **transfers**
- **Visible trade:** **Exports and imports** of **goods**
- **Invisible transactions:** **Services**, **transfer payments** and **net income**
- **Private remittances:** **Included** in the **Current Account**
- **Current Account Deficit:** **Payments exceed receipts** — from **goods**, **services**, **transfers** and **net income**
# 3. Capital Account
- **Cross-border capital movements** — **foreign loans**, **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**, **portfolio investment**, **international purchases** and **sales** of **assets**
# 4. Convertibility
- **Current Account Convertibility:** **Freedom** to **convert** **domestic currency** into **foreign currency** — for **current international transactions**
- **Capital Account Convertibility:** **Freedom** to conduct **investment** and **financial-asset transactions** **without capital controls**

## Foreign trade | from: 52-2, 25
# 1. Basic terms
- **Exports:** **Goods and services** **sold** to the **rest of the world**; **imports:** **purchased** from the **rest of the world**
- **Net exports:** **X − M** — **positive** net exports: a **trade surplus**; **negative:** a **trade deficit**
- **Trade surplus:** **Exports greater** than **imports**; **trade deficit:** **imports greater** than **exports**
- **Closed economy:** **Neither exports nor imports**
# 2. Invisible exports
- **Services** are **invisible exports** — **invisible exports** **mainly consist** of **services**
- **Examples** of **services exports:** **IT/software**, **tourism**, **banking**, **insurance**, **financial services**, **advertising**
- **Foreign tourist spending** in **India** is an **export of services** — an **invisible export**
# 3. Imports
- **Major-import hierarchy:** **Fuel** > **Petroleum, Oil & Lubricants** > **Capital Goods** > **Chemicals** > **Gold/Silver**

## Foreign exchange reserves and SDRs | from: 43
# 1. Components of India's reserves
- **Foreign Currency Assets (FCA)**; **gold**; **Special Drawing Rights (SDRs)**; the **Reserve Tranche Position (RTP)** with the **IMF**
# 2. SDR
- **Special Drawing Rights** — an **international reserve asset** **created** by the **IMF** in **1969** (an **IMF-created reserve asset**)
- **Created** to **supplement** **member countries' official reserves** — known as **paper gold**
- **Defined** in terms of **gold**; **redefined** in terms of a **basket of currencies** **after** the **collapse** of the **Bretton Woods System** in **1973**
# 3. Reserve Tranche Position
- A **reserve position** with the **IMF** — a **member's unconditional drawing position**
- **Historically called** the **gold tranche**

## Exchange rate | from: 47
# 1. Concepts
- **Exchange rate:** The **price** or **value** of **one currency** **expressed** in **terms** of **another currency**
- **Flexible** or **floating exchange rate:** The **market determines** the **currency value**
# 2. Four terms
| Term | System | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Devaluation** | **Fixed** | An **official reduction** — an **officially reduced** **currency value** |
| **Revaluation** | **Fixed** | An **officially increased** **currency value** |
| **Appreciation** | **Flexible** | A **market-driven rise** in **currency value** |
| **Depreciation** | **Flexible** | A **market-driven fall** in **currency value** |
- A **change** from **$1 = ₹60** to **$1 = ₹58** **represents** **rupee appreciation**; a **change** from **$1 = ₹60** to **$1 = ₹65** **represents** **rupee depreciation**
# 3. Devaluation
- Makes the **domestic currency cheaper** — **domestic exports** **relatively cheaper abroad**; **imports** **relatively expensive**
- **May improve export competitiveness** — ⚠ does **not necessarily improve** the **trade balance**
- **Rupee devaluations:** **1949** (the **first**), **1966**, **1991**
  - The **1991 devaluation** — **devalued twice** — in **two steps**: on **July 1** and **July 3**
# 4. NEER and REER
- **Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER):** A **weighted average** of **bilateral nominal exchange rates** — **against** a **basket** of **trading-partner currencies**
- **Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER):** The **NEER** **adjusted** for **relative price** or **inflation differentials** — **considers differences** between **domestic** and **foreign economies**
- ⚠ An **increase in NEER** does **not indicate appreciation** in the **cited convention**; an **increase in REER** does **not automatically mean** **better competitiveness**
# 5. Influences
- **Demand and supply** influence the exchange rate; **inflation** and a **fiscal deficit** can **affect** the **exchange value**
- ⚠ The **World Bank** does **not determine** the **currency price**
