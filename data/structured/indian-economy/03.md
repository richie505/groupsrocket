=== TOPIC indian-economy:3 | Economic reforms, industrial policy and CPSEs

## The 1991 reforms — LPG | from: 39, 37
# 1. Background
- The **1991 New Economic Policy** was **introduced** to **address** the **severe 1991 Balance of Payments crisis**
- **LPG model:** **Liberalisation**, **Privatisation** and **Globalisation** — the **New Industrial Policy** was **associated** with **LPG**; **LPG reforms** were **associated** with **multinational corporations**
# 2. Stabilisation vs structural adjustment
| Basis | Stabilisation | Structural Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Term | **Short-term** — **strictly short-term** | **Long-term** |
| Nature | **Demand-side** | **Supply-side** |
| Addresses | **Inflation** and **macroeconomic imbalances** | **Industrial**, **trade**, **financial** and **investment structures** |
# 3. Liberalisation vs globalisation
| Liberalisation | Globalisation |
|---|---|
| **Removes** or **reduces domestic controls** — **reduces restrictions**; **focuses** on **removing state controls** | **Integrates** the **domestic economy** with the **world economy** |
| **De-licensing** | Associated: **international trade** |
| **Deregulation** | Associated: **foreign investment** |
| **Import liberalisation** | Associated: **multinational corporation participation** |
| Promotes **greater market orientation** | Promotes **greater global integration**; **facilitates technology flows** |
# 4. Liberalisation
- **Instruments:** **Industrial de-licensing**; **import deregulation**; a **decrease in import duty** on **capital goods**
- **Private-sector expansion** is an **aspect** of Liberalisation
- **New Industrial Policy** — **announced** on **24 July 1991** — the **primary starting anchor** of **Liberalisation**
- **Instruments** **lower the cost** of **technology** and **capital equipment** — this **boosts domestic manufacturing competitiveness**
# 5. Globalisation
- Involves the **reduction** of **import duties** on **capital goods**
- **Facilitates** **expanded entry** and **participation** of **Multinational Corporations**
- **Occurs through** **trade**, **foreign direct investment capital** and **cross-border technology**
- **Free trade:** The **reduction** or **removal** of **trade barriers**
# 6. Outcomes after 1991
- **Foreign Direct Investment inflows** **increased**; **foreign-exchange reserves** **expanded**; **India's share** in **global exports** **grew**
- ⚠ **Agriculture's share** in **GDP** did **not increase enormously** — it **declined** after the **1991 reforms**

## Industrial policy and SEZ policy | from: 39, 37
# 1. Milestones
| Year | Policy | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **1948** | **Industrial Policy Resolution** | **Associated** with a **mixed economic model** |
| **1951** | **Industrial Development and Regulation Act** | **Established** the **licensing framework** — **introduced industrial licensing** |
| **1956** | **Industrial Policy Resolution** | **Promoted** **industrialisation**, the **public sector** and the **joint sector** — **established public-sector** and **joint-sector dominance** |
| **1991** | **New Industrial Policy** — the **Liberal Industrial Policy** (**July 24, 1991**) | **Associated** with **liberalisation** — **massive de-licensing** and **deregulation** |
| **2000** | **Special Economic Zone Policy** — **announced** on **1 April 2000** (**April 1, 2000**) | **Under** the **Globalisation framework** |
# 2. SEZ Policy objectives
- **Primary objective:** **Boost export competitiveness** — **promote exports**; **export promotion**
- **Attract foreign direct investment** — **foreign investment**
- **Create** — **provide** — an **internationally competitive** **trade environment**
- **Create** a **level playing field** for **exports**
# 3. Fiscal policy
- **Includes** **taxation**, **expenditure** and **public debt**
- **Objectives:** **Growth**, **employment**, **price stability** and **equity**
- **V. P. Singh:** **Associated** with the **Long-Term Fiscal Policy** of **1985–86**

## Capital productivity, growth and corridors | from: 68
# 1. Capital Output Ratio (COR)
| Basis | High COR | Low COR |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | **More capital** is **needed** for **each unit** of **output** — **investment produces relatively less output** | A **given amount** of **capital** **produces greater output** |
| Capital productivity | **Lower** | **Higher** |
- ⚠ **High COR** combined with **high savings** may **still result** in **slow output growth**
# 2. Hindu Rate of Growth
- **Coined** by **economist Raj Krishna**
- Refers to **India's slow rate of growth** of **national income** or **GDP** — around **3.5%**
- **Associated** with the **period** from the **1950s** to the **early 1980s** — the **relatively slow growth** of the **pre-reform planning era**
# 3. Corridors and zones
- **Industrial corridor:** **Combines** **industrial development** with **connectivity**
- **Economic corridor:** **Connectivity** that **facilitates** the **movement** of **goods**, **services** and **economic activity**
- **Dedicated Freight Corridor:** **Dedicated railway freight transportation infrastructure**
- **National Investment and Manufacturing Zone (NIMZ):** A **large developed area** with an **ecosystem** for **world-class manufacturing**
  - **Instruments** of the **National Manufacturing Policy, 2011** — **envisaged** as **large areas** of **developed land** with the **required ecosystem**

## Navratna and Maharatna CPSEs | from: 64, 65
# 1. CPSE categories
- **CPSEs** are **classified** as **Miniratna**, **Navratna** and **Maharatna**
- **Categorisation** is **based** on **factors** such as **turnover**, **net worth** and **net profit**
# 2. Navratna
- **Associated** with **selected Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs)**
- The **Navratna scheme**: **Introduced 1997** — **initially covered** **9 CPSEs** (the **key PYQ association**: **9 CPSEs** were **initially selected**)
- **Main purpose:** Give selected CPSEs **greater autonomy and powers**; **objective** — to **compete more effectively** in the **global market**
- **Examples:** **Bharat Electronics Ltd. (BEL)**, **NALCO**, **MTNL**, **RINL**
# 3. Maharatna
- A **status** or **category** given to **selected Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs)** — **introduced** (**originated**) in **2010**
- **Administered** by the **Department of Public Enterprises (DPE)**
- **Purpose:** **Greater autonomy**, **greater financial** and **managerial powers** to **large, high-performing CPSEs** — **still greater autonomy** and **power to expand**
- **Introduced** to give **greater autonomy** to **selected Navratna CPSEs** and **enable** them to **compete globally**
- **Factors** in categorisation: **Turnover**, **net worth**, **net profit**
- **Maharatna CPSEs:**
  - **BHEL** — **received** Maharatna status in **2013**
  - **GAIL**, **Coal India Ltd.**, **Indian Oil Corporation Ltd. (IOCL)**, **NTPC**, **SAIL**, **ONGC**
  - **BPCL**, **HPCL**, **Power Grid Corporation**, **Power Finance Corporation**, **REC**
  - **Oil India Ltd.** — **later upgraded** from **Navratna** to Maharatna; **HAL** — **later upgraded** to Maharatna
