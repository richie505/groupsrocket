=== TOPIC indian-economy:6 | RBI and monetary policy

## Reserve Bank of India — structure and functions | from: 8, 9
# 1. Basics
- **RBI:** **Reserve Bank of India** — the **central bank** of **India**; a **statutory body**
- **RBI Act:** **1934**; **established** on **1 April 1935**
- **Nationalised:** **1949** — **fully owned** by the **Government of India** **after nationalisation**
- **Headquarters:** **Original** — **Kolkata**; **permanent** — **Mumbai**, **since 1937**
# 2. Functions
- **Currency issue:** **Issues currency notes** **except** the **₹1 note**
- **Banker to banks:**
  - **Commercial banks** keep **deposits** and **reserves** with the RBI
  - **Lends** to **banks** when **required**; **advises banks** on **monetary matters**
  - **Lender of Last Resort** — provides **emergency liquidity** to **banks**
- **Banker to the Government** — of **India** (the **Central Government**) and **State Governments**:
  - **Conducts banking transactions** for the **government**
  - **Manages public debt** — **government borrowing** and **public debt**
  - **Advises** the **government** on **monetary** and **financial matters**
  - Acts as the **government's agent** for **India's IMF membership**
- **Monetary policy:** **Responsible** for **conducting monetary policy** — **controls credit**; **controls money and credit** through **monetary policy**
- **Others:** **Manages foreign exchange reserves**; **regulates** and **supervises** the **banking system**; **oversees payment and settlement systems**; **supports financial** and **economic development**
# 3. Who issues what
| Item | Issued by |
|---|---|
| **₹1 note** | The **Government of India**, through the **Ministry of Finance** |
| **Other currency notes** | The **Reserve Bank of India** |
| **Coins** | The **Government of India** |

## Monetary policy instruments | from: 9, 40, 42
# 1. Instruments at a glance
| Instrument | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bank Rate** | The **RBI's lending** or **discount rate** — a **traditional rate** associated with **RBI lending** or **rediscounting**; involves **bills of exchange** or **commercial paper** |
| **Repo Rate** | **Lending by the RBI** to **banks** **against securities** — the **rate** at which the **RBI lends** to banks |
| **Reverse Repo** | Enables the RBI to **absorb liquidity** from **banks** — the **rate** at which the **RBI borrows** from banks |
| **CRR** — **Cash Reserve Ratio** | A **cash reserve** **maintained with the RBI** |
| **SLR** — **Statutory Liquidity Ratio** | **Liquid assets** **maintained** by **banks themselves** |
| **OMO** | The RBI **buying** or **selling** **government securities** |
| **MSF** | An **emergency overnight borrowing facility** |
- **Monetary policy** instruments: **Repo Rate**, **Reverse Repo Rate**, **Bank Rate**, **Cash Reserve Ratio**, **Statutory Liquidity Ratio** — ⚠ **fiscal deficit** is **associated** with **fiscal policy**
# 2. Repo vs reverse repo
| Basis | Repo Rate | Reverse Repo Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Use | **Liquidity injection**; **regulate money supply** | **Liquidity absorption** — **absorb surplus liquidity** |
| Mechanism | A **bank borrows** from the **RBI** | A **bank parks money** with the **RBI** |
# 3. Repo vs Bank Rate
| Basis | Repo Rate | Bank Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Short-term lending** by the **RBI** | A **traditional rate** |
| Against | **Eligible securities** | **Bills of exchange** or **commercial paper** |
| Use | **Liquidity management** | **Monetary policy** |
| Associated with | **Repo transactions** | **Bank lending** or **rediscounting** |
# 4. MSF vs repo
| Basis | Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) | Repo |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | An **overnight borrowing facility** | A **short-term borrowing mechanism** |
| Borrower | **Banks borrow** from the **RBI** | **Banks borrow** from the **RBI** |
| Use | **Marginal** or **short-term liquidity needs** | **Regular liquidity management** |
| Collateral | Can involve **dipping** into the **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) portfolio** | **Against eligible securities** |
| Cost | ⚠ **Traditionally** a **higher cost** than the **repo rate** | **Principal policy lending rate** in the **source framework** |
# 5. Bank rate and stance
- **Lower bank rate:** **Borrowing cheaper**; **expands liquidity** — an **expansionary stance**
- **Higher bank rate:** **Borrowing costlier**; **contracts liquidity** — a **tight stance**
- **Central bank policy rates** **directly regulate** the **capacity** for **credit creation**

## CRR and SLR | from: 9, 41
# 1. CRR vs SLR
| Basis | CRR | SLR |
|---|---|---|
| Kept | **Deposited** with the **RBI** — an **RBI-held reserve** | **Maintained** by the **bank itself** — **not deposited** with the **RBI**; the **bank's own holdings** |
| Form | **Only cash** | **Cash**, **gold** and **approved securities** or **other liquid assets** |
| Returns | **Do not earn interest** | **Can earn returns** |
| Role | **Controls liquidity** and **credit** — **regulates credit and liquidity** | **Controls liquidity**; a **liquidity buffer** — **regulates credit and liquidity** |
# 2. SLR in detail
- The **minimum percentage** of **Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)** that **commercial banks** must **maintain** in **liquid assets** **before lending**
- **Eligible assets:** **Liquid cash**, **gold** and **other unencumbered approved securities**
- **Government securities** are **commonly used** — **most commonly held** in **Government Securities (G-Secs)**, which **earn interest income** while **meeting SLR requirements**
# 3. Changing the SLR
| Basis | SLR increase | SLR decrease |
|---|---|---|
| Funds | Banks **lock** a **higher percentage** of **funds** in **liquid assets** — **reduces investible loan funds** | Banks **hold fewer funds** in **mandatory liquid assets** — **frees up capital** for **loans**; **expands lending capacity** |
| Credit creation | **Decreases** | **Increases** |
| Effect | **Money supply contracts**; **controls inflationary pressures** | **Stimulates broader economic activity** |
- **Raising the SLR:** A **direct contractionary quantitative credit control tool** — the **central bank** uses a **higher SLR** during **inflation**; it **reins in excessive commercial bank credit growth**
- An **increase** in the **cash reserve ratio** **decreases credit creation**

## Money supply | from: 38
# 1. M1 and M3
- **M1** — **narrow money:** **Currency with the public** + **demand deposits** + **other deposits with the RBI**
  - **High liquidity** — **directly spendable transaction money**
- **M3** — **broad money:** **M1** plus **time deposits with banks** — the **most commonly used measure** of **broad aggregate money supply**
  - **M3 minus M1** equals **time deposits with banks**
# 2. Components
| Component | In M1 | In M3 | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Currency with the public** | **Included** | **Included** | **Immediate cash in hand** |
| **Demand deposits with banks** | **Included** | **Included** | Can be **withdrawn instantly** on **demand** |
| **Other deposits with the RBI** | **Included** | **Included** | **Highly liquid balances** |
| **Time deposits with banks** | ⚠ **Not included** — **excluded** | **Included** | **Locked** for a **fixed period** — the **least liquid deposits** |
# 3. Liquidity
- **Liquidity order:** **Currency**, **savings deposits**, **demand deposits**, **time deposits**
- **Cash:** The **most liquid** form of **money**; **fixed** or **time deposits:** the **least liquid**
# 4. Withdrawal and the multiplier
- ⚠ A **cash withdrawal** does **not change** the **aggregate money supply** — **withdrawing ₹1 lakh** from **demand deposits** **reduces demand deposits** by **₹1 lakh** and **increases currency with the public** by **₹1 lakh**
- **Money multiplier:** **Total money supply** **divided** by **base money** — expressed as **M3** **divided** by **M0**
- An **increase** in **banking habits** **increases** the **money multiplier**
