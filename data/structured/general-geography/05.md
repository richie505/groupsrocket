=== TOPIC general-geography:5 | Climate, air masses and fronts

## Elements and controls of climate | from: 7
# 1. Elements vs factors
| Elements of climate | Factors or controls of climate |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | **Latitude** |
| **Atmospheric pressure** | **Altitude** |
| **Wind** | **Distance from the sea** or **continentality** |
| **Humidity** | **Pressure and wind systems** |
| **Cloudiness** | **Relief** or **topography** |
| **Precipitation** | **Ocean currents** |
| **Sunshine** or **insolation** | **Vegetation** or **land cover** |
| **Temperature range** | **Jet streams** and **atmospheric circulation** |
# 2. The elements defined
- **Temperature:** **Measures** the **degree of heat** — includes **daily** and **annual ranges**; **controlled** by **latitude** and **altitude**
- **Atmospheric pressure:** The **force exerted by air** — **varies** with **temperature** and **altitude**
- **Wind:** The **horizontal movement** of **air** — moves from **high pressure** toward **low pressure**
- **Humidity:** The **water vapour present** in the **atmosphere**
- **Cloudiness:** **Condensed water** or **ice particles** in the **atmosphere**
- **Precipitation:** **Water falling** from the **atmosphere** — **rain**, **snow**, **sleet** and **hail**
- **Sunshine** or **insolation:** **Incoming solar energy** — **controls heating**
- **Temperature range:** The **difference** between **maximum** and **minimum temperature**

## Temperature, pressure and wind | from: 7
# 1. Controls of temperature
- **Latitude:** Temperature generally **decreases** from the **equator** toward the **poles** — through the **solar angle**
  - The **equator** receives **more direct rays** — **greater heating**; the **poles** receive **oblique rays** — **lower heating**
  - **Seasonal variation** **increases** with **latitude**; the **annual temperature range** is generally **greater** toward **higher latitudes**
- **Altitude:** Temperature generally **decreases** with **increasing altitude** — **lower temperature** at **higher altitude** produces a **cooler climate**
  - The **Himalayas** are **cooler** **despite** having a **lower latitude**
- **Sea:** **Moderates temperature** — the **sea heats and cools slowly**; **land heats and cools rapidly**
| Basis | Coastal regions | Interior regions |
|---|---|---|
| Temperatures | **Moderated** | **More extreme** |
| Annual temperature range | **Smaller** | **Larger** |
| Climate | **Equable** | **Extreme** — **continental** |
- **Ocean currents:** **Warm currents** **raise** coastal temperatures; **cold currents** **lower** them
- **Relief:** **Alters temperature** and **air movement**; **mountain slopes** and **aspects** affect temperature
# 2. Clouds and temperature
- **Cloud cover** **modifies heating and cooling** — affects **day and night temperature**
| Sky | Day | Night |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloudy** | **Reduces incoming solar radiation** — **cooler daytime temperatures** | **Reduces terrestrial heat loss** — **warmer nights** |
| **Clear** | **Greater solar heating** | **Greater terrestrial cooling** |
# 3. Temperature and pressure
- **Increasing temperature** → air **expands** → **air density decreases** → **low pressure**
- **Decreasing temperature** → air **contracts** → **air density increases** → **high pressure**
- **Increasing** — **higher** — **altitude** **decreases atmospheric pressure** — **lower atmospheric pressure** occurs at **higher altitude**
- **Temperature differences** **create pressure differences**
# 4. Wind
- **Horizontal pressure differences** produce **wind** — the **pressure gradient** **initiates wind**; a **greater pressure gradient** produces **stronger winds**
- **Earth's rotation** **deflects wind** — the **Coriolis force**; **latitude** **changes** the **Coriolis effect**
- **Relief** **channels** and **blocks** winds; **mountain relief** **modifies rainfall and wind**
- **Pressure belts** **control** **major global circulation**
- **Seasonal heating** causes **seasonal wind reversal**

## Humidity and precipitation | from: 7
# 1. Humidity
- **Absolute humidity:** The **actual mass** of **water vapour** **per unit volume**
- **Relative humidity:** **Actual moisture** **divided** by **moisture-holding capacity**, **multiplied by 100**
- **Warm air** can **hold more water vapour**; **cold air** can **hold less**
- **Dew point:** The **temperature** at which **air becomes saturated**
- **Condensation:** **Changes water vapour** into **liquid** or **solid form**; **clouds form** through **condensation** around **nuclei** at **altitude**
# 2. Types of precipitation
| Type | Mechanism | Typical location |
|---|---|---|
| **Convectional** | **Strong heating**, **rising air**, **cooling** and **condensation** | The **Western Ghats** — the **example location** given |
| **Cyclonic** or **frontal** | **Convergence** or **frontal uplift**, **rising air**, **cooling** and **condensation** | **Temperate regions** |
| **Relief-induced** | **Topography** **modifies rainfall distribution** | **Differs** between **windward** and **leeward** locations |
# 3. Relief and rainfall
- **Mountains** **block** or **redirect** **air masses**
- **Windward slopes** receive **orographic rainfall**; **leeward slopes** form **rain shadows**
- The **Western Ghats** produce a **strong rainfall contrast** **across their slopes**
- The **Himalayas** **block cold continental winds** and **influence** the **monsoon**

## Monsoon, ocean currents and jet streams | from: 7
# 1. Monsoon mechanism
- **Summer heating** produces **low pressure** over **heated land**; a **pressure gradient** **draws moisture-bearing winds**
- The **ITCZ** **shifts northward** during **summer** — this **northward shift** **helps monsoon circulation**
- **Winter cooling** **reverses** the **pressure pattern** — **seasonal pressure reversal**, which causes **seasonal wind reversal**
# 2. Ocean currents and climate
- **Warm currents:** **Warm adjoining coastal air**; **increase moisture availability**; can **moderate cold coastal climates**
- **Cold currents:** **Cool adjoining coasts**; can **suppress convection**
  - **Cold currents** **combined** with **subtropical highs** are often **associated** with **arid west-coast climates**
# 3. Jet streams and teleconnections
- **Subtropical Westerly Jet:** **Connected** with **winter weather** and **Western Disturbances**
- **Western Disturbances:** **Winter precipitation** over **northwestern India**
- **Tropical Easterly Jet:** **Supports** **summer monsoon circulation**
- **ENSO** or **El Niño:** Generally **weakens** the **Indian monsoon**
- **IOD:** **Modulates monsoon variability** — a **positive IOD** can **partly offset** the **effect** of **El Niño**
- **SST anomalies:** **Influence** **atmospheric circulation** and **rainfall**

## Air masses | from: 8
# 1. Nature
- An **air mass:** A **large body** of **air** with **relatively uniform temperature** and **humidity**
- **Forms** over a **source region** with **uniform surface properties** — the **source region** may be **land** or **ocean**
- **Classified** on the **basis** of the **temperature** and **moisture content** of their **source regions**
- **Control** **temperature**, **humidity**, **stability** and **precipitation**
# 2. Types
| Source region | Properties | Designation |
|---|---|---|
| **Tropical oceans** | **Warm** and **moist** | **mT** — **maritime Tropical** |
| **Tropical land areas** | **Warm** and **dry** | **cT** — **continental Tropical** |
| **Polar oceans** | **Cold** and **moist** | **mP** — **maritime Polar** |
| **Polar land areas** | **Cold** and **dry** | **cP** — **continental Polar** |
| **Arctic** and **Antarctic** | **Extremely cold** and **dry** | **cA** or **cAA** |
# 3. Modification
- An air mass **travels away** from its **source region** and **gets modified** — it **loses** its **original characteristics**
| Passes over | Change |
|---|---|
| A **warm surface** | **Warmer** and **more unstable** |
| A **cold surface** | **Cooler** and **more stable** |
| An **ocean** | **Gains moisture** |
| **Land** | **Loses moisture** — **becomes drier** |
- **Vertical mixing** **alters** the **temperature and humidity profile**
# 4. Climatic effects
- A **new air mass** can cause a **sudden rise or fall** in **temperature**
- **Seasonal shifts** in **air masses** produce **seasonal climatic changes** — e.g. **monsoon winds**
- **Persistent air-mass influence** creates **characteristic regional climates** — e.g. **maritime** and **continental climates**
- **Air masses and fronts** can produce **cold waves**, **heat waves**, **thunderstorms** and **blizzards**

## Fronts | from: 8
# 1. Nature
- A **front:** A **boundary zone** between **two contrasting air masses** — **forms** when **two air masses** with **different temperatures** and **humidity** **meet**
- **Lighter, warmer air** is **forced to rise** over **denser, colder air**
- **Mid-latitude cyclones** **form along fronts**; **frontal uplift** produces **cyclonic** or **frontal rainfall**
# 2. Types of front
| Front | Formation | Uplift | Weather |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm front** | **Warm air advances** over **cold air** | **Gradual** | **Widespread light to moderate rain** |
| **Cold front** | **Cold air undercuts** **warm air** | **Steep** | **Intense short-duration rain** and **thunderstorms** |
| **Occluded front** | A **cold front** **overtakes** a **warm front** | — | **Complex weather** — often **prolonged precipitation** |
| **Stationary front** | **Neither air mass advances** | — | **Prolonged cloudy and rainy conditions** |
# 3. The uplift chain
- **Uplift** at a **front** **forces air** to **rise** — the **uplift** of **warm air** produces **cooling**
- **Adiabatic cooling** causes **temperature** to **fall** to the **dew point** — **condensation begins** when **air cools** to the **dew point**
- **Condensation** **changes water vapour** into **liquid** or **ice particles** — **cloud formation** **results** — **clouds and precipitation**
- **Precipitation** occurs when **droplets grow and fall** — **rain**, **snow** or **sleet**
- **Final impact:** **Changes** in **wind**, **temperature**, **humidity** and **pressure**; in **weather systems** and **climatic patterns**
