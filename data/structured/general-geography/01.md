=== TOPIC general-geography:1 | Earth's motions, seasons and time

## Rotation — day, night and time | from: 2
# 1. Two primary motions
- Earth has **two primary motions:** **rotation** and **revolution**
- **Rotation:** Earth **spins** on its **own axis** — **movement on Earth's axis**
- **Earth's axis:** An **imaginary line** **joining** — **through** — the **North and South Poles**
# 2. Rotation facts
- **Direction:** **From west to east** — appears **counter-clockwise** when **viewed** from the **North Pole**
- **Period:** Completes **one rotation** — a **full rotation** of **360°** — in **24 hours**
- **Angular speed:** **15° per hour** — **1° every 4 minutes**
- **Linear rotational speed:** **Maximum** at the **Equator**; **zero** at the **Poles**
- **Results:** **Day and night** (the **main result**); **time differences** and **time zones**; the **Coriolis effect** (**force**); a **centrifugal effect**
# 3. Day and night
- **Day:** The **part** of **Earth** **facing** the **Sun**; **night:** the **part** **turned away** from the **Sun**
- **Circle of illumination:** **Divides** the **illuminated** and **dark portions** of **Earth**
- Caused by **Earth's rotation** on its **axis** — ⚠ **revolution** does **not** cause day and night; **axial tilt alone** does **not** either
# 4. Longitude and time
| Longitude difference | Time |
|---|---|
| **1°** | **4 minutes** |
| **15°** | **1 hour** |
| **180°** | **12 hours** |
- **Longitude difference** **produces** **local-time difference**
- **Eastward movement:** Local time **advances**; **westward movement:** local time **retards** (**decreases**)
- **24 theoretical world time zones** — **each covering 15°**
- **180° meridian:** **Associated** with the **International Date Line** — the **date changes** across it
# 5. Coriolis effect
- **Caused** by **Earth's rotation** — the **apparent deflection** of **moving objects** or **air**
- **Deflected** to the **right** in the **Northern Hemisphere**; to the **left** in the **Southern Hemisphere**
- **Zero** or **absent** at the **Equator**; **maximum** at the **Poles** — **increases** with **latitude** and **velocity**
- **Applies** to **winds**, **ocean currents** and **cyclones**
# 6. Centrifugal effect
- **Caused** by **Earth's rotation** — the **centrifugal force** is **maximum** at the **Equator**; **minimum** or **zero** at the **Poles**
- **Causes** **equatorial bulging** and **polar flattening** — **Earth's shape** resulting from this is an **oblate spheroid**
- **Weight:** **Minimum** at the **Equator**; **increases** toward the **Poles**; **maximum** at the **Poles** — rotation makes it so
# 7. Changes in rotation
- **Earth's rotation axis** has **shifted slightly** over **time**; the **length of day** has **changed** by a **very small measurable amount**
- **Ice-sheet melting** **redistributes mass and water** toward **lower latitudes** — this **redistribution** **can influence** **Earth's rotation and axis**
- **Solar flares** and **coronal mass ejections** **affect** the **upper atmosphere** — ⚠ **not** the **primary explanation** in the **relevant question**

## Revolution, orbit and the calendar | from: 2, 3
# 1. Revolution
- **Revolution:** **Earth's movement** **around the Sun** in an **elliptical orbit** — **revolution changes Earth's position** around the Sun
- **Period:** **365.25 days** — also given as **365 days, 6 hours, 9 minutes** and **9.51 seconds**
- **Average orbital speed:** **29.78 km/s**
- **Results:** A **year** — the **annual cycle** (the **major result**); **seasons** **along with axial tilt**
# 2. Axial tilt and parallelism
- **Axial inclination:** **23.5°** (**23½°**) from the **perpendicular** to the **orbital plane** — the axis makes an **angle** of **66.5°** (**66½°**) **with** — is **inclined** to — the **orbital plane**
- A **major factor** in **seasons** — changes the **angle** and **duration** of **solar illumination**
- **Parallelism:** Earth's **axis** **maintains approximately the same orientation** — **remains approximately parallel** — as **Earth revolves**
  - **Produces** **opposite seasons** — **seasonal reversal** — **between** the **two hemispheres**
  - ⚠ The **correct statement** is that **there is parallelism** of **Earth's axis**
# 3. Perihelion and aphelion
| Basis | Perihelion | Aphelion |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Earth **nearest** to the **Sun** — **minimum Earth–Sun distance** | Earth **farthest** from the **Sun** — **maximum Earth–Sun distance** |
| Date | **Early January** — approximately **4 January** | **Early July** — approximately **4 July** |
| Distance | Approximately **147 million km** | Approximately **152 million km** |
| Orbital speed | **Greater** / **faster** — **maximum** | **Lower** / **slower** — **minimum** |
| Northern Hemisphere | During its **winter period** | During its **summer period** |
- **Kepler's Second Law** **explains** Earth's **maximum speed** at **perihelion** and **minimum speed** at **aphelion**
- ⚠ **Distance from the Sun** does **not primarily cause** seasons — only a **minor** or **slight role**
# 4. Leap year
- A **normal calendar year:** **365 days** — the **extra fraction** is **one-fourth of a day**, which **accumulates** to **one day** in **four years**
- **Leap year:** **366 days** — the **extra day** is **29 February**
# 5. Rotation vs revolution
| Basis | Rotation | Revolution |
|---|---|---|
| Movement | On **Earth's axis** | **Around the Sun** |
| Period | **24 hours** | **365.25 days** |
| Direction / path | **West-to-east** | An **elliptical orbit** |
| Main result | **Day and night** | A **year** |
| Seasonal role | **Indirect** | **Direct** — **combined** with **axial tilt** |
| Coriolis effect | **Has a role** | **No primary role** |
| Centrifugal effect | **Has a role** | — |
| Time zones | **Produces** | Does **not produce** |
| Solstices and equinoxes | ⚠ **Not** the **direct cause** | **Causes** them **along with axial tilt** |
| Perihelion and aphelion | Does **not cause** | **Associated** |

## Seasons — causes and solar angle | from: 3, 2
# 1. Why seasons occur
- Earth **revolves** around the **Sun** (in **365.25 days**) and has an **axial tilt** of **23½°** — Earth is **tilted** by **23½°** **as it revolves**
- **Main consequence** of **revolution** and **axial tilt:** A **changing angle of insolation** during the **year** — **revolution** and **axial tilt** **produce** the **seasonal cycle**
- ⚠ Earth's **rotation** causes **day and night**, **not seasons**; **revolution alone** is **not sufficient** — it **needs axial tilt**
- **Axial tilt combined with revolution causes:** **Changing day length**; **solstices** and **equinoxes**; **opposite seasons** in the **hemispheres**; **six-month polar day and night**
# 2. Mechanism
- **Earth's position** relative to the **Sun** **changes** during **revolution**; the **axial tilt** **remains approximately parallel**
- The **hemispheres** **alternately tilt** **toward** and **away** from the **Sun**
- The **angle** of **solar rays** — the **angle of insolation** — and the **length of day** **change** during the **year**; **one hemisphere** receives **longer illumination** at a time
- **Changes in heating** **produce seasons**; the **seasons** in **opposite hemispheres** are **opposite**
# 3. Summer vs winter
| Basis | Summer | Winter |
|---|---|---|
| Hemisphere tilt | **Tilted toward** the **Sun** | **Tilted away** from the **Sun** |
| Solar rays / angle | **More direct** | **More oblique** — **more slanting** |
| Energy | **Greater concentration** — Earth **receives greater solar energy** | **Lower concentration** — **lower solar energy** |
| Days | **Longer** | **Shorter** |
| Sun's apparent altitude | **Higher** | **Lower** |
| Heating | **Greater** | **Lower** |
- **More vertical** or **direct** rays: **Concentrated energy** and **greater heating**; **more oblique** or **slanting** rays **spread energy** over a **larger area** — **lower heating**
- The **summer hemisphere** receives **more direct rays** and **longer days**; the **winter hemisphere** **more oblique rays** and **shorter days**
- **Seasonal temperature** **depends** on **insolation** and **day length**
# 4. Shadows
- A **higher Sun** in the sky: a **smaller shadow**; a **lower Sun:** a **longer shadow**; a **vertically overhead Sun:** **no shadow** — a **vertical Sun** produces **no noon shadow**
- **Noon shadow** at the **Tropic of Cancer:** **Minimum** or **absent** on **21 June**
- **Noon shadow** at the **Equator:** **Absent** in **March** and **September**

## Solstices and equinoxes | from: 2, 3
# 1. Solstices
| Basis | June solstice | December solstice |
|---|---|---|
| Date | **20 or 21 June** (approximately **21 June**) | **21 or 22 December** |
| Northern Hemisphere | **Summer solstice** — **summer**, **longest day**, **shortest night** | **Winter solstice** — **winter**, **shortest day**, **longest night** |
| Southern Hemisphere | **Winter**, **shortest day**, **longest night** | **Summer solstice** — **summer**, **longest day**, **shortest night** |
| Sun's direct rays — vertically overhead | **Tropic of Cancer** (**23.5°N** / **23½°N**) | **Tropic of Capricorn** (**23.5°S** / **23½°S**) |
| Pole tilted toward the Sun | **North Pole** — the **Arctic Circle** and **regions beyond it** can experience **continuous daylight** (lasting **six months**) | **South Pole** — the **Antarctic region** experiences **continuous daylight** |
- At the **June solstice** the **Equator** has approximately a **12-hour day**
- **Two solstices** per year — the Sun is **overhead** at the **Tropic of Cancer** or **Tropic of Capricorn**; **seasonal contrast** is **maximum**; ⚠ **day and night** are **not equal**
# 2. Equinoxes
- **Equinox:** **Approximately equal day and night** — occurs **twice a year** (**two equinoxes** per year) — a **transition point** between **seasons**
- **Dates:** **March equinox** approximately **20 or 21 March**; **September equinox** approximately **22 or 23 September** — the equinoxes occur in **March** and **September**
- The **direct rays** of the **Sun** fall on the **Equator**; both **hemispheres** have **approximately equal day and night**
- **March equinox:** **Spring begins** in the **Northern Hemisphere**; **autumn begins** in the **Southern Hemisphere**
- **September equinox:** **Autumn begins** in the **Northern Hemisphere**; **spring begins** in the **Southern Hemisphere**
# 3. The year at a glance
| Date | Vertical Sun overhead at | Northern Hemisphere | Southern Hemisphere | Sun then |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **21 March** | **Equator** | **Spring** | **Autumn** | **Starts moving northward** |
| **21 June** | **Tropic of Cancer** | **Summer** — **longest day** and **shortest night** | **Winter** — **shortest day** and **longest night** | **Starts moving southward** |
| **23 September** | **Equator** | **Autumn** | **Spring** | **Continues moving southward** after it |
| **21 or 22 December** | **Tropic of Capricorn** | **Winter** — **shortest day** and **longest night** | **Summer** — **longest day** and **shortest night** | — |
# 4. Day length through the year
| Period | Northern Hemisphere days | Southern Hemisphere days |
|---|---|---|
| **21 March to 21 June** | **Progressively lengthen** | **Progressively shorten** |
| **21 June to 23 September** | **Progressively shorten** | **Progressively lengthen** |
| **23 September to 21 December** | **Progressively shorten** | **Progressively lengthen** |
| **21 December to 21 March** | **Progressively lengthen** | **Progressively shorten** |
- **Northern Hemisphere longest day** on **21 June** — in the **second half** of **June**, **usually** on **20 or 21 June**; **shortest day** on **21 December**
- **Southern Hemisphere longest day** on **21 December** — in the **second half** of **December**; **shortest day** on **21 June**
# 5. Christmas in Australia
- **December** is **winter** in the **Northern Hemisphere** and **summer** in the **Southern Hemisphere** — **Christmas** is **summer** in **Australia**, which is in the **Southern Hemisphere**

## Latitudes, the Equator and the poles | from: 2, 3
# 1. Key latitudes
| Latitude | Value | Association |
|---|---|---|
| **Equator** | **0°** | The **Sun** is **overhead** **near the equinoxes** |
| **Tropic of Cancer** | **23.5°N** | The **Sun** is **overhead** **near** the **June solstice** |
| **Tropic of Capricorn** | **23.5°S** | The **Sun** is **overhead** **near** the **December solstice** |
| **Arctic Circle** | **66.5°N** | **Polar day and night effects** |
| **Antarctic Circle** | **66.5°S** | **Polar day and night effects** |
- **Angular distance** from the **Tropic of Cancer** to the **Tropic of Capricorn:** **47°** = **23½°** plus **23½°**
- **Noon altitude** at the **Tropic of Cancer** at the **December solstice:** **43°** — **calculated** as **90° − 47° = 43°**
# 2. The Equator
- **Day and night** **approximately equal** **throughout the year** — about **12 hours** of **day** and **12 hours** of **night**; **seasonal variation** in **day length** is **very small**
- The **Sun** is **overhead** at the **Equator** during an **equinox**
- **No distinct winter or summer** — generally **wet and dry seasons** instead
# 3. The poles
- **North Pole:** **Six months** of **continuous daylight** (around **June**) and **six months** of **darkness** (around **December**)
- **South Pole:** The **opposite** daylight and darkness **pattern** — **continuous darkness** around **June**, **continuous daylight** around **December**
- **Polar day** and **polar night** each last approximately **six months**
- **Main reason:** **Axial inclination** **combined** with **revolution** — the **23½° axial tilt** and **revolution** **tilt a pole toward the Sun**; during polar day the **Sun remains above** the **horizon**
- **Continuous daylight:** **Beyond** the **Arctic Circle** during **Northern Hemisphere summer**; **beyond** the **Antarctic Circle** during **Southern Hemisphere summer**
# 4. Midnight Sun
- The **Sun remains visible** around **midnight** — **north** of the **Arctic Circle** and **south** of the **Antarctic Circle**
- **Strongest** around the **respective summer solstice** — can produce **24-hour daylight**
- **Example:** **Norway**

## Monsoons and Indian seasons | from: 3
# 1. Monsoon
- A **seasonal reversal** of **wind pattern** — from the **Arabic** word **Mausim**, meaning **season**
- **Main mechanism:** **Differential heating** and **cooling** of **land** and **sea**
  - **Summer heating** produces **low pressure**; **winter cooling** produces **high pressure** — **seasonal pressure reversal** produces **monsoons**
- **Monsoonal winds** **reverse direction** according to the **season** — **seasonal wind reversal** produces **monsoons**
  - **Summer:** From **sea to land**; **winter:** from **land to sea**
- ⚠ **Land and sea breezes** are **daily** or **diurnal**, **rather than annual**; **mountain and valley breezes** are **periodic**
- **Monsoonal climate:** **Wet and dry seasons**; **seasonal rainfall** is **associated** with the **Indian monsoon**
# 2. Indian monsoon seasons
- **Southwest monsoon:** **June to September**
- **Retreating monsoon:** **October to November**
- **Northeast monsoon:** **Important** for **southeastern India**
# 3. Indian traditional seasons
| Season | Months |
|---|---|
| **Spring** | **March to April** |
| **Summer** | **May to June** |
| **Rainy** | **July to August** |
| **Autumn** | **September to October** |
| **Pre-winter** | **November to December** |
| **Winter** | **January to February** |
# 4. Seasonal crop cycles
| Crop | Sown | Harvested |
|---|---|---|
| **Rabi** | **October or November** | **March or April** — or in **summer** |
| **Kharif** | **June or July** | **October or November** |
| **Zaid** | **Grown between Rabi and Kharif** — a **short summer season** | — |
- **Seasonal vegetation:** Includes **tropical deciduous forests**
