=== TOPIC general-geography:4 | Atmosphere

## Composition of the atmosphere | from: 6
# 1. Gases
| Gas | Share | Type | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen** (N₂) | Approximately **78%** — the **most abundant** atmospheric gas | **Permanent** | An **essential nutrient**; a **major atmospheric constituent** |
| **Oxygen** (O₂) | Approximately **21%** — the **second most abundant** | **Permanent** | **Supports respiration** and **combustion** |
| **Argon** (Ar) | Approximately **0.93%** — a **major inert gas** | **Permanent** | An **inert gas** |
| **Carbon dioxide** (CO₂) | Approximately **0.04%** | **Variable** | **Supports photosynthesis**; **contributes** to the **greenhouse effect** |
| **Water vapour** | Approximately **0–4%** — **highly variable** | **Variable** | **Humidity**, **clouds**, **precipitation**, the **greenhouse effect** |
| **Ozone** (O₃) | **Trace quantities** | **Variable** | **Absorbs** (**harmful**) **ultraviolet radiation** |
- **Dry-air composition sequence:** **N₂ > O₂ > Ar > CO₂**
- **Permanent gases** — **nitrogen**, **oxygen** and **argon** — have **relatively constant proportions**; **variable gases** **vary spatially** and **temporally**
- **Minor trace gases:** **Neon**, **helium**, **xenon**, **hydrogen** and **krypton**
- **Greenhouse gases:** Also **methane** and **nitrous oxide** (N₂O)
# 2. Vertical distribution
- **Carbon dioxide:** **Concentrated** **relatively low** — **up to** approximately **20 km**
- **Oxygen** and **nitrogen** **extend much higher** than the **lower atmosphere**; **hydrogen** **extends higher** than **nitrogen and oxygen**
- **Water vapour:** **Decreases** with **altitude** — **strongly concentrated** **near the surface** or in the **troposphere**; **more abundant** in the **tropics** than at the **poles**
- **Ozone:** **Concentrated** mainly in the **stratosphere**
# 3. Dust and aerosols
- **Dust particles** act as **condensation nuclei**; **dust and moisture** **contribute** to **cloud formation**
- **Aerosols** **scatter** or **absorb radiation**; **dust concentration** **influences** **atmospheric transparency** and **radiation**
# 4. Evolution of the atmosphere
| Basis | Early atmosphere | Modern atmosphere |
|---|---|---|
| Character | **Reducing** | **Oxygen-rich** |
| Oxygen | **Little or no free oxygen** | **Significant oxygen** |
| Gases | **Carbon dioxide**, **methane**, **ammonia** and **water vapour** **among other gases** | **Nitrogen** and **oxygen** **dominate** |
- **Biological evolution** **altered** the **atmosphere**; the **biosphere** and **atmosphere** **interact**

## Layers of the atmosphere | from: 6
# 1. Layers at a glance
| Layer | Extent | Temperature with height | Keyword / most important association |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Troposphere** | **Surface** to approximately **8 km** at the **poles** and **18 km** at the **equator** (**0 to 8 or 18 km**); **average upper limit** approximately **13 km** | **Decreases** | **Weather** — its **major identity** |
| **Stratosphere** | Approximately **13 or 18 km** to **50 km** | **Increases** | **Ozone** — its **major identity** |
| **Mesosphere** | Approximately **50 km** to **80 km** | **Decreases** | **Meteors** — **meteors burn** here |
| **Thermosphere** | **Begins** at approximately **80 km** and **extends upward** | **Rises sharply** | **Ionisation** and **aurora**; **high temperatures** |
| **Exosphere** | The **outermost atmospheric layer** — an **outer limit** **rather than** a **definite height** | — | **Space** — the **outer atmospheric region** and **satellites** |
- **Ionosphere:** **Radio waves** and **aurora** are its **most important associations**
# 2. Troposphere
- The **lowest atmospheric layer** — **average thickness** **13 km**; **8 km** thick at the **poles**, **18 km** at the **equator**
- **Normal lapse rate:** **6.5°C per kilometre** — approximately **1°C per 165 metres**; **temperature** is **higher near the surface** than at **higher altitudes** and **very low near** the **tropopause**
- **Most atmospheric mass** is **concentrated** here; **water vapour** and **dust (particles)** are **concentrated** here
- **Strong vertical convection** and **condensation** occur here — **almost all weather phenomena** occur in the troposphere
  - **Clouds**, **rainfall**, **storms** and **weather** **result from** tropospheric **water vapour**, **dust**, **convection** and **condensation**
- The **most important layer** for **biological activity**
- **Thicker** at the **equator** than at the **poles** — the chain:
  - **Strong solar heating** causes **strong convection**, which **transports heat upward** at the **equator**
  - **Strong convection** causes **air** to **rise higher**; **rising air** causes the **tropopause** to **rise**
  - A **higher tropopause** produces a **thicker troposphere**
# 3. Tropopause
- The **upper boundary** of the **troposphere** — the **boundary** between the **troposphere** and the **stratosphere**
- **Higher** at the **equator** (**stronger convection** there) and **lower** at the **poles**
- **Separates** the **weather-dominated troposphere** from the **relatively stable stratosphere**
# 4. Stratosphere and the ozone layer
- **Lies above** the **troposphere**; **extends up to 50 km**
- **Temperature increases** with **altitude** — ⚠ **upward** in the stratosphere, whereas it **decreases upward** in the **troposphere**
  - **Ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation**; this **absorption** **converts solar energy** into **heat**, which causes **temperature** to **increase upward**
- **Very little water vapour**; **clouds** and **weather** **very limited**; **relatively stable** — **suitable** for **jet aircraft**
- **Ozone layer:** **Ozone gas** (O₃) — an **ozone molecule** contains **three oxygen atoms**
  - **Located** mainly in the **stratosphere** — present mainly in the **lower stratosphere**; **maximum concentration** roughly **between 15 km and 35 km**
  - **Absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation** — **protects life** from **excessive ultraviolet radiation**
# 5. Mesosphere
- **Lies above** the **stratosphere** — approximately **50 km** to **80 km**
- **Temperature decreases** with **altitude** — **very cold**
- **Meteors burn** or **ablate** here; **upper boundary:** the **mesopause**
# 6. Thermosphere and ionosphere
- **Thermosphere:** **Lies above** the **mesosphere** — **temperature rises sharply** with **altitude**; **air density** **extremely low**; **ionisation** **significant**
- **Ionosphere:** **Electrically charged** or **ionised particles** — **traditional approximate region** **80–640 km**
  - **Reflects radio waves** — **supports long-distance radio communication**; **radio communication** and **telecommunications** are **closely associated** with it
  - **Associated** with **auroral phenomena** — **aurora** occurs in the **upper atmosphere** or **ionospheric region**
# 7. Aurora
- **Caused** by **solar charged particles** that **interact** with **Earth's upper atmosphere** or **magnetic environment**
- The **interaction** **excites atmospheric particles**; **excited atmospheric particles** **produce** a **light display**
- **Aurora Borealis:** **Northern Hemisphere**; **Aurora Australis:** **Southern Hemisphere**
# 8. Exosphere
- The **outermost atmospheric region** — **very high** and **rarefied conditions**; **density** **extremely low**
- Contains **predominantly very light gases**; **gradually merges** with — **transitions into** — **outer space**
- **Communication satellites** operate in its **outer region**; **satellites** are **associated** with the **outer** or **exospheric region**
