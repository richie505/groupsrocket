## Caste and social stratification | from: 4, 5
# 1. Caste terms
- **Varna:** A **four-fold social division**; **Jati:** a **birth-based caste group**
- **Sanskritization:** The **adoption** of **upper-caste practices**; **Westernization:** the **adoption** of **Western values**
- **Dominant caste:** An **economically** and **politically powerful caste**
- **Untouchability:** **Social exclusion** based on **caste**; **casteism:** **loyalty** to **one's own caste**
# 2. Systems of stratification
| System | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Caste system** | **Birth-based**; **endogamy**; **hereditary occupation**; **restricted mobility**; **purity and pollution** | **Brahmin**, **Kshatriya**, **Vaishya**, **Shudra** |
| **Class system** | **Wealth** and **occupation**; **achieved status**; an **open system** — **social mobility** is **possible** | **Upper**, **middle** and **lower class** |
| **Estate system** | **Medieval Europe** | **Clergy**, **nobility**, **commoners** |
| **Slavery system** | **Extreme inequality** — a **person** was **treated** as **property**; **no freedom** | **Ancient Greece**, **Rome**, **America** |
- **G. S. Ghurye:** **Caste** is **characterized** by **hierarchy**, **endogamy** and **social restrictions**
- **Karl Marx:** The **history** of **all societies** is the **history** of **class struggle**
# 3. Bases of stratification in India
- **Traditional:** **Caste**, **varna**, **tribe**, **religion**
- **Modern:** **Education**, **occupation**, **income**, **political power**, **urbanization**

## Status and social mobility | from: 17, 5, 4, 19
# 1. Caste status
- The **social position** **occupied** by an **individual** within the **caste hierarchy** — **determined** by **birth**; a **fixed social position**
- **Linked** with **occupation**; **determines social prestige**
# 2. Ascribed vs achieved status
| Basis | Ascribed status | Achieved status |
|---|---|---|
| Obtained | By **birth** | Through **personal effort** |
| Examples | **Caste**, **gender**, **family background** | An **IAS Officer**, a **Doctor**, a **Scientist** |
- **Increased achieved status** in **modern India:** **Education**, **urbanization**, **industrialization**, **reservation policies**, **globalization**
# 3. Social mobility
- **Social mobility:** The **movement** of **individuals** from **one social position** to **another** — **movement between social positions**
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Vertical** | **Upward** or **downward movement** |
| **Horizontal** | A **change** with the **same status** — **change without status change** |
| **Intergenerational** | **Between generations** |
| **Intragenerational** | **Within** a **lifetime** |
- **Example of upward mobility:** A **farmer's son** becoming an **IAS officer**
# 4. Hereditary occupations
- **Hereditary characteristics**, **occupations**, **rights** and **positions** are **passed** from **one generation** to **another**
  - **Hereditary position:** **Kingship**; **hereditary right:** **property ownership**
  - **Hereditary occupations:** **Traditional occupations**; **family businesses**
- **In the caste system:** **Potter**, **carpenter**, **blacksmith**, **weaver**
- **Weakened** by **education**, **industrialization**, **urbanization** and **occupational mobility**

## Kinship, marriage rules and family terms | from: 4, 18
# 1. Endogamy and exogamy
- **Endogamy:** **Marriage within** one's **own social group** — within a **caste**, **tribe** or **religion**
  - **Preserves group identity**; **maintains caste boundaries**; **controls property transfer**; **promotes cultural continuity** and **social solidarity**; **preserves traditions**
- **Exogamy:** **Marriage outside** one's **own social group**
- **Hypergamy** — also known as **Anuloma:** **Marriage into** a **higher caste**
  - **Anuloma:** A **union** between a **high-caste male** and a **low-caste female**
- **Hypogamy** — also known as **Pratiloma:** **Marriage into** a **lower caste**
  - **Pratiloma:** A **union** between a **low-caste male** and a **high-caste female**
# 2. Forms of marriage
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Monogamy** | **One husband** and **one wife** |
| **Polygyny** | **One man** and **many wives** |
| **Polyandry** | **One woman** and **many husbands** |
| **Sororal polygyny** | **Marriage** to **sisters** |
| **Fraternal polyandry** | **Marriage** to **brothers** |
| **Levirate** | A **widow** **marries** her **husband's brother** |
| **Sororate** | A **widower** **marries** his **wife's sister** |
| **Child marriage** | **Marriage below** the **legal age** |
| **Widow remarriage** | The **marriage** of a **widow** **again** |

| Term | Meaning |

## Tribal and cultural terms; social stability | from: 4, 12
# 1. Tribal terms
- **Animism:** The **worship** of **nature spirits**; **totemism:** the **worship** of an **animal** or **plant symbol**
- **Shifting cultivation:** **Slash-and-burn agriculture** — **Jhum cultivation** in **Northeast India**
- **Ghotul:** A **youth dormitory** among **Muria tribes**
- **Tribe:** A **community** with **common territory** and **culture**
# 2. Cultural terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ethnocentrism** | **Viewing one's own culture** as **superior** |
| **Acculturation** | **Cultural exchange** between **groups** |
| **Assimilation** | **Absorption** into a **dominant culture** |
| **Integration** | **Participation** while **retaining identity** |
| **Ethnicity** | **Shared cultural identity**; an **ethnic group** shares a **common culture** |
| **Cultural pluralism** | The **coexistence** of **different cultures** |
| **Cultural lag** | **Delay** in **adjustment** to **change** |
| **Cultural diffusion** | The **spread** of **culture** |
| **Cultural relativism** | **Understanding** a **culture** on its **own terms** |
| **Multiculturalism** | The **coexistence** of **multiple cultures** |
| **Xenophobia** | **Fear** of **foreigners** |
| **Regionalism** | **Loyalty** to a **region** |
| **Communalism** | **Loyalty** to a **religious group** |
# 3. Social stability
- A **condition** in which **society maintains** **peace**, **order**, **harmony**, **continuity** and the **effective functioning** of **institutions** — **persists despite social changes**
- **Key features:** **Social cohesion**; **social harmony**; the **rule of law**; **economic security**; **political stability**; **inclusive development**
- **Promoted by:**
  - The **family** — the **primary agent** of **socialization**
  - **Education** and **value inculcation**; **constitutional values**; **democratic institutions**
  - **Welfare schemes** and **social justice measures**; **religious and cultural tolerance**
- **Threatened by:** **Poverty and unemployment**; **casteism**; **communalism**; **regionalism**; **extremism and terrorism**; **social inequality**; **gender discrimination**
