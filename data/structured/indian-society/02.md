=== TOPIC indian-society:2 | Family, marriage and inheritance

## Functions of the family | from: 6, 8
# 1. Family as an institution
- A **basic** and **primary social institution**
- **Functions:** **Reproduction**; **socialization**; **emotional support**; **economic security**; **cultural transmission**
- **Important scholars** of **family:** **George Peter Murdock**, **Talcott Parsons**, **Irawati Karve**
# 2. Biological and protective functions
- **Ensures** the **continuity** of **society** through **reproduction** — **produces** the **next generation**
- **Provides** **socially approved sexual relations** — **prevents social disorder** by **regulating sexual behaviour**
- **Protects** **infants**, **children**, **elderly** and **sick members**
- **Protects members** from **social** and **economic risks**; **supports** **children**, **women**, **elderly persons** and **disabled persons**
# 3. Socialization and education
- The **first school** of the **child** — **teaches** **language**, **customs**, **values**, **traditions** and **norms**
- **Encourages acceptable behaviour**; **discourages deviant behaviour**
- **Teaches basic education** **before school**; **develops discipline** and **moral values**; **encourages learning habits**
- A **child learns** **language**, **manners** and **respect** for **elders** in the **family**
# 4. Identity and status
- **Provides** **caste**, **religious**, **language** and **national identity**; **provides social status**
- **Transfers traditions** from **one generation** to **another**
# 5. Economic functions
- **Provides** **food**, **shelter** and **clothing**; **members perform different roles**
- **Transfers wealth and property** through **inheritance**; **consumes goods and services collectively**
# 6. Emotional and religious functions
- **Provides** **love**, **affection** and **care**; **builds confidence** and **self-esteem**; **helps members cope** with **stress and crises**
- **Introduces** **religious beliefs** and **rituals**; **celebrates** **festivals** and **ceremonies** — **Diwali**, **Eid**, **Christmas**, **Gurpurab**; **preserves religious traditions**
# 7. Murdock's four universal functions
- **George Peter Murdock** **identified** **four universal functions** of the **family**:
| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sexual regulation** | **Regulation** of **sexual relations** |
| **Reproductive** | The **birth** of **children** |
| **Economic** | **Economic cooperation** |
| **Educational** | **Socialization** of **children** |

## Social institutions of Indian society | from: 8
# 1. Marriage and kinship
- **Marriage:** A **socially approved union** between **individuals** — **regulates sexual behavior**; **legitimizes children**; **creates kinship ties**; **provides social stability**
  - **Types:** **Monogamy**, **polygyny**, **polyandry**, **endogamy**, **exogamy**; **important terms:** **Anuloma**, **Pratiloma**, **Levirate**, **Sororate**
- **Kinship:** A **system** of **social relationships** based on **blood and marriage** — **consanguineal** (**blood relations**) and **affinal** (**marriage relations**)
  - **Important concepts:** **Gotra**, **Sapinda**, **clan**, **lineage**; **scholar:** **Irawati Karve**
# 2. Religion
- A **system** of **beliefs and practices** concerning **sacred things** — **scholar:** **Emile Durkheim**
- **Promotes social cohesion**; **provides moral guidance**; **exercises social control**; **provides emotional support**; **strengthens social solidarity**
- **Major religions in India:** **Hinduism**, **Islam**, **Christianity**, **Sikhism**, **Buddhism**, **Jainism**, **Zoroastrianism**
# 3. Education
- The **process** of **transmitting** **knowledge**, **skills** and **values** — **performs socialization**; **develops skills**; **promotes social mobility**; **contributes** to **nation building**
- **Constitutional provision:** **Article 21A**; the **Right to Education Act** — **enacted 2009**
- **Important institutions:** **Schools**, **colleges**, **universities**
# 4. Economy and polity
- **Economy:** **Production**, **distribution** and **consumption** — **provides employment**; **creates wealth**; **performs resource allocation**; **sectors** — **primary**, **secondary**, **tertiary**
- **Polity** or **state:** **Exercises authority** and **maintains order** — **law and order**; **justice**; **welfare**; **governance**
  - **Important elements:** The **legislature**, **executive** and **judiciary**
# 5. Caste and tribe
- **Caste:** A **birth-based social institution** **unique** to **Indian society** — **endogamy**, **hierarchy**, **hereditary occupation**, **social restrictions**
  - **Scholars:** **G. S. Ghurye**, **M. N. Srinivas**; **concepts:** **Sanskritization**, **Dominant Caste**, **Westernization**
- **Tribe:** A **social group** sharing **common territory**, **culture**, **language** and **ancestry** — **practices endogamy**; **political organization**
  - **Scholar:** **Verrier Elwin**; **terms:** **Animism**, **Totemism**, **Ghotul**, **Jhum Cultivation**
# 6. Media
- A **modern social institution** — **disseminates information**; **creates awareness**; **forms public opinion**
- **Types:** **Print**, **electronic** and **social media**
- **Contributes** to **social change**; **promotes political participation**; **facilitates cultural diffusion**

## Hindu marriage types — Manu Smriti | from: 34
# 1. The eight forms
- **Manu Smriti** **recognizes** **eight types** of **Hindu marriages**
| Form | Nature | Status in Manusmriti |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahma** | **Giving** a **daughter** to a **learned and worthy groom** | **Approved** |
| **Daiva** | **Giving** a **daughter** to a **priest performing a sacrifice** | **Approved** |
| **Arsha** | **Receiving symbolic gifts** from the **groom** | **Approved** |
| **Prajapatya** | **Based** on **duty** and **family life** | **Approved** |
| **Gandharva** | **Based** on **mutual love and consent** | **Conditionally accepted** |
| **Asura** | **Purchasing** the **bride** by **giving wealth** to **her family** | **Disapproved** |
| **Rakshasa** | **Forceful abduction** after **battle** | **Disapproved** |
| **Paisacha** | **Taking** a **woman** through **deceit** or **exploitation** | The **most condemned form** |
- **Approved** or **Prashasta:** **Brahma**, **Daiva**, **Arsha**, **Prajapatya**
- **Disapproved** or **Aprashasta:** **Gandharva**, **Asura**, **Rakshasa**, **Paisacha** — ⚠ **Gandharva** is **listed** among the **disapproved** or **Aprashasta marriages** although **conditionally accepted**
# 2. The forms in detail
- **Brahma:** The **father voluntarily gives** his **daughter** to a **qualified**, **educated**, **virtuous groom** **knowledgeable** in the **Vedas**
  - **No dowry**; **no bride price** — **considered** the **highest form** of **marriage**
  - **Modern equivalent:** An **arranged marriage** based on **qualifications** and **compatibility**
- **Daiva:** The **daughter** is **given** to a **Ritvik priest** who **officiates** a **sacrifice** — **linked** with **religious rituals**; the **groom** is a **priest**
- **Arsha:** **Performed** after **receiving** a **symbolic gift** such as a **cow and a bull** from the **groom** — **only** a **symbolic exchange**; **common** among **sages** and **hermits**
- **Prajapatya:** **Performed** with **blessings emphasizing duty** — **emphasizes** **family welfare** and **procreation**; **focuses** on **Dharma**; **husband and wife share responsibilities**
- **Gandharva:** **Mutual love** and **consent** of the **couple** — **no parental approval** required; a **love marriage**; a **voluntary union**
  - **Example:** **Shakuntala and Dushyanta**; **many references** occur in **epics**
- **Asura:** The **groom gives wealth** to the **bride's family** — **purchasing** the **bride**; a **bride price**; **wealth** is the **primary consideration**
- **Rakshasa:** The **forceful abduction** of a **woman** — **associated** with **Kshatriyas**; **after warfare** or **conflict**; **abduction-type marriages** are **mentioned** in **epics**
- **Paisacha:** **Deceit**, **intoxication**, **coercion** — **exploitation** of a **woman** **without her consent**; the **most condemned form**; **considered immoral and sinful**

## Succession and inheritance | from: 20, 21
# 1. Succession
- The **transfer** of **authority**, **office**, **position** or **property** from **one person** to **another**
| Type | Transfer | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Lineal succession** | From **parent** to **child** | **Father-to-son** |
| **Collateral succession** | **Among relatives** | **Brother-to-brother** |
- **Importance:** **Continuity** of **institutions**; **political stability**; **social order**; **legitimacy**
- **Examples:** **Political leadership transition**; **royal succession**; **transfer** of **family property**
# 2. Inheritance
- The **transfer** of **property**, **wealth**, **rights** and **obligations** **after death** — from **one generation** to **another**
| Type | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Property inheritance** | **Land**, **houses**, **buildings** |
| **Financial inheritance** | **Money**, **shares**, **savings** |
| **Cultural inheritance** | **Language**, **customs**, **traditions** |
| **Social inheritance** | **Status**, **reputation**, **networks** |
# 3. Inheritance laws
| Law | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** | **Provides inheritance rights** to **Hindus** |
| **Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005** | **Granted daughters** **equal coparcenary rights** |
| **Indian Succession Act, 1925** | **Applies mainly** to **Christians** and **others** |
| **Muslim inheritance** | **Governed** by **Sharia principles** |
- **Contemporary issues:** **Women's property rights**; **gender equality**; **family disputes**; **property fragmentation**
