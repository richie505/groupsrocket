=== TOPIC indian-society:0 | Sociologists and their ideas

## Founders — Comte, Durkheim, Marx and Weber | from: 15, 14, 13, 16, 3
# 1. Auguste Comte (1798 to 1857)
- Known as the **Father of Sociology** — **coined** the term **Sociology**; the **founder** of **Positivism**
- **Motto:** "**Order and Progress**"
- **Positivism:** **Knowledge** should be **based** on **observation**, **experimentation** and **scientific evidence**
- **Law of Three Stages** — he **proposed** it:
| Stage | Events explained through | Rain occurs because of |
|---|---|---|
| **Theological Stage** | **Divine** or **supernatural forces** | **God's will** |
| **Metaphysical Stage** | **Abstract concepts** | **Nature's force** |
| **Positive Stage** | **Scientific** explanation — **events explained scientifically** | **Atmospheric processes** |
# 2. Emile Durkheim (born 1858, died 1917)
- Regarded as the **Father of Modern Sociology**
- **Important works:** **The Division of Labour in Society**; **Suicide**; **Rules of Sociological Method**
- **Society** is a **reality sui generis**
- **Social facts** — he **developed** the concept: **External forces** that **influence individual behaviour** — e.g. **laws**, **customs**, **religion**, **traditions**
- **Collective conscience:** **Shared beliefs and values** that **unite society**
- **Anomie:** A **condition** of **normlessness** caused by **rapid social change** — e.g. **crime**, **suicide**, **social unrest**
# 3. Karl Marx (born 1818, died 1883)
- Known as the **Father of Scientific Socialism** — one of the **most influential sociologists** in **conflict theory**
- **Works:** **Das Kapital** (**published 1867**); the **Communist Manifesto** with **Friedrich Engels** (**published 1848**)
- **Historical materialism:** **Economic conditions** **shape** **society**, **politics** and **culture**
- **History** is the **history** of **class conflicts** — **class struggle**
  - **Examples of class conflict:** **Master and slave**; **lord and serf**; **bourgeoisie and proletariat**
- **Surplus value:** **Workers produce more value** than the **wages they receive**
- **Alienation:** Workers become **alienated** from the **product** of their **labour**, the **production process**, **society** and **themselves**
- **Predicted** that **workers** would **overthrow capitalism** and **establish** a **classless society**
# 4. Max Weber (1864 to 1920)
- Known as the **Father of Interpretative Sociology** — wrote (**studied**) **The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism**
- **Ideal type** — he **developed** the concept: An **analytical model** used to **understand social reality**
- **Types of social action:**
| Action | Basis |
|---|---|
| **Instrumentally rational** | **Goal-oriented behaviour** |
| **Value-rational** | **Values** and **ethics** |
| **Traditional** | **Customs** and **traditions** |
| **Affective** | **Emotions** |
- **Bureaucracy:** **Hierarchy**; **rules and regulations**; **specialization**; a **merit system**; **impersonality**
- **Types of authority:** **Traditional** — **customs**; **charismatic** — **personality and leadership**; **legal-rational** — **laws and rules**

## Other Western sociologists | from: 24, 23, 25, 26, 3
# 1. C. Wright Mills (1916 to 1962)
- An **American sociologist** — **famous** for the concept of **sociological imagination**; wrote **The Sociological Imagination** (**published 1959**) — **introduced** the concept in **1959**
- **Sociological imagination:** **Understanding** the **relationship** between **biography**, **history** and **society** — the **ability** to **connect personal experiences** with **larger social forces** and **historical processes**
  - **Individual problems** are often **reflections** of **wider social issues**
| Personal trouble | Public issue |
|---|---|
| **Being unemployed** | **Large-scale unemployment** due to **economic slowdown** |
| **Student stress** | A **competitive education system** |
- **Power Elite Theory:** **Power** is **concentrated** among **political leaders**, **military leaders** and **corporate elites**
- **Highlighted** **inequality** and the **concentration of power** in **modern society**
# 2. George Simmel (1858 to 1918)
- A **German sociologist** — **studied** **social interaction** and **urban life**
- **Formal sociology:** **Studies patterns** of **social interaction** — **society exists** because **individuals interact**
- **Dyad:** A **group of two people** — e.g. a **husband and wife**; **triad:** a **group of three people** — e.g. a **teacher**, **parent** and **student**
- **Urbanization** **increases** **individualism**, **competition** and **social distance**
# 3. Herbert Blumer (1900 to 1987)
- An **American sociologist** — **founded** **Symbolic Interactionism** (his **major contribution**)
- **People act** based on the **meanings** they **attach** to **things**
- **Meaning develops** through **social interaction**, **communication** and **interpretation**; **language creates meaning**; **individuals interpret meanings**
- **Symbols:** The **national flag** represents **patriotism**; a **wedding ring** — **marriage commitment**; a **uniform** — **authority**

## Indian sociologists and the Indological approach | from: 22, 3
# 1. Indology
- The **study** of **Indian society** through **ancient texts**, **scriptures**, **epics**, **religious literature** and **historical records**
- **Seeks to understand** Indian society through the **Vedas**, **Upanishads**, **Smritis**, **Puranas**, **Ramayana**, **Mahabharata** and **Dharmashastras**
- **Features:** **Text-based**; a **historical perspective**; **cultural interpretation**; **emphasizes continuity**
