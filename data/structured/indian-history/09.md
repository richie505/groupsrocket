=== TOPIC indian-history:9 | Dynasties of the Deccan and South India

## Badami Chalukyas | from: 144-2
# 1. Dynasty
- Also called the **Chalukyas of Badami** or **Vatapi Chalukyas** — **c. 543 to 753 CE**
- **Capital:** **Vatapi** — **present-day Badami** in **Bagalkot**, **Karnataka**; **Badami** and **Vatapi** were **associated** with the **Chalukyas**
- Chalukya rule was **mainly confined** to the **Deccan** and **Southern India** — ⚠ **not mainly** in **North India**
# 2. Rulers
| Ruler | Ruled — reign | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Pulakeshin I** | **c. 543 to 566 CE** | **Founded** — the **real founder** of — the **Vatapi Chalukya dynasty**; the **Ashvamedha** |
| **Kirtivarman I** | **c. 566 to 598 CE** | **Annexed** the **Kadambas**, **Gangas** and **Mauryas of Konkan** |
| **Mangalesha** | — | An **important ruler** **between Kirtivarman I** and **Pulakeshin II** |
| **Pulakeshin II** | **610 to 642 CE** | The **most capable** and **most powerful** Chalukya ruler |
| **Vikramaditya I** | **c. 655 to 680 CE** | **Restored Chalukya power** after the **Pallava invasion** |
| **Vikramaditya II** | **c. 733 to 746 CE** | **Defeated** the **Pallavas** **three times**; **captured Kanchi** |
| **Kirtivarman II** | **c. 746 to 753 CE** | The **last ruler** — **overthrown** by **Rashtrakuta Dantidurga** |
# 3. Pulakeshin II
- **Defeated** or **stopped Harshavardhana** on the **Narmada** — **630–634 CE**; **Pulakeshin II** and **Harsha** were an **important enemy pair**
- **Title:** **Dakshinapatheshwara** — "**Lord of the Deccan**"
- **Received** a **Persian embassy** from **Khusrau II** around **626 CE**
- **Pallava king Narasimhavarman I** **captured** and **destroyed Vatapi** in **642 CE** — **assumed** the **title Vatapikonda**; **Narasimhavarman I** and **Pulakeshin II** were an **important enemy pair**
- **Aihole Inscription:** A **key source** — **composed** by **Ravikirti**; **Sanskrit** and **Southern Brahmi**; **dated 634 CE** according to the **Saka era**; **preserved** at the **Meguti Jain Temple**
  - Mentions **Kalidasa** and **Bharavi** — **Ravikirti** **compared** his **fame** with theirs
- **Lohnara inscription:** **630 CE** — does **not mention Harsha** among the **defeated enemies**
# 4. Other records
- A **seventh-century inscription** concerning **Vikramaditya I** — **discovered** at **Madapura Lake** in **Davangere**; concerns **land-grant practices**
# 5. Women
- The Chalukyas **frequently gave** **high-ranking administrative positions** to **women**
- **Vijaya Bhattarika:** **Queen** of **Chandraditya** — **issued two copper plates** in her **own name**; a **poetess**; **administered** the **kingdom** after her **husband's death**
- **Kumkumdevi:** **Sister** of **Vikramaditya I** — her **recommendation** led to the **grant** of a **village** to a **Jain monastery**
# 6. Architecture
- **Badami:** **Rock-cut caves** — **four rock-cut temples**; **Caves 1–3** are **Hindu temples**; **Cave 4** a **Jain temple**
- **Aihole:** **Experimental** or **early temples**
- **Pattadakal:** A **Chalukya architectural centre** — a **synthesis** of **Nagara** and **Dravida** styles
- **Vesara:** A **hybrid** of **Nagara** and **Dravida** styles — **associated** with the **Chalukya style**
- **Ma-Twa-Lin** — also called **Matwalin:** A **Chinese traveller** — an **account** of **China–India relations** during **Chalukya rule**
- **Associations:** **Kanchi** with the **Pallavas**; **Tanjore** with the **Cholas**

## Eastern Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas and Kalyani Chalukyas | from: 145, 146, 147
# 1. Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi
- Also known as the **Chalukyas of Vengi** — **originated** as a **branch** of the **Badami Chalukyas**
- **Founded** by **Kubja Vishnuvardhana** — **brother** of **Pulakeshin II**
- **Core region** and **capital:** **Vengi** in **present-day Andhra Pradesh**; **Rajamahendravaram** — also **Rajahmundry** — **later** an **important centre**
- An **important power** in **Andhra–Deccan politics** — **repeated political interaction** and **conflict** with the **Cholas**
- **Rajaraja Narendra:** A **major ruler** — **connected** with the **Chola royal family** through **marriage**; **associated** with **Rajamahendravaram**; **patronised Nannaya**
  - **Nannaya** **began** the **translation** of the **Telugu Mahabharata** — the **Andhra Mahabharatam**
- The period was an **important phase** in the **development** of **Telugu literary tradition**
- **Succession** became **intertwined** with the **Chola dynasty** — **Rajendra Chalukya**, also known as **Kulottunga I**, **emerged** from the **Eastern Chalukya–Chola line** and **united** the two **political traditions**
- **Region map:** **Badami** — **Karnataka**; **Vengi** — **Andhra Pradesh**
- **Matwalin:** A **traveller** **associated** with an **account** of **China–India relations** during **Chalukya rule**
# 2. Rashtrakutas
- **Dantidurga** **established** the **Rashtrakuta dynasty** — **Empire** — in **753 AD**; **performed** the **Hiranyagarbha ritual** at **Ujjayini**
- **Capital:** **Manyakheta**
- **Amoghavarsha I:** An **important ruler** — **born** in **800 AD** at **Shribhavan** (on or near the **Narmada**) in a **military camp**
- **Participated** in the **Tripartite Struggle** for **Kannauj** — **Govind III** **defeated** **Gurjara-Pratihara** ruler **Nagabhata II** during a **North Indian campaign**
- **Religion:** **Patronized** **Shaiva**, **Vaishnava** and **Shakta traditions**; an **inclination** toward **Jainism** — **Jinasena**, a **Jain scholar**, **authored** the **Adipurana**
- **Kailasa Temple** at **Ellora:** **Built** by **Krishna I** — **Cave 16**; **Dravidian architecture**; **designed** to **recall Mount Kailasa** — **Shiva's abode**
- **Ellora:** **Buddhist**, **Hindu** and **Jain rock-cut caves** **coexist**
- The **Elephanta Caves** were **built** by the **Rashtrakutas**
- **Stated chronology:** **Mahendravarman I** → **Gopala** → **Mihir Bhoja**
# 3. Kalyani Chalukyas
- Also known as the **Western Chalukyas** — approximately **973 to 1195 CE**; a **distinctive Deccan cultural tradition**
- **Overthrew** the **Rashtrakutas** in **973 CE** — led by **Tailapa II**
- **Kalyani Chalukya style:** **Dravida influences** with **Deccan innovation** — **ornate pillars**; **stepped superstructures**; **decorated doorways**; **intricate wall motifs**; **balanced proportions**
- **Temples:** **Kasivisvesvara** at **Lakkundi**; **Mahadeva** at **Itagi**; **Doddabasappa** at **Dambal** — the **Lakkundi–Gadag–Itagi tradition** was a **regional** tradition
- **Sculptures:** **Deities**, **dancers**, **musicians**, **mythical figures**; **floral** and **geometric motifs** — **increasing ornamentation** and **dynamism**; **fine detailing**
- A **precursor** to the **Hoysala tradition**
- **Literature:** **Royal patronage** enabled **Sanskrit** and **Kannada** to **flourish**
| Author | Work |
|---|---|
| **Someshvara III** | **Manasollasa** |
| **Bilhana** | **Vikramankadeva Charita** |
| **Vijnaneshwara** | **Mitakshara** |
| **Nagavarma II** | **Kavyavalokana**; **Karnataka Bhashabhushana** |

## Imperial Cholas | from: 148
# 1. Rulers
- **Imperial Chola period:** Approximately the **9th** to the **13th century CE**
| Ruler | Ruled — reign | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Vijayalaya Chola** | — | **Founded** the **Imperial Chola dynasty**; **captured Tanjavur** |
| **Aditya I** | — | **Defeated** the **Pallava ruler Aparajita**; **expanded** **northward** |
| **Parantaka I** | — | **Defeated** the **Pandyas** and **Sri Lanka**; **lost** the **Battle of Thakkolam** to the **Rashtrakutas** in **949 CE** |
| **Rajaraja I** | **985 to 1014 CE** | **Major imperial expansion**; **captured northern Sri Lanka**; **destroyed Anuradhapura**; the **first Chola king** to **create** a **naval army** (as **credited** in the **source**); **conquered** the **Maldives** |
| **Rajendra I** | **1014 to 1044 CE** | **Captured** the **southern part** of **Sri Lanka** — **completed** the **conquest** of **Ceylon**; **defeated Mahinda V**; **conquered** the **Ganga valley**; **built Gangaikonda Cholapuram** around **1035 CE** and the **Chola Gangam** (a **huge artificial lake**); **naval expedition** against **Kadaram** and **Srivijaya** in **1025 CE** |
| **Kulottunga I** | **1070 to 1122 CE** | **Abolished tolls**; gave **complete freedom** to **Sri Lanka**; **married** his **daughter** to a **Sinhala prince** |
| **Rajendra III** | **1246 to 1279 CE** | — |
- The **Battle of Thakkolam** (**949 CE**) was **fought** between the **Cholas** and the **Rashtrakutas**
# 2. Maritime network and trade
- **Extended** from **Sri Lanka** to the **Malay Peninsula**, from the **Malay Peninsula** to **Sumatra**, and from **Sumatra** to **Southeast Asia**
- **Merchant guilds:** **Ayyavole 500**; **Manigramam**
# 3. Administration
- **Ur:** **Ordinary villages**; **Sabha:** **Brahmana villages**; **Nagaram:** **merchant towns**
- **Uttaramerur:** **Village self-government** — the **Kudavolai system**; **Variyams** — **specialized village committees**
- **Hierarchy:** **Mandalam** → **Kottam** or **Valanadu** → **Nadu** → **Ur**
- **Extensive temple-wall inscriptions** — an **unusually rich historical record**; the **Thanjavur inscription** records **Rajaraja I's military achievements** and the **defeat** of a **Chera naval force** at **Kandallur**
# 4. Temples and art
- **Dravida style**; **monumental temples** that **served** as **cultural centres**
- **Brihadeeswara Temple** — also the **Peruvudaiyar Temple:** **Thanjavur**; **Rajaraja I**; **completed 1010 CE**; a **granite vimana** approximately **66 metres high**
- **Great Living Chola Temples:** The **Brihadeeswara Temple**; **Gangaikondacholapuram** (**Rajendra I**); the **Airavatesvara Temple** at **Darasuram** (**Rajaraja II**)
- **Chola bronzes:** The **lost-wax technique** — **Nataraja**
- **Literature:** **Patronized** **Tamil** and **Sanskrit literature** — **Kamban** wrote **Ramavataram**
- **Religious life:** **Emphasized Shaivism** — **temple institutions** supported **music**, **dance** and **festivals**

## Hoysalas, Yadavas and Kakatiyas | from: 149, 149-2, 150
# 1. Hoysalas
- **Karnataka** — **capital** **Dwarasamudra**, **present-day Halebidu** (**Hassan district**) — **famous** for **Hoysala architectural heritage**
- **Hoysaleshwara Temple:** **Halebidu** — **dedicated** to **Shiva**; **12th–13th century**; **architect** **Kedroz**; **construction** associated with **Narasimha I's reign**
- **Chennakeshava Temple:** **Belur** — **built** by **Vishnuvardhana**; **dedicated** to **Vishnu**
- **Halebidu** and **Belur** are **Hoysala monuments**
- **Architecture:** A **distinctive style** — **star-shaped** or **stellate plans**; **soft chlorite-schist stone**; **lathe-turned pillars**; **mantapas**, **kalyanis**, **pushkaranis**; **blends Nagara** and **Dravidian styles**
- **Sculpture:** **Highly detailed friezes**; **mythological depictions**
- After **Ballala III's death** (**1343 CE**), **Hoysala territories** were **absorbed** into the **emerging Vijayanagara sphere**
# 2. Yadavas of Devagiri
- A **medieval South Indian dynasty** — the **12th century** to the **mid-14th century**; **ruled** around **1187–1317 AD** in **western India**
- **Initially** **feudatories** of the **Western Chalukyas**; also known as the **Seuna rulers** of **Devagiri**
- **Capital:** **Devagiri** — the **realm** was in **western India** and the **Deccan**, **centred** on **Devagiri**
# 3. Kakatiyas
- The **Eastern Deccan** — **12th–14th centuries CE**; **capital** **Warangal**
- **Rulers:** **Rudradeva** — also **Pratap Rudradeva I**; **Mahadeva** (after Rudradeva); **Ganapati Dev** — **father** of **Rudrama Devi**
- **Rudrama Devi:** **Daughter** of **Ganapati Dev** — the **fourth independent ruler**; a **female ruler**
  - **Defeated** the **Pandyas** (of **South Tamil Nadu**), the **Eastern Gangas** (**rulers** of **Orissa**) and the **Seuna rulers** of **Devagiri**
- The **Kakatiyas** had a **conflict** with the **Seuna**, or **Yadava**, rulers of **Devagiri**
# 4. Capitals
| Dynasty | Important capital |
|---|---|
| **Yadavas** | **Devagiri** |
| **Kakatiyas** | **Warangal** |
| **Hoysalas** | **Dwarasamudra** |
| **Pandyas** | **Madura** or **Madurai** |
| **Pallavas** | **Kanchi** |
