## Upanishads and Vedanta | from: 53
# 1. Upanishadic philosophy
- **Philosophical literature** — **philosophy** for **all times** and for **all**
- **Universal approach** — does **not oppose** **any school of thought**, **religion** or **scriptural interpretation**
- **Moksha:** **Liberation** — **part** of Upanishadic philosophy; **first discussed** in the **Upanishads**
- **Atman:** The **concept** — the **core** — of the **self**; **not** the **body** or **mind**
- The **human soul** is **associated** with the **life–death cycle** — the **continuous cycle** of **life and death** is **not** the **actual aim** of the **human soul**
- **Katha Upanishad:** A **conversation** between **Yama** and **Nachiketa** — on **death** and **what lies beyond it**
# 2. Upanishads and maxims
| Upanishad | Maxim | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Mundakopanishad** | The **national motto** — **Satyameva Jayate** | **Truth alone triumphs** |
| **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** | The **spiritual maxim** **Tamasoma Jyotirgamaya** | **Lead** from **darkness** to **light**; from **ignorance** to **truth** |
# 3. Vedanta
- A **philosophical system** — **literally** **Vedic philosophy**; the **end of the Vedas** or the **climax** of **Vedic ideology**
- **Prasthanatrayi:** The **foundations** of **Vedanta** — the **Upanishads**, **Brahma Sutra** and **Bhagavad Gita**
- The **Prime Prasthan:** The **Upanishads**; the **Bhagavata Prasthan** is also **associated** with **Vedanta**
# 4. Yoga
- A **philosophical tradition** — **described** in the **Upanishads**, **Mahabharata**, **Bhagavad Gita**, **Jain texts** and **Buddhist texts**
- **Patanjali** **later explained** Yoga as a **compact philosophical theory** — **Patanjali Darshan**
