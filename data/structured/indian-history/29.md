=== TOPIC indian-history:29 | National movement - Swadeshi to Quit India

## Partition of Bengal | from: 128
# 1. Partition — 1905
- **Announced** in **1905** — **Viceroy Lord Curzon**; **became effective** on **16 October 1905**
- **Bengal** was the **main province divided** — the **new province:** **Eastern Bengal and Assam**
| Province | Capital |
|---|---|
| **Eastern Bengal and Assam** | **Dacca** (**Dhaka**) |
| **Western Bengal** | **Calcutta** |
- **Nationalists interpreted** it as a "**Divide and Rule**" **policy** — the **Swadeshi Movement** was the **major response**
# 2. Annulment — 1911
- The **Partition** was **annulled** in **1911** — the **capital shifted** from **Calcutta** to **Delhi** in **1911**

## Extremists — Assertive Nationalists | from: 119
# 1. Nature
- **Extremists:** Also called **Assertive Nationalists** — the **aggressive nationalist wing**; **influence increased** after **1906**
- **Main objective:** **Swaraj** — **complete independence** from **English rule**
- **Methods:** **Self-sacrifice**, **mass movements**, **aggressive resistance** — **opposed Moderate methods**; **rejected Prayer**, **Petition** and **Protest**
- **Supported Swadeshi** and **Boycott** — **wanted** to **extend** the movement (a **wider** and **extended Swadeshi–Boycott movement**); **lacked faith** in **Moderate negotiations** with the **British**
# 2. Moderates vs Extremists
| Basis | Moderates | Extremists |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | **Gradual self-government** | **Swaraj** or **complete independence** |
| Method | **Prayer**, **Petition** and **Protest** | **Self-sacrifice** and **resistance** |
| Strategy | **Constitutional agitation** | **Mass movement** |
| View of British | **Initially** had **faith** in **British liberalism** | **Increasing distrust** of **British rule** |
| Swadeshi–Boycott | **Less willing** to **extend** it | Wanted it **wider** and **extended** |
| Representative leaders | **Dadabhai Naoroji**, **Gokhale** | **Tilak**, **Lajpat Rai**, **Bipin Chandra Pal**, **Aurobindo Ghosh** |
# 3. Surat Split — 1907
- A **conflict** between the **Moderates** and the **Extremists** — **major issues:** **Leadership**, **methods**, **Congress strategy** and **Swadeshi–Boycott**
# 4. Lal–Bal–Pal–Ghosh
- **Lal–Bal–Pal:** **Lajpat Rai**, **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** and **Bipin Chandra Pal** — a **famous Extremist trio**
- **Lal–Bal–Pal–Ghosh:** Adds **Aurobindo Ghosh** — **represented four major Extremist leaders** (the **Extremists** or **Assertive Nationalists**)
| Leader | Belonged to | Associated with |
|---|---|---|
| **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** | **Maharashtra** | **Swaraj** — **gave** the **slogan** "**Swaraj** is my **birthright…**"; the **Shivaji Festival** and **Ganpati Festival** — **used** for **mass mobilisation**; **published Kesari** and **Mahratta**; **wrote** the **book Gita Rahasya** — **Swaraj**, **festivals** and **newspapers** |
| **Lala Lajpat Rai** | **Punjab** | Known as **Sher-e-Punjab** and **Punjab Kesari**; the **safety-valve theory** — **represented Punjab** and the **safety-valve issue**; the **Simon Commission protest** |
| **Bipin Chandra Pal** | **Bengal** | **Lal–Bal–Pal**; the **Swadeshi Movement**; **New India**; the **Brahmo Samaj** — **Swadeshi** and **New India** |
| **Aurobindo Ghosh** | **Bengal** | The **main leader** of the **Swadeshi Movement** during the **Partition** of **Bengal**; the **Maniktalla Garden raid**; **Pondicherry** — **Swadeshi**, **Maniktalla** and **Pondicherry** |
# 5. Key events
- **Tilak:** An **Extremist leader** — **imprisoned** in **1908** for **six years** in **Mandalay**; **Sir Valentine Chirol called** him the "**Father** of **Indian unrest**"
- **Lajpat Rai:** An **Extremist** — a **member** of **Lal–Bal–Pal** along with **Tilak** and **Bipin Chandra Pal**; **suffered** a **lathi-charge injury** during the **Simon Commission protest**; **died** on **17 November 1928**
  - **Young India** was the **source** of the **safety-valve theory**
- **Aurobindo Ghosh:** An **Extremist** — **arrested** in **1908** during the **Maniktalla Garden raid**; the **Maniktalla case involved** him — **sentenced** to **one year**, **later released** for **lack** of **evidence**
  - **Later moved** to **Pondicherry** — **withdrew** from **revolutionary political activity**

## Jallianwala Bagh | from: 122
# 1. Background — Rowlatt Satyagraha
- **Rowlatt Act:** **March 1919** — **popularly described** as "**No Vakil**, **No Appeal**, **No Daleel**"; **Gandhi responded** through the **Rowlatt Satyagraha** (the **immediate context**)
- **Country-wide strike:** **6 April 1919**
- **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal:** The **Punjab leaders arrested** on **9 April 1919**
# 2. The incident
- **Place:** **Amritsar**, **Punjab** — **date:** **13 April 1919**; **occasion:** **Baisakhi**
- **Viceroy:** **Lord Chelmsford**
- The **crowd gathered** for a **protest** and to **celebrate Baisakhi**
- **Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer ordered** the **firing** — **ordered troops** to **open fire**
- **Michael O'Dwyer:** The **Lieutenant Governor** of **Punjab** — Dyer was **associated** with the **firing**, O'Dwyer with **Punjab administration**
- **British supporters described Dyer** as the "**Lion** of **British Empire**" — he **received** the **Sword** of **Honour**
# 3. Inquiries
- **Official inquiry:** The **Hunter Committee** — also called the **Disorders Inquiry Committee**; **chairman** **Lord William Hunter**
  - **Seven members** — **four British** and **three Indian**: **C.H. Setalvad**, **Pandit Jagat Narayan**, **Sultan Ahmad Khan**
  - **Criticised Dyer's actions** — **described** them as an "**Error** in **judgement**"
- **Congress investigation:** **Conducted** by the **Congress Inquiry Committee** — **Gandhi** was **associated** with **drafting** the **report**
# 4. Reactions
- **Rabindranath Tagore renounced** or **returned** his **Knighthood** on **31 May 1919**
- **Shankaran Nair resigned** from the **Viceroy's Executive Council**
- **Montagu described** the **action** as "**Preventive Murder**"
- **Udham Singh killed Michael O'Dwyer** — **assassinated** on **13 March 1940** at **Caxton Hall** in **London**
# 5. Impact
- **Deepened hostility** towards **British rule**; **faith** in **British justice** **declined**; **strengthened** the **nationalist movement**
- A **major turning point** in **Gandhi's political career** — an **important background** to the **Non-Cooperation Movement**
- The **Amritsar Congress Session** **took place** in **December 1919**

## Gandhi — early life and first campaigns | from: 100
# 1. Early life and South Africa
- **Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi:** **Born** on **2 October 1869** in **Porbandar** — a **barrister**; **legal training** at the **Inner Temple** in **London**
- **1894:** **Founded** the **Natal Indian Congress** in **South Africa**
- **1903:** **Indian Opinion** — a **newspaper** **established** in **South Africa**; **published** in **English**, **Gujarati**, **Hindi** and **Tamil**; **first issue** in **June 1903**
- **1904:** **Established Phoenix Ashram** in **Durban** — **Gandhi's first ashram**
- **1910:** **Gandhi** and his **colleagues** **established Tolstoy Farm**
- **1915:** **Returned** to **India**
- **February 1916:** **First major public speech** in **India** at **BHU**, **Varanasi**
# 2. Early Satyagrahas in India
| Campaign | Year | Issue | Associates / result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Champaran Satyagraha** | **1917** | **Indigo cultivation** — the **Tinkathia system** | **Raj Kumar Shukla invited Gandhi** to **Champaran** — the **site** of **Gandhi's first Satyagraha** in **India** |
| **Ahmedabad Mill Strike** | **1918** | **Industrial workers** — a **plague bonus** and a **wage dispute** | **Lasted 21 days**; **workers received** a **35% bonus** as a **result**; **Ambalal Sarabhai** and **Anasuya Behn** were **important associates** |
| **Kheda Satyagraha** | **1918** | **Peasants affected** by **crop failure** — **demanded revenue remission** or **suspension** | **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** |
- **Ahmedabad fast:** Gandhi **used** a **fast unto death** in **Ahmedabad** in **1918** — his **first hunger strike used** as a **weapon**; the **strike produced** a **result** in **1918**

## Gandhi — mass movements | from: 100
# 1. Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience
- **Non-Cooperation Movement:** **Began** in **1920** — **involved boycott** and **non-cooperation**; **withdrawn** after the **Chauri Chaura incident** (**1922**)
- **Civil Disobedience Movement:** **Began** in **1930** — Gandhi **led** the **Dandi March**, which **began** on **12 March 1930** and **violated** the **Salt Law**
# 2. Gandhi–Irwin Pact — 1931
- **Signed** on **5 March 1931** — **Gandhi** and **Lord Irwin**; **associated** with **Congress** in **1931**; **Congress withdrew** the **Civil Disobedience Movement**
- **The 1931 Pact provided for:**
  - **Release** of **non-violent political prisoners**
  - **Salt-making** in **coastal areas** without **salt tax**
  - **Peaceful picketing**
- **Congress agreed** to **participate** in the **Second Round Table Conference**
- **Sarojini Naidu called Gandhi** and **Irwin** "**Two Mahatmas**" — **associated** with the **Gandhi–Irwin discussions**
# 3. Second Round Table Conference — 1931
- Gandhi was the **sole Congress representative** — **travelled** to **London** on the **S.S. Rajputana** in **1931**; **stayed** at **Kingsley Hall**
- **B. R. Ambedkar demanded separate electorates** for the **Depressed Classes**
# 4. Communal Award and Poona Pact — 1932
- **Communal Award:** **Announced** in **1932** — the **background** to **Gandhi's fast**
- **Poona Pact:** **Concluded** in **1932** — **Gandhi** and **Ambedkar**; **joint electorates** and **reserved seats** for the **Depressed Classes**
  - ⚠ The **representatives** or **followers signed** it — the **PDF states** that **Gandhi himself** did **not sign** the **Poona Pact**
- Gandhi **founded** the **All India Anti-Untouchability League**; **Harijan** — his **weekly newspaper** (**1933**)
- Gandhi **resigned** from **Congress** in **1934** — ⚠ **not** a **formal Congress member** at the **time** of **Independence**
# 5. Quit India — 1942
- **Quit India Resolution:** **Passed** on **8 August 1942** — **represented Gandhi's call** — "**Do or Die**"
- **Important associates:** **Jayaprakash Narayan**, **Ram Manohar Lohia**, **Aruna Asaf Ali**, **Matangini Hazra**, **Kanaklata Barua**
# 6. Death
- **Assassinated** by **Nathuram Godse** on **30 January 1948**
- **Jawaharlal Nehru:** "**The light** has **gone out** of **our lives**"

## Ideas and theories of the national movement | from: 133
# 1. Theories
| Idea | Associated with | Meaning — states, holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Drain Theory** | **Dadabhai Naoroji**; **R.C. Dutt** | **India's wealth** was being **transferred** to **Britain** — an **economic critique** of **colonialism**; **colonial policies caused Indian economic exploitation** |
| **Doctrine of Lapse** | **Lord Dalhousie** | **Annexation** of **states** where the **ruler died** without a **natural heir** |
| **Divide and Rule** | A **British colonial policy** | **Used political** and **communal divisions** to **weaken nationalism** |
| **Safety Valve Theory** | **A.O. Hume** — an **early INC debate** | **Viewed** the **INC** as a **means** of **channelling political discontent** |
| **Two-Nation Theory** | **Muhammad Ali Jinnah**; the **Muslim League** | **Muslims** and **Hindus constituted separate nations** |
| **Hindutva** | **V.D. Savarkar** | A **cultural-national identity centred** on **Hindutva** |
| **Swaraj** | **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** — a **nationalist objective advocated** by him | **Self-rule** — **political self-government** |
| **Satyagraha** | **Mahatma Gandhi** | **Truth-force** and **non-violent resistance** |
| **Trusteeship** | **Mahatma Gandhi** | **Wealth holders** should **treat wealth** as a **social trust** |
