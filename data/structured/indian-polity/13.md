=== TOPIC indian-polity:13 | Parliament - sessions, questions and motions

## Sessions — summoning, adjournment, prorogation and dissolution | from: 64
# 1. Summoning — Article 85 (Part V)
- **The President** summons each House at such time and place as he thinks fit
- **Maximum gap** between two sessions: **six months** — between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next
  - So Parliament must meet at least **twice a year**
- ⚠ **Not in the Constitution:**
  - A fixed number of sessions — **Budget, Monsoon and Winter** sessions are a **convention**
  - A minimum number of sitting days — not **60**, **90** or **100** per year
# 2. Ending a sitting or session
| Device | By whom | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | **Presiding Officer** (Speaker or Chairman) | Suspends the sitting for a specified time — **hours, days or weeks**; session unaffected; pending business stays active |
| **Adjournment sine die** | **Presiding Officer** | Ends the sitting **indefinitely** — no day named for reassembly; precedes **prorogation** |
| **Prorogation — Article 85(2)(a)** | **President**, on the advice of the Council of Ministers | **Terminates the session**; pending **bills unaffected**; **notices lapse** unless re-introduced |
| **Dissolution — Article 85(2)(b)** | **President** | Ends the **very life of the Lok Sabha** — pending **bills, motions and resolutions lapse** (subject to **Article 107** rules); the **Rajya Sabha** is permanent |
# 3. Linked time limits
- **Ordinance (Article 123):** Valid **six weeks** after reassembly; maximum life without approval **six months plus six weeks**
- **Absence of an MP (Article 101(4)):** Unexcused absence for **60 days** can lead to vacation of the seat — the calculation excludes periods of **prorogation** and adjournments of more than **four consecutive days**

## Question Hour and Zero Hour | from: 55, 33, 34
# 1. Purpose
- **Executive accountability:** Parliamentary hours and procedural motions are the main means of enforcing executive accountability, scrutinising **government policies and actions**, and controlling the **Council of Ministers**
- **Accountability triangle:** **Question Hour + Supplementary Questions + Adjournment Motions** — directly ensure **cabinet (executive) responsibility** to the legislature
- **Indigenous Indian contributions:** **Zero Hour** and the **Calling Attention Motion**
# 2. Question Hour vs Zero Hour
| Basis | Question Hour | Zero Hour |
|---|---|---|
| Timing — Lok Sabha | **First hour** of every sitting — **11:00 AM to 12:00 Noon** | Immediately **follows** Question Hour — **12:00 Noon to 1:00 PM** |
| Timing — Rajya Sabha | **12:00 Noon to 1:00 PM** (shifted after **2014**) | **11:00 AM to 12:00 Noon** |
| Nature | **Formal** procedure — codified in the **Rules** | **Informal convention** — an **Indian** innovation of the early **1960s** |
| Purpose | **Asking and answering questions** addressed to **Ministers** on administrative actions | Raising **urgent matters of public importance** without prior notice |
| Duration | Maximum **one hour** | Generally up to **one hour** — some notes say it is **not fixed** |
# 3. Types of questions — the three primary categories
| Basis | Starred Question | Unstarred Question | Short Notice Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mark | **Asterisk** | **No asterisk** | Identified by a **shorter notice** period |
| Answer | **Oral** — on the floor of the House | **Written** — laid on the Table (purely written; no oral answer) | **Oral** |
| Supplementary questions | **Allowed** — after the oral answer | **Not allowed** | **Allowed** |
| Subject | **General / ordinary** public importance | **General / ordinary** public importance | **Urgent** public importance |
| Daily limit | At most **20** listed for oral answer | **No** strict limit of 20 | At the **discretion** of the Speaker or Chairman |
- All three are taken up during **Question Hour**

## Motions and other procedural devices | from: 55, 33
# 1. Motions on the Government's survival
| Motion | Moved by / where | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **No-Confidence Motion** | Opposition — **Lok Sabha only** — **Rule 198** | Tests the Council of Ministers' majority; needs the support of **50** members; if passed, the **Government must resign**; first moved by **J. B. Kripalani** in **1963** against **Jawaharlal Nehru** |
| **Confidence Motion** | **Government** (Council of Ministers) — **Lok Sabha only** | Proves majority **floor** support; if defeated, the **Government falls** |
| **Censure Motion** | **Lok Sabha only** | Strong **disapproval** / criticism of **specific policies**; **reasons** must be stated; does **not** force resignation — shows political weakness |
# 2. Calling Attention vs Adjournment Motion
| Basis | Calling Attention Motion | Adjournment Motion |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | **Indian** parliamentary innovation | **Traditional** parliamentary device |
| House | **Either** House | **Lok Sabha only** |
| Permission | **Presiding Officer's** permission | **Presiding Officer's** consent |
| Objective | Draw a **Minister's attention** to an **urgent public matter** | Discuss a **definite** matter of **urgent public importance** |
| Business | Does **not** disrupt the daily agenda | **Interrupts / suspends** normal business |
| Censure | **No** element of condemnation | Involves **censure or condemnation** of the Government |
- **Adjournment Motion conditions:** A **single**, **definite**, **specific** matter of **recent** occurrence; cannot raise a **question of privilege**; only **one** per sitting; admissible only when other tools (e.g. **Short Duration** discussions) are too late
- **Restriction:** Calling Attention notices restricted the scope of the **Adjournment Motion** — **not** of Question Hour or Zero Hour
# 3. Other motions
- **Privilege Motion:** Against a **Minister** for **withholding or distorting facts** — either House; Presiding Officer's consent; referred to the **Committee of Privileges**
- **Closure / Guillotine Motion:** **Lok Sabha only** — when the allotted debate time **expires**, debate closes immediately and all **undiscussed financial demands and clauses** are put to the **vote**
# 4. Cut Motions — on a demand for grant
| Cut Motion | Reduction | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy Cut** | Reduce to **₹1** | **Complete disapproval** of the underlying policy |
| **Economy Cut** | By a **specified amount** | **Administrative economy** |
| **Token Cut** | By **₹100** | Ventilate a **specific grievance** within government responsibility |
- **Passing any Cut Motion** in the Lok Sabha signals **loss of financial control** — the Government must **resign**
# 5. Rules and conventions
- **Rule 184 (Lok Sabha):** Discussion on a matter of public interest with a formal **motion and voting** — defeat can mean potential collapse of the ruling party
- **Rule 377 (Lok Sabha):** Raise **urgent matters** not covered under Questions, Calling Attention or other motions
  - **Written notice** to the **Secretary-General**
  - Number of matters raised daily increased from **20 to 30** in **2015**
- **Interpellation:** The formal right of **asking questions** of the Executive — demanding explanations of government actions or policies
- **Yielding the Floor:** Convention — the **Speaker** orders a speaking member to **yield** (stop) so that **another member** can speak
