## Political parties in India | from: 54
# 1. Formation of major parties
| Party | Formed | Key facts |

# 2. Party system
- **Rajni Kothari:** Coined the terms "**One-Party Dominant System**" and "**Congress System**"
  - The **Congress** party **dominated** India's **democratic, multi-party electoral landscape** after **independence** — the Congress System
- **1989 General Election:** Marked the **end** of the Congress System — ushered in an era of **minority** and **coalition** governments
  - **V.P. Singh** led the **National Front** — with **outside support** from the **Bharatiya Janata Party** and the **Left**
# 3. Leader of Opposition
- **First formally recognized:** **1969**
- **Threshold:** The **largest opposition party** must hold at least **10%** of the **total House strength** — approximately **55 seats** in the **Lok Sabha**
# 4. Constitutional recognition
- Political parties were **not** included in the **original 1950** constitutional text
- **Introduced** — **explicitly recognized** for the **first time** — through the **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985**, which introduced the **Tenth Schedule**
# 5. National Party — Route 2
- At least **6%** of **votes** in **four states** **and** **four Lok Sabha seats** — the **two** recognition **conditions** under **Route 2** are **both mandatory**

## Anti-defection law — Tenth Schedule | from: 77, 54
# 1. Origin and amendment
- **52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985:** Introduced the **Anti-Defection Law** — added the **10th Schedule**; amended **Articles 101, 102, 190 and 191**
  - **Bill passed:** **15 February 1985**; **in force:** **1 March 1985**
- **Jammu and Kashmir:** Enacted its **own state** Anti-Defection legislation in **1979**
- **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003:** **Strengthened** the law
  - **Deleted** — **omitted** — the original **one-third split** exception; the one-third split protection is **completely abolished** (**obsolete** threshold)
  - **Capped** the **Council of Ministers** at **15%** of the **Lower House**
# 2. Grounds of disqualification
| Member | Disqualified for | Protected / exception |
|---|---|---|
| **Party-elected member** — an **MP** or **MLA** | **Voluntarily giving up** party **membership**; **voting** or **abstaining** from voting **contrary to the party whip** without **prior permission** | **Prior permission** obtained, or the party **condones** the action within **15 days** |
| **Independent elected member** | **Joining** any **political party** after **election** — including joining the **government** as an **independent minister** without taking **party membership** | — |
| **Nominated member** | Joining a **political party** after the **expiry** of **six months** from **taking a seat** | May join a party **within six months** of taking a seat |
| **Presiding officer** — **Speaker** or **Chairman** | **Joining another political party** **while holding office** or **after vacating** office | May **voluntarily give up** party membership upon **election to the Chair** and **rejoin** the **original party** after **demitting office** |
- **Voluntarily giving up membership:** **Broader** than **formal resignation** — includes **conduct**; e.g. **publicly campaigning** for an **opposition party**, even **without** a **formal resignation letter**
# 3. Merger exception
- **No disqualification** when a member's **original political party** **merges** with **another party** and at least **two-thirds** of the **legislature (legislative) party** members **agree** to the **merger**
- ⚠ **Trap:** Protection requires **two-thirds**, **not one-third**
# 4. Who decides — defection vs general disqualification
| Basis | Defection — 10th Schedule | General disqualification — Union MP | General disqualification — State MLA / MLC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provisions | **10th Schedule**; **Articles 102(2) and 191(2)** | **Articles 102(1) and 103** | **Articles 191(1) and 192** |
| Decided by | The **Presiding Officer** of the **concerned House** — the **Speaker or Chairman alone**, acting as a **tribunal** | The **President of India** | The **Governor** of the State |
| ECI's role | **Not consulted** | **Opinion** must be obtained — **mandatory and binding** | **Opinion** must be obtained — **mandatory and binding** |
| Review | **Judicial review** — **Kihoto Hollohan** (**1992**), Supreme Court | — | — |
- ⚠ **Trap:** The **President** and the **Governor** have **no jurisdiction** over **10th Schedule** defection matters
- **Time limit:** The 10th Schedule prescribes **no statutory time limit** for the Presiding Officer to decide a **defection petition**
