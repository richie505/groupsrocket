=== TOPIC indian-polity:18 | High Courts and tribunals

## High Courts — establishment, judges and jurisdiction | from: 70
# 1. Framework and history
- **Part VI, Chapter V:** **Articles 214 to 231** (the Supreme Court: **Articles 124 to 147**, Part V)
- **Article 214:** A **High Court for each State**
- **Indian High Courts Act, 1861:** First three High Courts in **1862** — **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras**
- **North-Western Provinces High Court:** Set up at **Agra** in **1866** → shifted to **Allahabad** in **1869**
# 2. Key Articles
- **Article 215:** **Court of Record** — evidentiary value; power to punish for **contempt**
- **Article 216:** **Composition** — a **Chief Justice** and other judges appointed by the President; the **President** fixes each High Court's strength
  - ⚠ Not **Parliament**, the **Governor** or the **Chief Minister** — contrast: **Parliament** fixes the **Supreme Court's** strength by law (**Article 124(1)**)
- **Article 217:** **Appointment and conditions of office** — removal under **Article 217(1)(b)** read with **Article 124(4)**
- **Article 218:** Applies certain Supreme Court **removal** provisions to High Courts
- **Article 219:** **Oath** — by the **Governor** of the State
- **Article 220:** **Restriction on practice** after being a permanent judge
- **Article 221:** **Salaries, allowances and pensions**
- **Article 222:** **Transfer** of a judge from one High Court to another — by the **President**
- **Article 223:** **Acting Chief Justice**; **Article 224:** **Additional and Acting Judges**; **Article 224A:** **Retired judges** attending
- **Article 226:** **Writs**; **Article 227:** **Superintendence**
- **Article 230:** Extending jurisdiction to **Union Territories**; **Article 231:** **Common High Court** for two or more States or UTs — established by **Parliament**
# 3. Appointment, qualifications and removal
- **Chief Justice:** Appointed by the **President** after consulting the **CJI** and the **Governor**
- **Other judges:** After consulting the **CJI**, the **Governor** and the **Chief Justice of that High Court**
- **Qualifications:** **Citizen** of India, and at least **10 years** in **judicial office** in India **or** **10 years** as an **advocate** of a High Court — **no** provision for a **distinguished jurist**
- **Removal:** By the **President alone** — after an **Address** by **each House** by **special majority**, only for **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**
- **After retirement:** A permanent judge cannot plead or act before any court or authority in India — except the **Supreme Court** and a High Court where he was **not** a permanent judge
# 4. High Court vs Supreme Court
| Basis | High Court | Supreme Court |
|---|---|---|
| Strength fixed by | **President** — **Article 216** | **Parliament** by law — **Article 124(1)** |
| Appointment | **President** by **warrant** — **Article 217** | **President** by **warrant** — **Article 124** |
| Oath | **Governor** — **Article 219** | **President** — **Article 124(6)** |
| Resignation to | **President** — **Article 217(1)(a)** | **President** — **Article 124(2)(a)** |
| Retirement | **62** years | **65** years |
| Court of Record | **Article 215** | **Article 129** |
| Writs | **Fundamental Rights and ordinary legal rights** — **Article 226** | **Fundamental Rights only** — **Article 32** |
| Advisory jurisdiction | **None** | **Article 143** |
| Salary / pension | Salary on the **State Fund**; pension on the **Consolidated Fund of India** | Both on the **Consolidated Fund of India** |
# 5. Writs and superintendence
- **Article 226:** **Broader** than the Supreme Court's writ jurisdiction — Fundamental Rights **and any other legal purpose or right**; the writ power is **discretionary**
  - **Article 32** is itself a **Fundamental Right**
- **Article 227:** **Administrative and judicial superintendence** over all subordinate **courts and tribunals** in its territory
  - ⚠ **Exception — Article 227(4):** Not over courts or tribunals under laws relating to the **Armed Forces**
# 6. High Courts with more than one State or UT

## Tribunals — Part XIV-A | from: 72
# 1. Basics
- **Part XIV-A:** **Articles 323-A and 323-B** — inserted by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** on the **Swaran Singh Committee's** recommendations
- **Nature:** **Quasi-judicial**, specialised dispute-resolution bodies — to **reduce pendency** in regular courts and provide **domain expertise**
- ⚠ Tribunals are not placed in **Part XI** (Union–State relations) or **Part X** (Scheduled and Tribal Areas)
- **Related Parts:** **Part IX** Panchayats (**243 to 243-O**); **Part IX-A** Municipalities (**243-P to 243-ZG**); **Part IX-B** Co-operative Societies (**243-ZH to 243-ZT**); **Part XIV** Services (**308 to 323**)
# 2. Article 323-A vs Article 323-B
| Basis | Article 323-A | Article 323-B |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects | **Exclusively public service matters** — **recruitment** and **conditions of service** of **Union**, **State**, **local-authority** and **statutory-authority** employees | **Taxation**, **foreign exchange**, **industrial disputes**, **land reforms**, **ceiling on property**, **foodstuffs**, **elections** to Parliament and State Legislatures |
| Who can create | **Only Parliament** by law — **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985**; **State Legislatures have no power** | **Parliament** and **State Legislatures** — States only within their **legislative competence** |
| Structure | **One Central Administrative Tribunal** + **State Administrative Tribunals** (separate or **joint** for States) | A **hierarchy** of tribunals can be created |
# 3. L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)
- **Judicial review** by the High Courts (**Articles 226 and 227**) and the Supreme Court (**Article 32**) is an integral part of the **Basic Structure**
- Clauses in **Articles 323-A and 323-B** excluding the jurisdiction of the **High Courts** and **Supreme Court** — declared **unconstitutional**
- Tribunal decisions (e.g. **CAT**) face scrutiny first before a **Division Bench** of the concerned **High Court**; the **Supreme Court** may be approached after that
# 4. Important tribunals
- **Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT):** **1985** under the **Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985** (**Article 323-A**) — **original jurisdiction** over service matters of Union civil servants; appeals to the **Division Bench** of the jurisdictional **High Court**
- **TDSAT:** Set up by the **TRAI (Amendment) Act, 2000** (parent: **TRAI Act, 1997**) — disputes in **telecommunications**, **broadcasting** and **airport tariffs**
  - **Telecom, broadcasting and tariff** appeals go **directly to the Supreme Court** on substantial questions of law; **cyber** appellate orders go to the **High Court**
- **National Green Tribunal:** **Statutory** — **NGT Act, 2010**; guided by **natural justice**, **not bound** by the **Code of Civil Procedure, 1908**; appeals **directly to the Supreme Court** within **90 days**
- **Armed Forces Tribunal Act:** **2007**
# 5. IMDT Act case
- **Illegal Migrants (Determination by Tribunals) Act, 1983 (IMDT Act):** Struck down in **Sarbananda Sonowal v. Union of India (2005)**
- **Reason:** It violated **Article 355** — the Union's duty to protect States against **external aggression** and **internal disturbance**
