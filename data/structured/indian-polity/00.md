=== TOPIC indian-polity:0 | Constitutional development

## Company rule — Regulating Act 1773 to Charter Act 1853 | from: 1, 22, 43
# 1. Regulating Act, 1773
- **Governor of Bengal → Governor-General of Bengal:** **Warren Hastings** became the Governor-General of Bengal
- **Supreme Court at Calcutta (Fort William):** Provided for by the Act; established in **1774** — the **first** Supreme Court
  - First Chief Justice: **Sir Elijah Impey**
- **Banned:** **Private trade** and **bribes**
# 2. Pitt's India Act, 1784 and the Act of 1786
- **Pitt's India Act (1784):** Established **Dual Control**
  - **Board of Control** → **political** affairs
  - **Court of Directors** → **commercial** affairs (commerce)
- **Act of 1786:** Governor-General could **override his Council** and assume the powers of **Commander-in-Chief**
  - First to exercise these powers: **Lord Cornwallis**
# 3. Charter Act, 1813
- **Trade monopoly in India:** Ended (partially abolished) — except the **tea** trade and **trade with China** (both monopolies retained)
- **Christian missionaries:** Permitted to operate in India
- **Education:** Annual grant of at least **₹1 lakh** for the education of Indians — first such provision
# 4. Charter Act, 1833
- **Governor-General of Bengal → Governor-General of India** (office created): **Lord William Bentinck** was the first Governor-General of India
- **Law Member:** A **fourth member** added to the Governor-General's Council — **Macaulay**, the first Law Member
- **Commercial status:** The Company's commercial role ended entirely — trade monopoly completely abolished in **1833**
- **Section 87:** Opened Company service to Indians for the first time — **no discrimination** based on religion, colour or other such distinctions
# 5. Charter Act, 1853
- **Legislative and executive functions** of the Governor-General's Council clearly separated for the first time
- **Civil Services:** **Open competition** introduced

## Crown rule — Act of 1858 and the Indian Councils Acts | from: 1, 22, 43
# 1. Government of India Act, 1858
- **Transfer of power:** From the Company to the **British Crown**
- **Viceroy:** Governor-General designated as **Viceroy** — **Lord Canning** was the first Viceroy of India
- **Abolished:** **Board of Control** and **Court of Directors**
- **Secretary of State for India:** Created, assisted by a **15-member Council**
# 2. Indian Councils Act, 1861
- **Decentralisation:** Restored the legislative powers of **Bombay** and **Madras**
- **Indian participation** in law-making began
- **Portfolio System** introduced
- **Viceroy's Ordinance Power:** Introduced (origin of the ordinance power) — an ordinance could last at most **six months**
- **High Courts Act, 1861:** High Courts of **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** established in **1862**
# 3. Indian Councils Act, 1892
- **Budget:** Discussion permitted — but **no voting** on the Budget
- **Questions to the executive:** Permitted, with **six days'** prior notice — **no supplementary** questions
- **Principle of (limited) indirect election** introduced
# 4. Indian Councils Act, 1909 — Morley-Minto Reforms
- **Separate electorates for Muslims** introduced — **Lord Minto**: "Father of Communal Electorate"
- **Indians in Executive Councils:** First associated with the Executive Councils of the Viceroy and Governors
  - **Satyendra Prasad (S.P.) Sinha:** First Indian in the Viceroy's Executive Council, as **Law Member**
- **Supplementary questions** allowed
- **Central Legislature:** Enlarged but still **unicameral**
- ⚠ **Trap:** The 1909 Act did **not** introduce dyarchy and gave **no** Provincial Autonomy; existing **High Courts** continued

## Government of India Acts, 1919 and 1935 | from: 1, 22, 43
# 1. Government of India Act, 1919 — Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms
- **Dyarchy in the provinces:** Introduced for the first time (Provincial Dyarchy, **1919**)
- **Bicameral Central Legislature:** First time (bicameralism at the Centre)
- **Direct elections:** First time (at the Centre)
- **"Responsible government":** Term used for the first time
- **Budgets:** Central and Provincial budgets separated; initial separation of subjects
- **Communal representation:** Extended
- **Chamber of Princes:** Established — **120** members; first Chancellor: **Maharaja Ganga Singh of Bikaner**
- **Public Accounts Committee:** Set up under this Act in **1921**
- **Public Service Commission:** Provided by this Act; established on **1 October 1926**
# 2. Government of India Act, 1935
- **All-India Federation:** Provided for the first time — **never came into existence** (not implemented)
- **Dyarchy:** Abolished in the provinces; **Central Dyarchy** proposed (**1935**) — **never implemented**
- **Provincial Autonomy:** Introduced in full
- **Federal Legislature:** Bicameral, proposed
- **Communal representation:** Retained or extended
- **Federal Court:** Established; inaugurated on **1 October 1937** — first Chief Justice: **Sir Maurice Gwyer**
- **Territory:** **Burma** separated from India; **Sindh** and **Orissa** created as new provinces
- **Residuary powers:** Vested in the **Governor-General**
- **Ordinance:** **Section 42** of this Act is the model for **Article 123** (President's Ordinance Power)
# 3. Comparison of the Acts
| Feature | Indian Councils Act 1909 | GoI Act 1919 | GoI Act 1935 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dyarchy | Not introduced | **Introduced in the provinces** | Abolished in provinces; **proposed at the Centre** |
| Provincial Autonomy | None | Initial separation of subjects | **Full Provincial Autonomy** |
| Communal representation | **Separate electorates for Muslims** | Extended | Retained or extended |
| Central Legislature | Enlarged, unicameral | **Bicameral** | Bicameral Federal Legislature proposed |
| Courts | Existing High Courts continued | Existing High Courts continued | **Federal Court (1937)** |

## Independence and the making of the Constitution | from: 1, 22, 43
# 1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly
- **Idea first put forward:** **M.N. Roy**, **1934**
- **First formal demand:** **Swaraj Party**, Ranchi conference, **May 1934**
- **Constituted under:** **Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946**
- **First explicit demand for Fundamental Rights:** **Constitution of India Bill, 1895**
# 2. Interim Government
- **Announced:** **24 August 1946**; took charge **2 September 1946**
- **Vice-President of the Executive Council:** **Jawaharlal Nehru**
# 3. Constituent Assembly — timeline
- **9 December 1946:** First meeting — temporary President **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha**
- **11 December 1946:** **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** — first permanent elected Chairman/President
- **13 December 1946:** **Jawaharlal Nehru** moved the **Objectives Resolution** (at the **fifth** meeting of the first session)
- **22 January 1947:** Objectives Resolution **unanimously adopted**
- **22 July 1947:** **National Flag** adopted
- **29 August 1947:** **Drafting Committee** constituted, headed by **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**
- **26 November 1949:** Constitution **adopted and enacted** — observed as **Constitution Day**
- **24 January 1950:** **Final meeting**; **National Anthem** adopted
- **26 January 1950:** Constitution fully came into force; **Republic of India** established
- **Office-bearers:** **Frank Anthony** — first nominated temporary Deputy Chairman; **H.C. Mukherjee** — first permanent Vice-President
- **First draft:** **B.N. Rau**, as Constitutional Advisor
- **Women members:** **15**
# 4. Indian Independence Act, 1947
- **Ended:** British rule (British sovereignty)
- **Created:** Two independent Dominions — **India** and **Pakistan**
- **Constituent Assemblies:** Given dual powers — **constitution-making** and **legislative**

## Landmark constitutional amendments | from: 1, 22, 43
- **1st Amendment (1951):** Added the **Ninth Schedule**, **Articles 31A and 31B** and **Article 15(4)** (clause (4) to Article 15); restricted the rights under **Article 19**; addressed **agrarian reforms**
- **42nd Amendment (1976) — "Mini-Constitution":**
  - Added the terms **Socialist, Secular and Integrity** to the Preamble
  - Inserted **Fundamental Duties** as **Part IVA** (**Article 51A**)
- **44th Amendment (1978):** Nullified emergency-era distortions; restored fundamental safeguards
  - Removed the **Right to Property** from Fundamental Rights → now **Article 300A**
  - Inserted the term **"armed rebellion"**
- **52nd Amendment (1985):** **Anti-Defection Law** — inserted the **Tenth Schedule**; amended **Articles 101, 102, 190 and 191**; gave **political parties** constitutional recognition for the first time
- **61st Amendment (1988/89):** Voting age **21 → 18**; effective **28 March 1989**; first exercised in the **1989** General Election
- **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992):**
  - **73rd:** Constitutional status to **Panchayati Raj** — **Part IX**, **Articles 243 to 243O**, **Eleventh Schedule**, **three-tier** system; enforced **24 April 1993**
  - **74th:** **Municipalities** — **Part IXA**, **Articles 243P to 243ZG**, **Twelfth Schedule**; enforced **1 June 1993**
- **84th Amendment (2001):** Seat-allocation **freeze** — Lok Sabha seat allocations based on the **1971 Census**, continued up to **2026**
- **86th Amendment (2002):** **Article 21A** — Right to Education for children of **6–14 years**; **Article 51A(k)** — the **eleventh** Fundamental Duty (education of children)
- **91st Amendment (2003):** Strengthened anti-defection provisions; limited the size of the **Council of Ministers** — capped at **15%** of the strength of the House
- **99th Amendment (2014):** **National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC)** — struck down by the Supreme Court as unconstitutional in **2015**
- **101st Amendment (2016):** **Goods and Services Tax** — effective **1 July 2017**
- **103rd and 104th Amendments (2019):**
  - **103rd:** **10%** reservation for **Economically Weaker Sections**
  - **104th:** Ended **Anglo-Indian** nominations — effective **January 2020**
- **106th Amendment (2023):** About **one-third** reservation for **women** in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies

## Important statutes | from: 22, 43
# 1. Representation of the People Acts
- **RPA, 1950:** Framework for **seat allocation**, **electoral rolls** and **Legislative Council composition** — associated with **Section 10**, **Schedule 3** and **Article 171**
- **RPA, 1951:** **Registration of political parties** by the Election Commission of India; **conduct of elections**; **disqualification on conviction**
  - Convicted and sentenced to at least **two years** → disqualified during the imprisonment and for **six years** after release
# 2. Rights and accountability laws
- **Protection of Civil Rights Act:** **1955**
- **Maintenance of Internal Security Act (MISA):** **1971** — an **internal security** statute, **not** a social welfare act
- **Child Labour (Prohibition & Regulation) Act:** **1986**
- **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act:** **1989** — **23 sections**; effective **1990** (enforced **30 January 1990**)
- **Protection of Human Rights Act:** **1993** — enforced **28 September 1993**; concerns **life, liberty, equality and dignity**
  - Established the **NHRC** (on **12 October 1993**), **State Human Rights Commissions** and **Human Rights Courts**
- **PESA — Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act:** **1996**
- **Right to Information Act (2005):** Assent **15 June 2005**; enforced/effective **12 October 2005**
  - A legal right supporting **transparency** and **accountability**; rooted in the **MKSS** movement
  - **Namit Sharma Case (2012)** related to the RTI Act
- **Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act:** **2005** — enforced **26 October 2006**
- **National Green Tribunal Act:** **2010**
# 3. Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 (in effect 2014)
- **Selection Committee — five members:**
  - **Prime Minister** (Chair)
  - **Speaker** of the Lok Sabha
  - **Leader of the Opposition** in the Lok Sabha
  - **Chief Justice of India** or a Supreme Court nominee
  - An **eminent jurist**
  - ⚠ **Trap:** The **Rajya Sabha Chairman** is **not** a member
- **Minimum age of the Lokpal:** **45** years
- **Covers:** The **Prime Minister** and public servants **inside and outside India**
# 4. Panchayati Raj milestones
- **Community Development Programme (1952):** First major rural development programme after independence
- **National Extension Service:** **1953**
- **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957):** Recommended a **three-tier** model
- **Panchayati Raj first launched:** **2 October 1959**, **Nagaur (Rajasthan)**, inaugurated by **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- **Committees:** **Ashok Mehta (1977)**, **G.V.K. Rao (1985)**, **L.M. Singhvi (1986)**

## Polity firsts — persons and offices | from: 1
# 1. Heads of State and Government
- **First President:** **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** (from **Bihar**)
- **First woman President:** **Pratibha Patil**; **first tribal woman President:** **Droupadi Murmu**
- **First Vice-President:** **Dr. S. Radhakrishnan**
- **First Deputy Prime Minister:** **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**
- **First PM who was a Rajya Sabha member when appointed:** **Indira Gandhi**
- **First Finance Minister of independent India:** **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty** — some sources say **John Mathai**
# 2. Parliament
- **First Speaker of the Lok Sabha:** **G.V. Mavalankar** — also the first Speaker of the **Central Legislative Assembly** (**1925**)
- **First general election:** **1951–52**; first session of the First Lok Sabha: **13 May 1952**
- **First no-confidence motion:** Moved by **Acharya J.B. Kripalani** against Nehru's government, **Third Lok Sabha**, **1963**
- **First joint sitting:** **1961** — on the **Dowry Prohibition Bill**
- **Leader of the Opposition** first recognised in the Lok Sabha: **1969**
- **First actress nominated to the Rajya Sabha:** **Nargis Dutt**, **1980**
# 3. Judiciary, states and parties
- **First woman Supreme Court Judge:** **Justice M. Fathima Beevi**, **1989** — also the first Muslim woman in the higher judiciary
- **First woman Governor:** **Sarojini Naidu** (**Uttar Pradesh**)
- **First woman Chief Minister:** **Sucheta Kripalani** (**Uttar Pradesh**)
- **First President of the Bharatiya Jana Sangh:** **Dr. Shyama Prasad Mukherjee**
- **First President of the BJP:** **Atal Bihari Vajpayee**, **1980**

## Polity firsts — institutions, bodies and events | from: 1
# 1. Forms of government and doctrines
- **Parliamentary form of government:** First introduced in **Great Britain**
- **Presidential form of government:** First formed in the **USA**
- **Judicial Review:** First propounded in the **USA** — **Marbury v. Madison (1803)**
- **Basic Structure doctrine:** First propounded in **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**
- **Fundamental Rights amendable:** First held in the **Shankari Prasad** case
- **First Public Interest Litigation:** **Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)**
- **First Schedule:** Lists the **States and Union Territories**
# 2. Committees, commissions and bodies
- **Estimates Committee:** First constituted in **1950**
- **Ethics Committees:** **Rajya Sabha (1997)** — the first in any Indian legislature; **Lok Sabha** first constituted in **2000**
- **Finance Commission:** First Chairman **K.C. Neogy**; constituted in **1951** (some sources: set up in **1952**)
- **Planning Commission:** First Chairman (ex officio) **Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru**; first Deputy Chairman **Gulzarilal Nanda**
- **NITI Aayog:** First Vice-Chairman **Arvind Panagariya**
- **Inter-State Council:** First established in **May or December 1990**
- **First Chief Election Commissioner:** **Sukumar Sen** (**1950 to 1958**); first woman CEC: **V.S. Ramadevi**
- **VVPAT first used:** **Noksen** Assembly by-election, **Nagaland**, **4 September 2013**
- **NHRC first Chairman:** **Justice Ranganath Misra** (**1993**)
- **First Law Commission of independent India:** Chairman **M.C. Setalvad**, **1955**
- **First Official Language Commission:** **1955**, headed by **B.G. Kher**
- **First Classical Language:** **Tamil**
# 3. Ombudsman, Lokpal and Lokayukta
- **Ombudsman:** First created in **Sweden**, **1809**
- **First Administrative Reforms Commission (1966):** Recommended **Lokpal and Lokayuktas** — interim report submitted by **Morarji Desai**
- **First Lokpal Bill:** **1968**; **first Lokpal of India:** **Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose**
- **First state with a Lokayukta:** **Maharashtra** (**1971 or 1972**); first to pass a Lokayukta Act: **Odisha**
# 4. Emergency and local government
- **President's Rule (Article 356) first imposed:** **PEPSU, 1953** — some sources say **Punjab, 1951**
- **First Municipal Corporation:** **Madras**, **1688**
- **Panchayati Raj:** **Rajasthan** the first state, **Andhra Pradesh** the second; **Madhya Pradesh** first to implement the 73rd Amendment in its true spirit
