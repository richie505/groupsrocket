=== TOPIC indian-polity:14 | Parliament - legislative procedure and rules

## Kinds of Bills | from: 21
# 1. Basics
- **Bill:** Proposed legislation — becomes an **Act** only after the **President's assent** (**Article 111**)
- **Parliament** = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha** — the President is an **integral part** of Parliament but **not a member** of either House
- **Private Member's Bill:** Introduced by an MP who is **not a Minister** — not necessarily a nominated member
# 2. Money Bill — Articles 109 and 110
- **Introduced:** **Only in the Lok Sabha**, with the President's **prior recommendation**
- **Contents:** Provisions on **taxes**, **borrowings**, **custody** of and **withdrawal** from the **Consolidated Fund of India**
  - A Bill dealing merely with **fines or penalties** is **not** a Money Bill
- **Rajya Sabha:** Only a **recommendatory** role — recommendations within **14 days**; **cannot amend or reject** it
- **Certification:** The **Speaker's** decision (**Article 110(3)**) is **final**
- **Joint Sitting:** **Not** possible
- **Every Money Bill is a Financial Bill** — but **not every Financial Bill is a Money Bill**
# 3. Financial Bills — Article 117
- **Financial Bill (I) — Article 117(1):** Contains **Article 110** matters **plus general legislation**
  - **Only in the Lok Sabha**; needs the President's **recommendation** for introduction
  - The Rajya Sabha can **reject or amend** it fully; **Joint Sitting** possible
- **Financial Bill (II) — Article 117(3):** Involves **expenditure** from the **Consolidated Fund of India**; **no Article 110** matters
  - In **either House**; President's recommendation needed **before consideration**; **Joint Sitting** possible
# 4. Comparison — Ordinary, Money and Constitution Amendment Bills
| Basis | Ordinary Bill | Money Bill | Constitution Amendment Bill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Articles | **107 and 108** | **109 and 110** | **368** |
| House of origin | **Either House** | **Lok Sabha only** | **Either House** — **not** a State Legislature |
| President's prior recommendation | **Not required** | **Required** | **Not required** |
| Rajya Sabha's power | **Equal** — may amend or reject; **no fixed time limit** | Only **recommendations** within **14 days** | **Equal** — passed **separately** by each House |
| Majority | **Simple** | **Simple** | **Special** — a majority of the **total membership** + **two-thirds** of members present and voting |
| Joint Sitting (Article 108) | **Yes** — in a deadlock | **No** | **No** |
| President's options | **Assent**, **withhold**, or **return once** | **Assent** or **withhold** — **cannot return** | **Must give assent** — **no veto** |
- **Federal provisions** in an Amendment Bill also need ratification by at least **one-half** of the **State Legislatures**

## Passing a Bill — readings, joint sittings, assent and lapse | from: 21
# 1. Readings
- **General discussion** on a Bill: **Second Reading** — **not** the First or Third Reading
# 2. Joint Sitting — Article 108
- **Summoned by the President** (who expresses the intention to summon it)
- **Presided over by** the **Lok Sabha Speaker** → in his absence the **Deputy Speaker** → then the **Deputy Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha
- **Quorum:** **One-tenth** of the total membership; questions decided by **simple majority**
- **Three** Joint Sittings so far:
  - **1961:** **Dowry Prohibition Bill**
  - **1978:** **Banking Service Commission Repeal Bill**
  - **2002:** **Prevention of Terrorism Act (POTA)**
# 3. Vetoes used
- **Pocket Veto:** President **Giani Zail Singh** — **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986**
- **Absolute Veto:** President **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** — **PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954**
# 4. Lapse of Bills on dissolution of the Lok Sabha
- **Lapses:** A Bill **pending in the Lok Sabha**; a Bill **passed by the Lok Sabha** but pending in the Rajya Sabha
- **Does not lapse:** A Bill pending in the **Rajya Sabha** not yet passed by the Lok Sabha; a Bill for which a **Joint Sitting** has been notified
- **Prorogation** does **not** cause a Bill to lapse
# 5. State Bills
- **Legislative Council** can delay an Ordinary Bill **three months** the first time and **one month** the second time — maximum **four months**
- **State Money Bill:** Originates in the **Legislative Assembly** with the **Governor's** recommendation; the Council gets **14 days**
- **Reservation:** The **Governor** can reserve a State Bill for the President — **Article 200**; the **President** considers it — **Article 201**
# 6. Articles at a glance
- **Article 108:** Joint Sittings; **Articles 109 and 110:** Money Bills; **Article 111:** Presidential Assent
- **Articles 112 to 114:** **Budget**, **Demands for Grants**, **Appropriation Bill**; **Article 116:** **Vote-on-Account**; **Article 117:** **Financial Bills**
- **Articles 200 and 201:** Governor's and President's assent to **State Bills**; **Article 368:** **Constitutional amendment**

## Budget and financial terms | from: 38
- **Annual Financial Statement (Article 112):** The constitutional name for the **Union Budget**
- **Appropriation Bill (Article 114):** Authorises **withdrawal** from the **Consolidated Fund of India** — for **voted grants** and **charged expenditure**
- **Vote on Account (Article 116):** An **advance grant** by the **Lok Sabha** for estimated expenditure for **part of the financial year**, pending passage of the budget
- **Vote of Credit:** An **unexpected**, **blank-check** grant — for an **emergency** or **indefinite** demand whose details **cannot be stated** in a budget
- **Charged Expenditure:** **Non-votable** — paid directly out of the **Consolidated Fund of India**; can only be **discussed**
- **Cut Motions:**
  - **Policy Cut:** Reduce a demand to **₹1** — **complete disapproval** of the underlying policy
  - **Economy Cut:** Reduce by a **specified amount** — demands **economy** in expenditure
  - **Token Cut:** Reduce by **₹100** — ventilates a **specific grievance** within the Government's responsibility

## Rules of procedure in the Lok Sabha — quick recap | from: 29, 38
# 1. Rules vs Constitution
- **Constitution:** Gives the broad institutional framework; day-to-day proceedings follow the **Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha**
- **Not in the Constitution** — derive from parliamentary **rules or conventions:** **No-Confidence Motion**, **Zero Hour**, **Rule 377**
- **Primary mechanisms for executive accountability:** **Question Hour**, **Supplementary Questions**, **Adjournment Motions**
# 2. Motions
- **No-Confidence Motion (Rule 198):** **Lok Sabha only**; against the **entire Council of Ministers** — **not** an individual minister
  - **Written notice** to the **Secretary-General**; support of at least **50** members for leave; **no reasons** need be stated
  - Tests the **majority support** of the Council of Ministers
- **Confidence Motion:** Moved by the **Government** (Council of Ministers) — demonstrates majority on the floor
- **Censure Motion:** Strong disapproval of **specific policies** — against an **individual minister** or the **entire Council**; **reasons** must be stated
- **Rule 184:** A **substantive** motion — discussion on a specific matter of public interest **followed by voting**; significant political consequences
- **Rule 377:** Raise matters of **general public importance** that cannot be raised under Questions, Calling Attention or other Motions
  - **Written notice** + the **Speaker's consent**; daily limit **20 → 30** in **2015**
- **Adjournment Motion:** **Interrupts / suspends** normal business — a **definite** matter of **urgent public importance** and **recent occurrence**; element of **censure or condemnation**
- **Calling Attention Motion:** **Indian** innovation — draws a **Minister's attention** to an urgent matter; **resembles** an Adjournment Motion but has **no censure** element
- **Closure Motion:** Cuts short debate and puts the matter to **vote**
  - **Guillotine:** A form of closure when the allotted time **expires** — all remaining **discussed and undiscussed** clauses or **demands for grants** go to vote immediately
# 3. Question Hour, Zero Hour and questions
- **Question Hour:** **First hour** of every sitting — **11 AM to 12 Noon**; a **formal** procedure under the **Rules of Procedure**; for asking and answering **classified questions**; lasts **one hour**
- **Zero Hour:** Follows Question Hour — **12 Noon to 1 PM**; **Indian** innovation of the early **1960s**; **informal** convention; **urgent matters** of great public importance without prior formal notice; generally up to **one hour**
- **Starred:** **Oral** answer on the floor; **supplementaries** allowed; at most **20** per day
- **Unstarred:** **Written** answer laid on the table; **no** supplementaries
- **Short Notice:** **Urgent** matter of public importance; notice shorter than **10 clear days**; **oral** answer; supplementaries allowed
# 4. Other rules and constitutional basis
- **Rule 8(1):** Election of the **Deputy Speaker** — date fixed by the **Speaker**, notified by the **Secretary-General**
- **Money Bill:** **Article 110**; **Joint Sitting:** **Article 108**

## Parliamentary and constitutional glossary | from: 38
# 1. Sessions and sittings
- **Summoning:** By the **President** — **Article 85**; maximum gap between two sessions **six months**
- **Session:** From the **first sitting** of a House to its **prorogation or dissolution** — standard sessions: **Budget, Monsoon, Winter**
- **Sitting:** A **daily** meeting of a House during a session — **morning** and **post-lunch** sittings
- **Adjournment:** Ends a daily sitting for a specified time — **hours, days or weeks** — by the **Presiding Officer** (Speaker or Chairman)
- **Adjournment sine die:** Ends a sitting **without** assigning a day for reassembly
- **Prorogation (Article 85(2)):** By the **President** (or **Governor**) — terminates a **session**, **not** the House itself
- **Dissolution:** By the **President** (or **Governor**) — ends the **life of the House**; applies **only to the Lok Sabha** (the **Rajya Sabha** is permanent)
- **Recess:** Period between **prorogation** and the start of a new session — the President may issue **ordinances** (**Article 123**) during recess
- **Quorum (Article 100):** Minimum members needed to conduct business — **one-tenth** of the total membership
# 2. Writs — recap
| Writ | Meaning | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | "**To have the body**" | Quashes illegal or unlawful physical detention |
| **Mandamus** | "**We Command**" | Directs a **public official**, **inferior court** or **statutory authority** to perform a mandatory public or legal duty |
| **Prohibition** | "**To Forbid**" | **Preventive** — a higher court stops an inferior court or **tribunal** from exceeding its jurisdiction |
| **Certiorari** | "**To be Certified**" | A higher court **quashes an order already passed** — for **lack of jurisdiction** or an **error of law**; **curative and preventive** |
| **Quo-Warranto** | "**By what warrant/authority**" | Inquires into the legality of a claim to a **public office** — prevents illegal usurpation |
# 3. Judicial review and basic structure
- **Judicial Review:** An **inherent power** of constitutional courts — examines **legislative enactments** and **executive actions** against constitutional validity
- **Basic Structure Doctrine:** **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — core structural features cannot be **abrogated or destroyed**, even through **Article 368**
# 4. Pardoning terms
- **Pardon:** Completely **absorbs and removes** both the **sentence** and the **conviction** — the offender is in the same position as if the offence had **never been committed**
- **Commutation:** Substitutes a **lighter** form of punishment for a heavier one — e.g. **death sentence → rigorous life imprisonment**
- **Remission:** Reduces the **period** of a sentence without changing its **character** — e.g. **two years** of rigorous imprisonment → **one year**
- **Respite:** Awards a **lesser sentence** in place of the original penalty, due to **special personal circumstances** — **pregnancy**, **mental** or **physical disability**
- **Reprieve:** A **temporary stay / postponement** of execution — especially for the **death penalty** — giving time to seek a **Presidential pardon**
# 5. Elections and representation
- **First-Past-the-Post:** A **plurality** system — the candidate with the **highest number of valid votes** wins; **no absolute majority** (over **50 percent**) needed
- **PR-STV** (Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote): **Multi-preference**, **quota-based**, **indirect** — to ensure **proportional representation**
  - Used to elect the **President**, **Vice-President**, **Rajya Sabha** and **Legislative Councils**
- **Delimitation:** Statutory process of **redrawing constituency boundaries** on recent **census** figures — enforces **equal representation**
- **Right to vote:** A **legal / statutory** right — **not** a Fundamental Right
# 6. Other terms
- **Dyarchy:** "**Double Rule**" — introduced in the provinces by the **GoI Act, 1919**
  - **Reserved** subjects: **Governor and Executive Council**; **Transferred** subjects: **Governor and popular Ministers**
- **Collective Responsibility (Article 75(3)):** The Council of Ministers is collectively answerable to the **Lok Sabha** (the **popular** House) — all ministers **sink or swim together**
- **Panchayati Raj:** **Three-tier** system everywhere — intermediate tier **Panchayat Samiti** is **optional** in States with a population of **20 lakh** or less
