=== TOPIC indian-polity:5 | Fundamental Rights and writs

## Fundamental Rights — features and history | from: 15, 16
# 1. Basics
- **Location:** **Part III** — **Articles 12 to 35** (**24** Articles)
- **Borrowed from:** Mainly the **Bill of Rights** of the **USA**
- **Number:** Originally **seven**; presently **six**
  - **Right to Property** removed by the **44th Amendment (1978)** — **Article 19(1)(f)** deleted; now a legal right under **Article 300-A**
- **Available against the State** — **Article 12** defines the State
- **Justiciable:** Enforceable by courts — the **judiciary** (Supreme Court and High Courts) is the guardian of Fundamental Rights
# 2. Milestones in the demand
- **1895:** First demand — **Swaraj Bill**
- **1928:** **Motilal Nehru Report**
- **1931:** **Karachi Session** of the Congress
# 3. Citizens only vs everyone
- **Only citizens (five rights):** **Articles 15, 16, 19, 29 and 30**
- **Citizens and foreigners:** The remaining rights — **Articles 14, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27 and 28**
# 4. Amendment and suspension
- **Parliament can amend** Fundamental Rights but **cannot destroy the Basic Structure** — **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**
- **During a National Emergency** Fundamental Rights can be suspended:
  - **Article 358:** **Article 19** suspended **automatically** — only in case of **war** or **external aggression**
  - **Article 359:** **President** (by order) suspends the enforcement of other Fundamental Rights — **except Articles 20 and 21** (after the **44th Amendment**)

## The six Fundamental Rights | from: 15, 16
# 1. Right to Equality — Articles 14 to 18 (five Articles)
- **Article 14:** **Equality before law**
- **Article 15:** **Prohibits discrimination**
- **Article 16:** **Equality of opportunity** in public employment
- **Article 17:** Abolishes **untouchability**
- **Article 18:** Abolishes **titles**
# 2. Right to Freedom — Articles 19 to 22 (four Articles)
- **Article 19 — six freedoms:**
  - **19(1)(a):** **Speech and expression**
  - **19(1)(b):** **Assembly**
  - **19(1)(c):** **Association, union and cooperative societies** (cooperative societies added by the **97th Amendment, 2011**)
  - **19(1)(d):** **Movement**
  - **19(1)(e):** **Residence**
  - **19(1)(g):** **Profession, occupation, trade and business**
  - **19(1)(f):** Originally the **Right to Property** — deleted
- **Reasonable restrictions:** **Eight** grounds for speech; other freedoms **three to four** grounds
- **Article 20:** Protection in respect of **conviction** for offences
- **Article 21:** Protection of **life and personal liberty**
- **Article 21-A:** **Right to Education** — added by the **86th Amendment (2002)**
- **Article 22:** Protection against **arrest and detention** — preventive detention without an advisory board — maximum period **three months**
# 3. Right against Exploitation — Articles 23 and 24 (two Articles)
- **Article 23:** Prohibits **traffic in human beings** and **forced labour**
- **Article 24:** Prohibits **employment of children** below **14 years** in **factories** and similar establishments
# 4. Right to Freedom of Religion — Articles 25 to 28 (four Articles)
- **Article 25:** Freedom of **conscience**; freely **profess, practise and propagate** religion
- **Article 26:** Freedom to **manage religious affairs**
- **Article 27:** Freedom from **taxes** for the promotion of any religion
- **Article 28:** Freedom from attending **religious instruction** in certain educational institutions
# 5. Cultural and Educational Rights — Articles 29 and 30 (two Articles)
- **Article 29:** Protects the **interests of minorities**
- **Article 30:** Minorities' right to **establish and administer** educational institutions
# 6. Right to Constitutional Remedies — Article 32 (one Article)
- Right to **move the Supreme Court** for enforcement of Fundamental Rights — itself a Fundamental Right
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** "**Heart and Soul**" of the Constitution

## Writs — Article 32 and Article 226 | from: 15, 16, 42
# 1. Writ jurisdiction
- **Origin:** Prerogative remedies borrowed from **English common law**
- **Supreme Court — Article 32:** **Narrower** — only for **Fundamental Rights** (Part III); a direct and effective Fundamental Right to constitutional remedies
- **High Courts — Article 226:** **Wider** — can be approached for Fundamental Rights **and any other purpose**, including ordinary statutory or legal rights
- **Article 32(3):** Parliament may by law empower **any other court** to exercise these powers within its **local jurisdiction**
- **Five writs:** **Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto**
# 2. The five writs
| Writ | Literal meaning | Issued against | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | "**To have the body**" / "Let us have the body" | **Public authorities** and **private individuals** | Release from **unlawful or illegal detention** — safeguards **individual liberty** (bodily freedom) |
| **Mandamus** | "**We command**" | **Public officials**, **statutory bodies**, **inferior courts**, **tribunals** | Compels performance of a **mandatory public or legal duty** refused or neglected |
| **Prohibition** | "**To forbid**" / "To restrain" | **Judicial and quasi-judicial bodies only** | A **higher court** stops a **lower court** or tribunal from **exceeding** or **usurping** jurisdiction — while the proceeding is **pending** |
| **Certiorari** | "**To be certified**" / "To be informed" | **Judicial, quasi-judicial and administrative** bodies | **Quashes** an illegal order **already passed** — lack or excess of jurisdiction, or error of law on the face of the record (**postmortem** review) |
| **Quo-Warranto** | "**By what authority?**" / "By what warrant?" | A person **holding or claiming** a substantive **public office** | Prevents **illegal usurpation** of a **permanent public office** created by **statute or the Constitution** |
# 3. Key distinctions
- **Habeas Corpus:** Applies whenever detention (custody) is illegal — the **only** writ directly against **private individuals** in cases of illegal confinement
- **Mandamus:**
  - Does **not** lie against purely **private bodies** unless they discharge a **public duty**
  - Does **not** lie against the **President** or **Governors**
  - **Can** lie against a **government company** or **statutory corporation** performing a public function
- **Prohibition vs Certiorari:**
  - **Prohibition:** **Before** the order — halts a pending proceeding to prevent **excess of jurisdiction**
  - **Certiorari:** **After** the order — corrects or quashes decisions with **jurisdictional error**
- **Quo-Warranto:** Challenges the legal **qualification or title** of a person holding public office
  - **Any public-minded citizen** (any member of the public) can move a petition — **no aggrieved-party** status needed (the petitioner need not be personally aggrieved); the traditional **locus standi** requirement is relaxed
