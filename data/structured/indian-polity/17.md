=== TOPIC indian-polity:17 | Supreme Court and judges

## Supreme Court — establishment, Chief Justice and judges | from: 30, 69
# 1. Establishment
- **Part V, Chapter IV:** **Articles 124 to 147**
- **Inaugurated:** **28 January 1950** — succeeded the **Federal Court** (set up under the **GoI Act, 1935**) and the **Judicial Committee of the Privy Council**
- **Article 124:** Establishment and constitution of the Supreme Court; appointment of judges
  - **124(1):** **Parliament** alone can increase the number of judges by law
  - **124(2):** Judges retire at **65 years**
- **Article 130 — seat:** **Delhi** — the Chief Justice may decide other places for sittings with the **President's** approval
# 2. Chief Justice of India
- **Heads** the Union Judiciary
- **Appointed** by the **President** — **Article 124(2)**; by **convention**, the **senior-most** SC judge
- **Term:** No fixed term in years — holds office until **65 years** of age
- **Oath:** By the **President** (or a person he appoints) — form in the **Third Schedule**
- **Resignation:** In writing to the **President**
# 3. Acting, ad hoc and retired judges
- **Acting Chief Justice — Article 126:** Appointed by the **President** when the CJI's office is **vacant**, the CJI is **absent** or **unable** to perform duties
- **Ad hoc judges — Article 127:** Appointed by the **Chief Justice**, with the **previous consent of the President** and after consulting the concerned **High Court Chief Justice** — to meet a lack of **quorum**
- **Retired judges — Article 128:** The Chief Justice may request them to attend sittings, with the **President's** prior consent
# 4. Court of Record — Article 129
- **Judgments** have **evidentiary value** and **cannot be questioned** when produced before a subordinate court
- **Power to punish for contempt** of itself — an **inherent** power
- High Courts have parallel status — **Article 215**
# 5. Removal of judges — Article 124(4)
- **By order of the President** after an **Address** by Parliament
- **Grounds:** **Proved misbehaviour** or **incapacity**
- **Special majority** in **each House**, in the **same session**: a **majority of the total membership** + at least **two-thirds** of members present and voting
- **Statutory inquiry:** Procedure under the **Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968**

## Jurisdiction and powers of the Supreme Court | from: 30, 69
# 1. Original jurisdiction — Article 131 (exclusive)
- **Federal disputes involving a legal right:**
  - **Centre vs one or more States**
  - **Centre and one or more States vs other States**
  - **One State vs another State**
- **Excludes:**
  - **Political questions** that involve no legal right
  - Treaties and agreements made **before the Constitution** (pre-Constitution treaties)
  - **Inter-State water disputes** — **Article 262**
  - **Finance Commission** matters — **Article 280**
  - Recovery of damages by a **private party** against the Union
# 2. Writ jurisdiction — Article 32
- A **Fundamental Right** to approach the SC **directly** for enforcement of **Part III** rights — the SC **cannot refuse** an Article 32 petition
- **Writs:** **Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto**
- **Narrower** than the High Courts' — **Article 226** covers Fundamental Rights **and legal rights**
# 3. Appellate jurisdiction
- **Article 132 — constitutional:** High Court judgments involving a **substantial question of law** on **interpreting the Constitution** — needs a High Court **certificate** (**Article 134A**)
- **Article 133 — civil:** Civil proceedings with a **substantial question of law of general importance** — the High Court certifies under **Article 134A** that the SC must decide it
- **Article 134 — criminal:** Where a High Court **reversed an acquittal into a death sentence**, or **withdrew a trial** to itself — capital-punishment reversals appealable **as of right**, **without** a certificate
- **Article 136 — Special Leave Petition:** **Discretionary**, **plenary and residual** power — special leave to appeal from **any judgment or order** of **any court or tribunal**
  - **Article 136(2):** **Not** against **courts-martial / Military Tribunals / Armed Forces Courts**
# 4. Review, advice and complete justice
- **Article 137 — review:** The SC reviews its **own judgments, orders and pronouncements** — distinct from **judicial review of statutes**; subject to Parliamentary law and court rules
- **Article 143 — advisory:** The **President** refers questions of **law or fact** of **public importance**
  - **143(1):** SC **may** tender (or **refuse**) its opinion on matters of public importance
  - **143(2):** SC **must** (is bound to) tender its opinion on **pre-constitutional** disputes
  - Opinions are **strictly advisory** — **not binding** on the President
- **Article 141:** Law declared by the SC is **binding on all courts** in India
- **Article 142:** Pass any decree or order for **complete justice** in any cause pending before it — unhindered by ordinary statutory limitations
- **Curative petition:** Evolved in **Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)** — from the SC's **inherent powers** anchored in **Article 142**
  - Available after a **review petition** (Article 137) is **dismissed**; prevents **abuse of process** and cures **gross miscarriage of justice**
# 5. Constitution Bench — Article 145(3)
- At least **five judges** — for cases with a **substantial question of law** on interpreting the Constitution, and for **Article 143** references

## Collegium system, the Judges Cases and the NJAC | from: 30, 69, 47
# 1. The Collegium
- **Governs:** **Appointment and transfer** of **Supreme Court** and **High Court** judges
- **Not** a constitutional body — **not** expressly in the Constitution, **not** created by an Act of Parliament — **judicially evolved** through the **Judges Cases** (Supreme Court rulings)
- **Formal appointment:** By the **President** — SC judges under **Article 124(2)**, HC judges under **Article 217(1)** — on the Collegium's **binding** recommendations ("consultation" with the prescribed collegium = concurrence)
- **Executive representation:** **Zero**
# 2. The Judges Cases
| Case | Year | Held |
|---|---|---|
| **First Judges Case — S.P. Gupta v. Union of India** | **1981** | Consultation does **not** mean **concurrence** — **executive primacy (supremacy)** |
| **Second Judges Case — SCAORA v. Union of India** | **1993** | Reversed the first: consultation **means concurrence** — **judicial primacy**; the **Collegium** was born — **CJI + two** senior-most judges |
| **Third Judges Case** — from a **Presidential Reference** | **1998** | Clarified the Collegium's working; the CJI's opinion must reflect **plural consultation**; expanded the SC Collegium to **CJI + four** senior-most judges |
| **Fourth Judges Case** | **2015** | **NJAC struck down** as unconstitutional — violated the **independence of the judiciary**; the Collegium **restored** and operational from **2015** |
# 3. Collegium compositions
- **SC appointments and elevations:** **CJI + four** senior-most SC judges — **five** members
- **High Court appointments:** Initiated by the **HC Chief Justice + two** senior-most HC judges → forwarded to the **SC Collegium** of the **CJI + two** senior-most SC judges
- **High Court transfers:** **CJI + four** senior-most SC judges, consulting the Chief Justices of the **transferring** and **receiving** High Courts
# 4. National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC)
- **Created by:** The **99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014** and the **NJAC Act, 2014** (2014–15)
- **Six members** (total strength):
  - **Chief Justice of India** — Chairperson
  - **Two** senior SC judges
  - **Union Law Minister** — a permanent member
  - **Two eminent persons** — nominated by the **Prime Minister**, the **CJI** and the **Leader of the Opposition**
- **Struck down (2015):** **Executive involvement** infringed the **independence of the judiciary** — part of the **basic structure** of the Constitution

## Supreme Court judges vs High Court judges | from: 71
# 1. Comparison
| Basis | Supreme Court Judge | High Court Judge |
|---|---|---|
| Provisions | **Part V, Chapter IV** — **Articles 124–147** (**Article 124**) | **Part VI, Chapter V** — **Articles 214–231** (**Article 217**) |
| Appointed by | **President** — by **warrant** under his hand and seal | **President** — by **warrant**; the **Governor has no power** to appoint |
| Oath | **President** — **Article 124(6)** | **Governor** — **Article 219** |
| Resignation to | **President** — **Article 124(2)(a)** | **President** — **Article 217(1)(a)** |
| Retirement age | **65** | **62** — raised from **60** by the **15th Amendment Act, 1963** |
| Distinguished jurist | **Eligible** — **Article 124(3)(c)** | **Not** eligible — no such provision |
| Transfer | **Not** applicable | **Article 222** — by the **President** on the advice of the **collegium** led by the **CJI** |
| Pleading after retirement | **Completely barred** — no court or authority in India (**Article 124(7)**) | Only in the **Supreme Court** and **other High Courts** where not a permanent judge (**Article 220**) |
| Salary charged on | **Consolidated Fund of India** | **Consolidated Fund of the State** — **Article 202(3)** |
| Pension charged on | **Consolidated Fund of India** | **Consolidated Fund of India** (not the State) — **Article 112(3)(d)(iii)** |
| Court of record | **Article 129** | **Article 215** |
- **Removal of both:** By the **President's** order after a parliamentary **Address** passed by **each House** by **special majority** — at least **50%** of the **total strength** and **two-thirds** of members present and voting — for **proved misbehaviour** or **incapacity**
# 2. Other High Court judges
- **Acting Chief Justice — Article 223:** Appointed by the **President** from among the HC judges — when the Chief Justice's office is **vacant**, or he is **temporarily absent** or **unable** to perform duties
- **Additional Judges — Article 224(1):** By the **President** — for a **temporary increase** in business or **arrears** of work; for a period not exceeding **two years**, and not beyond **62** years of age
- **Acting Judges — Article 224(2):** By the **President** — when a permanent judge is **temporarily absent** or **acting as Chief Justice**; ceases at **62**
- **Retired judges — Article 224A:** The HC **Chief Justice** may request a **retired HC or SC judge** to sit and act as an HC judge — with the **President's prior consent**
# 3. High Court Chief Justice — administrative capacity
- **Administrative acts** (e.g. **staff appointments**, **disciplinary actions**) are subject to **writ jurisdiction** under **Article 226** — another judge of the **same High Court** may exercise it
- **No absolute immunity** from judicial review of administrative decisions
