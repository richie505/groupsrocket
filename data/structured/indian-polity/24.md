=== TOPIC indian-polity:24 | Emergency provisions

## Emergency provisions — the three emergencies | from: 50
# 1. Framework
- **Part XVIII:** **Articles 352 to 360**
- **K.M. Nambiar:** Called the President's emergency powers a "**fraud on the Constitution**"
# 2. Comparison
| Basis | National Emergency — Article 352 | President's Rule — Article 356 | Financial Emergency — Article 360 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | **War**, **external aggression** or **armed rebellion** | **Failure of constitutional machinery** in a State | **Threat** to the **financial stability or credit** of India or any part |
| Approval by Parliament | Within **1 month** (was **2 months** — reduced by the **44th Amendment**) | Within **2 months** | Within **2 months** |
| Majority | **Special** majority in both Houses | **Simple** majority | **Simple** majority |
| Duration | **6 months** at a time — **no maximum** limit | **6 months** at a time — **maximum 3 years** | Until **revoked** by the President |
| Lok Sabha dissolved | Survives **30 days** from the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha | **30 days** | **30 days** |
| Effect | Parliament legislates on the **State List**; **Articles 358 and 359** operate | State Assembly **dissolved or suspended**; Parliament passes the **State Budget** | Executive gives **financial directions**; **salaries** may be cut — including **Supreme Court and High Court** judges |
| Fundamental Rights | **Articles 20 and 21** can never be suspended | Not directly suspended | Not directly suspended |
| Used | **3 times** — **1962, 1971, 1975** | Over **100** times across States | **Never** invoked since independence |
# 3. President's Rule — Article 356
- **When:** The government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution
- **How:** On the **Governor's report** or otherwise (**suo motu**)
- **Beyond 1 year** only if:
  - A **National Emergency** is in operation in all of India or any part of the State, **and**
  - The **Election Commission of India** certifies that Assembly elections cannot be held

## National Emergency — effects and the 44th Amendment | from: 50
# 1. Proclamation
- Only on the **written advice** of the **Union Cabinet**
- **1975 Emergency:** Proclaimed **25 June 1975** on the ground of **Internal Disturbance**; withdrawn **21 March 1977**
# 2. Effects on legislation and finance
- **State List:** Parliament gets **overriding** jurisdiction — such laws **cease 6 months** after the Emergency is **revoked**
- **Terms of the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies:** Parliament may extend them by law **1 year at a time** — at most **6 months** after the Emergency ceases
- **Article 354:** The President may **modify** the distribution of **revenues** between the Union and the States — valid until the end of the **financial year** in which the proclamation ceases
# 3. Article 358 vs Article 359
| Basis | Article 358 | Article 359 |
|---|---|---|
| What | **Automatically suspends** the **6** freedoms of **Article 19** | Suspends the right to **approach courts** for enforcement of specified Fundamental Rights |
| How | **Automatic** on proclamation | Needs a **separate Presidential Order** |
| Ground | Only **war** or **external aggression** (external emergency) — **not** armed rebellion | Both **external** and **internal** (armed rebellion) emergencies |
| Exception | — | **Articles 20 and 21** can never be suspended under any circumstances |
# 4. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
- Replaced "**Internal Disturbance**" with "**Armed Rebellion**" in **Article 352**
- Cut the parliamentary approval time for a National Emergency from **2 months to 1 month**
- Established the **non-suspendability** of **Articles 20 and 21** — they can **never** be suspended under **Article 359**
