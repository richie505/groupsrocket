=== TOPIC physics:7 | Electricity and magnetism

## Electrical quantities and units | from: 16
| Quantity | SI unit | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Electric charge** | **Coulomb (C)** — also **esu** and **emu** | **Elementary charge** **1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C**; **one coulomb** = the charge of **6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons** |
| **Electric current** | **Ampere (A)** — **1 A = 1 C/s** (one coulomb per second) | **Small currents:** **milliamperes (mA)**, **microamperes (μA)**; **conventional direction** **positive → negative**; **electron flow** **negative → positive** |
| **Potential difference** / **voltage** / **emf** | **Volt (V)** — **1 V = 1 J/C** (one joule per coulomb) | — |
| **Resistance** | **Ohm (Ω)** — **1 Ω = 1 V/A** (one volt per ampere) | **High resistance:** **kilohms (kΩ)**, **megohms (MΩ)** |
| **Resistivity** | **Ohm-metre (Ω m)** — also **ohm-centimetres (Ω cm)** | — |
| **Conductivity** | **Siemens per metre (S/m)** | The **reciprocal** of **resistivity** — **σ = 1/ρ** |
| **Conductance** | **Siemens (S)** — **older unit** **mho** | The **reciprocal** of **resistance** — **G = 1/R** |
| **Power** | **Watt (W)** — **commercial:** **kilowatt (kW)**, **megawatt (MW)** | **One kilowatt** = **1000 watts**; **one horsepower** = **746 watts** |
# 1. Electrical energy
- **One kilowatt-hour:** The energy consumed by a **1 kW** device in **1 hour** — **3.6 × 10⁶ joules**
- **One unit of electricity** = **one kilowatt-hour** — the **board definition**: energy equivalent to **1 kWh**; **electricity cost** is **charged per unit** in **kWh**

## Resistance, materials and circuits | from: 16
# 1. Resistance and temperature
| Material | When temperature rises |
|---|---|
| **Metals** | Resistance **increases** |
| **Semiconductors** | Resistance **decreases** |
| **Electrolytes** | Resistance **decreases** |
| **Nichrome**, **constantan** | **Very small change** |
| **Superconductors** | **Zero** resistance **below** the **critical temperature** |
# 2. Classes of material
| Class | Resistivity | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Conductors** | **Very low** | **Silver** — the **best conductor** (**lowest resistivity**); **copper**; **aluminium** |
| **Alloys** | **Higher** than **pure metals** | **Nichrome**, **constantan**, **manganin** |
| **Semiconductors** | **Intermediate** | **Silicon**, **germanium** |
| **Insulators** | **Very high** | **Rubber**, **glass**, **plastic** |
# 3. Series and parallel
| Basis | Series | Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| **Current** | The **same current** flows through **all components** | **Current divides** — **inversely proportional** to **resistance** |
| **Voltage** | **Voltage divides** — total **V = V₁ + V₂ + …** | The **same voltage** across **all components** |
- **Kirchhoff's first law:** The **sum of currents** at a **junction** is **zero**
- **Kirchhoff's second law:** The **sum of voltage drops** in a **closed loop** is **zero**

## Effects of current and electrical safety | from: 16
# 1. Heating effect
- **Uses:** **Electric iron**, **heater**, **geyser**, **toaster**; **incandescent bulbs** — **old-style lighting**; **electric stoves** — **cooking** (**induction** devices **partly**); **electric fuses** — **protection**; **electric furnaces** — **industrial heating**; **welding**
# 2. Magnetic effect
- **Motors**, **fans**, **pumps**; **electric vehicles**
- **Generators** — **power stations**
- **Electromagnets** — **cranes**, **relays**, **MRI** devices
- **Measuring instruments** — **galvanometers**, **ammeters**, **voltmeters**
- **Communication** — **loudspeakers**, **microphones**; **storage** — **magnetic tapes**, **older hard disks**
# 3. Electric fuse
- **Principle:** The **heating effect** of current
- **Material:** A **low-melting-point alloy** — **tin-lead**
- **Melts** when current **exceeds** its **rated value** — **breaks the circuit**; melts **immediately** during a **short circuit**
- Placed **in series** with the **device** or **circuit** — **overcurrent protection** **prevents damage** due to **excess current**
- **Household wiring:** A **main fuse** and **individual circuit fuses**; **some appliances** have **built-in fuses**
# 4. Earthing
- **Protects** against **electric shock** — a **low-resistance path** to **ground** for **leakage current**
- Keeps the **metal body** of an appliance at **zero potential** — during a **fault**, **excess current** flows to **earth** and the **fuse blows**
- **Colour code:** **Green** or **green-yellow**
# 5. Short circuit and overloading
- **Short circuit:** **Live and neutral wires** **touch** each other **directly**; also **insulation failure**
- **Overloading:** **Too many high-power devices** — **very high current** → **heating** and a **fire hazard**
- **Prevention:** A **fuse** or **MCB**; **proper earthing**

## Generator, motor, transformer and induction | from: 16
# 1. Generator vs motor
| Basis | Generator | Motor |
|---|---|---|
| Principle | **Electromagnetic induction** — generators operate according to **Faraday’s law**: **induced emf** when **magnetic flux changes** | **Force** on a **current-carrying conductor** in a **magnetic field** — **Fleming's left-hand rule**; a **current-carrying coil** experiences **torque** |
| Converts | **Mechanical → electrical** | **Electrical → mechanical** |
| Input | **Mechanical energy** from **rotation** | **Electrical energy** |
| Output | **Electrical energy** | **Mechanical energy** — **rotation** |
| Working | A **coil rotates** in a **magnetic field** — **induces emf and current** | Electrical energy produces **torque and rotation** |
| Components | **Armature**, **field magnets**, **slip rings** or a **commutator**, **brushes** | **Armature**, **magnets**, a **commutator**, **brushes** |
| Types | **AC generator** — an **alternator**; **DC generator** | — |
# 2. Transformer
- **Principle:** **Mutual induction** — requires a **varying magnetic field**
- **Components:** **Primary coil**, **secondary coil**, a **soft iron core**
- **Requires** **alternating current** — AC provides **changing magnetic flux**, which **induces emf**
- ⚠ Does **not work** with **direct current** after the **initial moment** — **no change in flux**
# 3. Rectifier and inverter
| Device | Converts | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectifier** | **AC → DC** | Uses a **diode** — **half-wave**, **full-wave**, **bridge**; used in **power supplies** and **chargers** |
| **Inverter** | **DC → AC** | **Alternating voltage** for **AC appliances** — **UPS**, **solar power** and **battery backup** systems |
# 4. Uses of electromagnetic induction
- **Generators** — **power generation**; **transformers** — **voltage conversion**
- **Induction cooktops** — **heating**
- **Wireless charging** — **mobile phones** and **electric vehicles**
- **Magnetic brakes** in **trains**
- **Dynamic microphones**; **induction motors** (**industrial**)
- **EMI sensors**; **metal detectors**
