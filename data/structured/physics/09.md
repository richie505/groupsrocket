## Temperature behaviour and doping | from: 15
# 1. Effect of temperature
- When **temperature rises**, a semiconductor's **conductivity increases** and its **resistivity** / **resistance** **decreases** (**falls**)
  - **More covalent bonds break** — **more free electrons and holes**
  - The **intrinsic carrier concentration** **increases exponentially** with temperature
- **Opposite** to **metals** — a semiconductor has a **negative temperature coefficient** of resistance; **metals** a **positive** one
- At **0 K:** A **pure semiconductor** has **high resistance** — acts as an **insulator**
- A **doped** semiconductor has **lower resistance** than an **intrinsic** one
# 2. Doping
- The **controlled addition** of **impurity atoms** to a **pure semiconductor** — to **increase conductivity** in a **controlled manner**
- **Dopant concentration:** **Very small** — **one impurity atom** in **10⁶ to 10⁸** atoms
- Produces an **extrinsic semiconductor** — **donor doping** = **n-type**; **acceptor doping** = **p-type**
# 3. n-type vs p-type
| Basis | n-type | p-type |
|---|---|---|
| **Dopant** | **Pentavalent** — **Group 15**: **phosphorus**, **arsenic**, **antimony** — a **donor** | **Trivalent** — **Group 13**: **boron**, **aluminium**, **gallium**, **indium** — an **acceptor** |
| **Effect** | The **extra electron** becomes a **free electron** | The **deficiency** creates a **hole** |
| **Majority carriers** | **Electrons** — provide the **main conduction** | **Holes** |
| **Minority carriers** | **Holes** | **Electrons** |
| **Charge** | **Overall neutral** — **positive ions** balance the **extra electrons** | **Overall neutral** — **negative ions** balance the **holes** |
# 4. Charge carriers
- **Intrinsic semiconductor:** **Equal numbers** of **electrons and holes**
- **Electron:** **Negative charge** — conduction by **free electrons** in the **conduction band**
- **Hole:** A **vacancy** of an **electron** in a **covalent bond** — a **positive charge carrier**; its **apparent movement** is **opposite** to that of **electrons**

## Diodes and transistors | from: 15
# 1. Transistor — transfer resistor
- **Works** by **current amplification** or **switching** using **two p-n junctions** — types **n-p-n** and **p-n-p**
- **Emitter:** **Heavily doped**; **base:** **thin** and **lightly doped**; **collector:** **moderately doped**
- **Biasing:** **Emitter-base** junction **forward biased**; **collector-base** junction **reverse biased**
- A **small input current** **controls** a **large output current**
- **Uses:**
  - **Amplifier** — **increases** the **strength** of **weak signals**
  - **Switch** — **ON/OFF** control in **digital circuits**
  - **Oscillator** — **generates oscillating signals**
  - **Building blocks** of **integrated circuits** in **computers** and **mobile devices**
  - **Signal processing** in **radio and television**; **switching high-power loads**
# 2. Diodes
| Device | Use |
|---|---|
| **Rectifier** | **Converts AC to DC** — **half-wave** or **full-wave** |
| **LED** — **Light Emitting Diode** (LEDs) | **Indicators**, **displays**, **lighting** |
| **Zener diode** | **Voltage regulation** |
| **Photodiode** | **Light detection**; **solar cells** |
| **Solar cell** | **Converts light** to **electricity** |
| **Diode** (switch) | **Fast switching** in circuits |
| **Clipping** and **clamping** | **Wave-shaping circuits** |
| **Varactor** | **Voltage-controlled capacitance** |

## Superconductors | from: 15
- **Zero electrical resistance** **below** its **critical temperature** — the temperature below which **superconductivity appears**
- **Perfect diamagnetism** — the **Meissner effect** **expels** the **magnetic field**
- **Destroyed** above the **critical magnetic field**; the **critical current density** is the **maximum current** it can **carry**
- **Examples:** **Mercury** (**critical temperature 4.2 K**); **niobium alloys**; **high-Tc cuprates** — **YBCO**
- **Uses:** **MRI magnets**; **Maglev trains**; **lossless power transmission** in the **future**; **SQUIDs**
