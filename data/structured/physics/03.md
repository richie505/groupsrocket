=== TOPIC physics:3 | Fluids and elasticity

## Density, buoyancy and Pascal's law | from: 22
# 1. Density
- An object **floats** when its **density** is **less** than the **liquid's density**
- **Hydrometer** and **lactometer:** Measure the **relative density** of **liquids** — the **lactometer** **checks** the **purity of milk**
- **Centrifugation:** **Separates mixtures** — **denser particles** **settle**
- **Ships and submarines:** **Control** **average density** using **ballast** — submarines control **buoyancy** using **ballast tanks**
- **Atmospheric density:** **Decreases** with **height**
# 2. Pascal's law — hydraulics
- **Hydraulic lift** and **press:** **Multiply force**
- **Hydraulic brakes:** **Transmit pressure equally** to **all wheels**
- **Hydraulic jack:** **Lifts heavy vehicles**
- **Syringe:** **Pressure applied** at **one end** is **transmitted** to the **other end**
- **Excavators** and **bulldozers:** **Hydraulic systems** for **force amplification**
# 3. Buoyancy — Archimedes' principle
- **Cause:** **Higher hydrostatic pressure** at **greater depth** — pressure at the **bottom** of a submerged object is **greater** than at the **top**; the **net upward buoyant force** acts **vertically upward** through the **centre of buoyancy**
- **Ships and boats:** **Float** when their **weight equals** the **buoyant force**
- **Hot-air balloons** and **airships:** **Rise** when **buoyant force** is **greater** than **weight** — **heated air** has **lower density**, producing a **net upward force**
- **Life jackets:** **Increase** the **buoyant force**; buoyancy helps **swimmers** **stay afloat**
- **Iceberg:** Only a **small part** remains **above water** — **ice** is **less dense** than **water**
- **Fish bladder:** **Controls buoyancy** for **depth regulation**

## Surface tension and capillarity | from: 22
# 1. Surface tension
- **Water strider** / **insect walking on water:** The surface acts like an **elastic membrane**
- **Small liquid drops:** **Spherical** — **minimises surface area**; **molten metals** form a spherical shape in **space** and in **powder metallurgy**
- **Floating needle** / **razor blade:** Surface tension **supports** the **weight**
- **Soap bubbles:** **Two surfaces** — **higher excess pressure**
- **Contact angle:** In **wetting**, depends on **surface tension** — **waterproofing fabrics** **increases** the **contact angle**
- **Detergents** and **soaps:** **Reduce surface tension** — **better cleaning action**
- **Lung surfactants:** **Reduce surface tension** in **alveoli**
- **Drops:** **Medicine droppers** and **ink pens** — the **formation of drops**
# 2. Capillarity
- **Capillary rise or fall** — **meniscus formation**
| Liquid in a glass tube | Behaviour | Meniscus |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** (narrow tube) | **Rises** | **Concave** |
| **Mercury** | **Falls** — the level is **depressed** | **Convex** |
- **Examples of capillary action:**
  - **Water** rises in **plants and trees** — **xylem** (against **gravity**)
  - **Ink** absorbed by **blotting paper** — its **pores**
  - **Oil** rises in a **lamp wick**
  - **Moisture** rises in **walls** — **bricks**

## Viscosity, elasticity and compressibility | from: 22
# 1. Viscosity
- **Viscous oils:** **Reduce friction and wear** — **machine lubrication**
- **Streamlined shapes:** **Reduce viscous drag** on **ships and aeroplanes**
- **Blood:** Viscosity affects **blood pressure** and **blood flow**
- **Hydraulic shock absorbers:** **Viscous damping**
- **High-viscosity liquids** — **paint**, **honey** — **flow slowly**
- **Terminal velocity:** **Raindrops** and **parachutes** reach a **constant speed**
# 2. Elasticity
- **Springs** and **shock absorbers:** **Store** and **release** **elastic energy**
- **Bridges and buildings:** **Materials** selected for **high elasticity** or **strength**
- **Rubber bands** and **tyres:** A **high elastic limit** and **resilience**
- **Cranes** and **lifting machines:** **Steel cables** with **high Young's modulus**
- **Musical instrument strings:** Remain under **tension** — **elastic property**
- **Railway tracks:** **Expansion gaps** in **spans** to handle **thermal stress**
# 3. Compressibility — bulk modulus
- **Bulk modulus applications:** The **incompressibility of liquids** and the **compressibility of gases**
- **Hydraulic systems:** **Liquids** with **low compressibility** **transmit force**
- **Submarine hulls:** Must **withstand high pressure** — **materials** with **low compressibility**
- **Speed of sound:** **Depends** on the **compressibility** of the **medium**
- **Gas storage cylinders:** **High-pressure gases** — gases have **high compressibility**
- **Pneumatic shock absorbers:** **Air compressibility** for **cushioning**
