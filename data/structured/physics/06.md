=== TOPIC physics:6 | Light and optics

## Nature of light and media | from: 18
# 1. Dual nature
- Light has a **dual nature**:
  - **Wave nature** — explains **interference**, **diffraction**, **polarisation**
  - **Particle nature** — **photons** — explains the **photoelectric effect** and the **Compton effect**
- **Electromagnetic waves:** **Transverse**; **no medium** needed — light **travels freely** through **vacuum**, at its **maximum speed** there, denoted **c**
# 2. Media
| Medium | Light | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** | **Passes through** | **Glass**, **water**, **air** |
| **Translucent** | **Partially** passes | **Frosted glass**, **butter paper** |
| **Opaque** | **Does not** pass | **Wood**, **metal**, **stone** |
- A medium with a **higher refractive index** is **optically denser**

## Reflection and mirrors | from: 18
# 1. Laws of reflection
- **First law:** The **incident ray**, **reflected ray** and **normal** lie in the **same plane**
- **Second law:** The **angle of incidence** **equals** the **angle of reflection**
- **Normal:** **Perpendicular** to the **surface** at the **point of incidence**
| Reflection | Surface | Image | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular** / **specular** | **Smooth** | **Clear** | **Mirror** |
| **Irregular** / **diffused** | **Rough** | **Not clear** | **Wall**, **paper** |
# 2. Images formed
| Mirror | Image |
|---|---|
| **Plane** | **Virtual**, **erect**, **same size** — **laterally inverted** (**left and right reversed**); **image distance** = **object distance**; **magnification m = +1** |
| **Concave** | **Real and inverted** when the object is **beyond C**; **virtual and erect** when **within F** |
| **Convex** | **Always virtual** and **erect**; **diminished** |
# 3. Uses of mirrors
| Mirror | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Plane** | **Looking glass**, **periscope**, **kaleidoscope** |
| **Concave** | **Shaving** / **makeup mirror** — **magnified erect image** (object within F); **headlights** — a **parallel beam** with the **bulb** at the **focus**; **solar cooker** / **furnace** — **converges sun rays** at the focus; **torch** / **searchlight** — a **strong parallel beam**; **dentist mirror** — **magnified image** of **teeth**; **reflecting telescope** — a **large aperture**, **no chromatic aberration** |
| **Convex** | **Rear-view mirror** — a **wide field of view**, **erect diminished** image; **security** / **shop mirror** — **large-area coverage**; **street lamps** — the **reflector** **diverges light** over a **larger area**; **ATM** or **parking area** — a **wider view** for **safety** |

## Refraction, total internal reflection and optical fibres | from: 18
# 1. Refraction
- **Cause:** A **change** in the **speed of light** — light travels at **different speeds** in **different media** (an **optical-density difference**)
- **Frequency** stays the **same**; **wavelength** **changes**
- **Applications:** **Lenses** **converge** or **diverge** light; **prisms** — **deviation** and **dispersion**; **optical fibres** — **transmit signals**; **spectacles** — **correct eye defects**; **cameras** and **microscopes** — **form images**
- **Apparent depth:** **Objects in water** appear **raised**
# 2. Critical angle and total internal reflection (TIR)
- **Critical angle** — denoted by **C**: The **angle of incidence** in a **denser medium** for which the **angle of refraction** in the **rarer medium** is **90°** — depends on the **pair of media**, and **slightly** on **wavelength**
- **Conditions for TIR:** Light travels from an **optically denser** to a **rarer medium**; the **angle of incidence** **exceeds** the **critical angle** (**i > C**)
- **Result:** The **entire light** is **reflected back** into the **denser medium** — **no refraction**, **100% reflection** in the **ideal case**
- **Based on TIR (TIR devices):**
  - **Optical fibres** — keep light inside the **core**; **communication** and **endoscopy**
  - **Diamond brilliance** / **sparkle** — **multiple TIRs**; a **very small critical angle** of about **24.4°**
  - **Porro prism** / **binoculars** — **image inversion** **without loss**; prisms in **binoculars** or **periscopes** **reflect** light **without silvering**
  - **Endoscope** — **medical viewing**
  - **Mirage** — **atmospheric** TIR
  - **Reflectors** in **road signs** — **high visibility**
  - **Dew drops** **sparkle** — **optical reflection** effects
# 3. Optical fibres
- **Structure:** The **core** has a **higher refractive index** than the **cladding** (which has a **lower** one); the **angle of incidence** at the **core-cladding interface** must satisfy **i > C**
- **Advantages:** **Low loss**; **high bandwidth**; **immune to interference**
- **Uses:**
  - **Telecommunication** — **internet**, **telephone** and **cable-TV** signals
  - **Medical endoscopy** — **viewing internal organs**; **precise laser delivery** in **surgery**
  - **Sensors** — **temperature**, **pressure**, **strain**
  - **Military** and **aircraft** — **lightweight data transmission**
  - **Decorative** and **medical illumination**
- **Endoscope:** A **bundle** of **optical fibres** using **TIR** — **one bundle** for **illumination**, **one** for **image transmission**; **flexible** enough for **body cavities**; **non-invasive** or **minimally invasive** viewing
# 4. Mirage
- Caused by a **continuous decrease or increase** of **refractive index** with **height** — can appear as an **inverted image** of a **distant object**
- **Inferior mirage:** In a **desert** — TIR due to **hot air** near the **ground** (**lower refractive index**); a **water-like appearance**
- **Superior mirage:** TIR due to **cold air layers**
# 5. Diamond
- A **very high refractive index** — about **2.42**; a **very small critical angle** — about **24.4°**
- **Most light** undergoes **TIR** — **multiple internal reflections**; **facets** designed to **maximise** TIR
- **Brilliance and fire:** **TIR** and **dispersion**

## Lenses | from: 18
# 1. Types
| Lens | Shape | Types |
|---|---|---|
| **Convex** | **Thicker** at the **centre** | **Double-convex**, **plano-convex**, **concavo-convex** |
| **Concave** | **Thicker** at the **edges** | **Double-concave**, **plano-concave**, **convexo-concave** |
| **Cylindrical** | — | **Corrects astigmatism** |
| **Bifocal** / **progressive** | — | **Combines two powers** |
# 2. Images by a convex lens
| Object position | Image |
|---|---|
| **Beyond 2F** | **Real**, **inverted**, **diminished** |
| **At 2F** | **Real**, **inverted**, **same-sized** |
| **Between F and 2F** | **Real**, **inverted**, **magnified** |
| **At F** | **Real**, **inverted**, **highly magnified** — at **infinity** |
| **Within F** | **Virtual**, **erect**, **magnified** |
- **Concave lens:** **Virtual**, **erect**, **diminished** for **any object position**
# 3. Uses
| Lens | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Convex** | **Magnifying glass** — a **virtual magnified image** (object within F); **camera** — a **real inverted image** on **film** or a **sensor**; **microscope** and **telescope** **objectives** — a **real image**; **projector** — a **magnified real image** on a **screen**; the **eye lens** — a **real image** on the **retina**; **burning glass** — **converges sun rays** |
| **Concave** | **Spectacles** for **myopia** — **diverges rays** so the image forms on the **retina**; **peephole** / **door viewers** — a **wide field of view**; some **Galileo telescopes** — an **eyepiece** |
| **Combination** | **Microscopes**, **telescopes**, **binoculars** — can **correct** certain **aberrations** |
# 4. Power of a lens
- The **reciprocal** of its **focal length** in **metres** — unit **dioptre** — symbol **D**
- **Convex:** **Positive** power; **concave:** **negative** power

## The human eye and its defects | from: 18
# 1. Parts of the eye
| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cornea** | The **main refraction** — about **70%** of the **focusing** |
| **Iris** | **Controls** **pupil size** — the **amount of light** entering |
| **Pupil** | The **aperture** for **light entry** |
| **Eye lens** | **Fine focusing** through **accommodation** |
| **Ciliary muscles** | **Change** the **curvature** of the **eye lens** |
| **Retina** | The **screen** where the **image** is **formed** — **rods and cones** |
| **Optic nerve** | **Carries signals** to the **brain** |
| **Aqueous** and **vitreous humour** | **Maintain** the **shape** of the eye; **contribute** to **refraction** |
# 2. Accommodation
- The **ability** of the eye to **focus** objects at **different distances** — **ciliary muscles** change the lens **curvature**
- **Near vision:** The lens becomes **thicker** — **more converging**; **distant vision:** **thinner**
- **Range:** From the **near point** (**25 cm**) to the **far point** (**infinity**); **loss** of accommodation causes **presbyopia**
- **Near point:** The **closest distance** the eye **sees clearly** — **normal 25 cm**, the **least distance of distinct vision**
- **Far point:** The **farthest distance** seen clearly — **normal infinity**
# 3. Defects of vision
| Defect | Meaning / cause | Image | Correction | Near / far point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Myopia** — **short-sightedness** | **Distant objects** blurred — an **elongated eyeball** or **excessive converging power** | **In front of** the **retina** | **Concave** (**diverging**) lens — **negative power**, e.g. **–2 D** — diverges rays onto the retina | **Near point** **closer than 25 cm**; **far point** **finite** — **closer than infinity** |
| **Hypermetropia** — **long-sightedness** | **Nearby objects** blurred — a **shortened eyeball** or **insufficient converging power** | **Behind** the **retina** | **Convex** (**converging**) lens — **positive power**, e.g. **+2 D** — converges rays onto the retina | **Near point** **farther than 25 cm**; **far point** **infinity** — affects **near vision** despite it |
| **Presbyopia** | **Age-related loss of accommodation** — **weakening** of the **ciliary muscles**; **loss** of **lens flexibility** | The **near point recedes** (**farther than 25 cm**) | **Bifocal** or **progressive** lenses — the **near portion** **convex** | Can involve a **combination** of **myopia and hypermetropia** in **old age** |
| **Astigmatism** | A **distorted image** — **irregular curvature** of the **cornea or lens**; **different powers** in **different meridians** | **Blurred** or **distorted** vision at **all distances** | **Cylindrical lens**; **toric contact lenses**; **surgery** | — |
| **Cataract** | **Clouding** of the **eye lens** | — | — | — |
| **Colour blindness** | A **genetic defect** in **cones** | — | — | — |

## Dispersion, scattering and atmospheric optics | from: 18
# 1. Dispersion
- **Cause:** **Different wavelengths** have **different refractive indices**
- **Violet:** **Maximum deviation** — the **highest refractive index** in the **visible spectrum**
- **Red:** **Minimum deviation** — the **lowest refractive index**
- **Visible spectrum:** **Continuous** — **VIBGYOR**; a **prism spectrometer** gives a **pure spectrum**; a **rainbow** is a **natural spectrum**
- **Chromatic aberration:** A **defect** in **lenses** — **minimised** by an **achromatic doublet**
- **Spectroscopy:** **Analyses light** from **stars** and **elements**
- **Diamond fire:** **Dispersion** of **white light**
# 2. Rainbow
- **Phenomena:** **Dispersion**, **total internal reflection** and **refraction** in **water drops**
  - **Sunlight enters** a **water drop** and **disperses** → **TIR** at the **back** of the drop → the **dispersed light** **refracts out**
- **Primary rainbow:** **One** TIR — **red outermost** (outer side), **violet innermost** (inner side)
- **Secondary rainbow:** **Two** TIRs — **reversed colours**; **fainter**
- The **Sun** is **behind** the **observer**; **water drops** in **front**
# 3. Scattering
| Type | Particles | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Rayleigh** | **Much smaller** than the **wavelength** — varies as **1/λ⁴** | **Blue sky**; **red sunset** |
| **Mie** | **Larger** particles | **Clouds** (**white clouds**); **milk** |
- **Shorter wavelengths** **scatter more strongly** — by **dust** and **air molecules**
- **Blue sky:** **Blue and violet** are **scattered the most** — the **highest frequencies** among the relevant visible colours; the **eye** is **more sensitive** to **blue** than **violet**, so the **sky** therefore **appears blue** to the **human eye**; **scattered light** reaches the eye from **all directions**
- **Red sunset:** Sunlight travels a **greater distance** through the **atmosphere** — **blue** is **scattered away**; the **remaining red or orange** light reaches the eye; **pollution and dust** **enhance** the **red colour**
# 4. Atmospheric refraction
- **Twinkling stars:** **Air-density variations** cause **continuous changes** in **refractive index** — starlight is **bent randomly**: **random apparent positions** and **fluctuating apparent intensity**
- **Planets do not twinkle:** They appear as **discs** — an **averaging effect**
- **Advance sunrise** and **delayed sunset:** About **two minutes** each
- The **apparent position** of **stars** is affected
# 5. Summary of phenomena
| Phenomenon | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Rainbow** | **Dispersion** and **total internal reflection** in **water drops** |
| **Blue sky**, **red sunset** | **Rayleigh scattering** |
| **Mirage** | **Total internal reflection** |
| **Twinkling of stars** | **Atmospheric refraction** |
| **Eclipse** | **Shadow formation** — the **rectilinear propagation** of light |
