=== TOPIC physics:2 | Gravitation and satellites

## Gravity and the value of g | from: 23
# 1. Effects of gravity
- **Weight:** The **force** with which **Earth attracts** a body
- **Free fall:** The **acceleration** of a **freely falling body** — equals **g** when **air resistance** is **neglected**; in a **vacuum** **all bodies** fall with the **same acceleration**, **independent of mass**
- **Orbital motion:** Keeps **planets** and **satellites** in **orbit**
- **Tides:** The **differential gravitational pull** of the **Moon** and the **Sun**
- **Earth's shape:** **Slightly oblate** — a **bulge** at the **equator**, due to **rotation** and **gravity**
- **Atmosphere:** Gravity **prevents** it from **escaping**
- **Direction:** Gravitational acceleration is **always towards** the **centre of Earth**
# 2. Variation of g
- **Maximum** at the **poles**, **minimum** at the **equator** — the poles are **closer** to Earth's **centre**
- **Decreases** with **altitude** and with **depth**; **g = 0** at the **centre of Earth**; **approaches zero** at **very large height**
- **Earth's rotation** **slightly reduces** g — the **centrifugal effect**
| Place | Distance from centre | Centrifugal effect | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Poles** | **Minimum** | **Zero** | **Maximum** on the surface — **greater** than at the equator |
| **Equator** | **Maximum** | **Maximum reduction** | **Slightly less** than at the poles |

## Mass, weight and weightlessness | from: 23
# 1. Mass vs weight
| Basis | Mass | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | **Inertia** — the **amount of matter** | A **force** — given by the formula **W = mg** |
| Nature | A **scalar** quantity | A **force vector** |
| Varies? | **Remains constant** | **Depends** on **location** |
| SI unit | **Kilogram (kg)** | **Newton (N)** |
| In weightlessness | **Unchanged** | **Zero** |
# 2. Weight at different places
- **Earth's surface:** **Maximum** under **normal conditions** — maximum at the **poles**
- **Height** / **depth:** Weight **decreases** with **height above** and **depth below** the surface; **zero** at the **centre of Earth**; **approaches zero** at very large height and in **far space**
- **Moon:** About **one-sixth** of a person's **weight on Earth**
# 3. Weightlessness
- **Apparent weight** is **zero** inside a **freely falling elevator** and an **orbiting satellite** — in **free fall** the **normal force** becomes **zero**; apparent weight **N** is **zero** when **effective gravity** is zero
- An **orbiting satellite** and its **astronaut** both **accelerate towards Earth** with the **same g**
- **Astronauts** in **space stations** experience **continuous free fall**
- **Zero-g training:** **Aircraft parabolic flights**
- **Microgravity experiments:** **Fluid behaviour**, **crystal growth**, **human physiology**; **satellite instruments** are **designed** for a **weightless environment**

## Satellite orbits | from: 23
| Orbit | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Low Earth orbit** | **160 km to 2000 km** — the **International Space Station** about **400 km** above Earth |
| **Geostationary** | About **36,000 km** above the **equator** — a **period** of **24 hours** |
| **Polar** | **Passes over** the **poles** |
| **Elliptical** | **Different** **apogee** and **perigee** distances |
| **Circular** | **Centripetal force** **equals** **gravitational force** |
