=== TOPIC physics:5 | Waves and sound

## Types of waves | from: 20
# 1. Classification
| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| **Medium** | **Mechanical** and **electromagnetic** waves |
| **Direction of vibration** | **Transverse** and **longitudinal** waves |
| **Energy propagation** | **Progressive** and **stationary** waves |
| **Dimension** | **One-**, **two-** and **three-dimensional** waves |
| **Others** | **Matter waves** — **de Broglie**; **shock waves** |
# 2. Medium
- **Mechanical waves:** Require a **material medium** — a **solid, liquid or gas**; e.g. **sound**, **waves on a string**, **water surface waves**
- **Electromagnetic waves:** **No medium** — **travel** in a **vacuum**; e.g. **light**; **all** electromagnetic waves are **transverse**
- **Matter waves:** Associated with **moving particles** — **no medium** needed
# 3. Transverse or longitudinal
| Wave | Type |
|---|---|
| **Sound** in **air** | **Longitudinal** — travels through **gas** |
| **Waves on a string**; **water surface waves** | **Transverse** |
| **Seismic waves** | **P-waves** (**primary** — travel through **solids, liquids and gases**) and **S-waves** (**secondary**) — **both** longitudinal and transverse |
| **Spring** / **slinky** | **Longitudinal** or **transverse** — **compression waves** in a **solid** |
| **Ultrasonic waves** | **Longitudinal** — in **various media** |
| **Pressure waves** | Travel through **fluids** |
# 4. Electromagnetic wavelengths
| Wave | Wavelength |
|---|---|
| **Radio waves** | **Greater than 1 metre** |
| **Microwaves** | **1 millimetre to 1 metre** |
| **Infrared** | **700 nanometres to 1 millimetre** |
| **Visible light** | **400 to 700 nanometres** |
| **Ultraviolet** | **10 to 400 nanometres** |
| **X-rays** | **0.01 to 10 nanometres** |
| **Gamma rays** | **Less than 0.01 nanometre** |

## Wave properties, energy and uses | from: 20
# 1. Amplitude, velocity and wavelength
- **Higher amplitude:** **Louder sound**; **brighter light** — **energy** and **intensity** **proportional** to the **square of amplitude**; ⚠ **no effect** on **wave speed**, **frequency** or **wavelength**
- **Velocity:** **Independent** of **frequency** in the **same medium** — **changes** with the **properties** of **different media**; the **speed of light** is the **same** for **all frequencies** in a **vacuum**
- **Wavelength:** At the **same frequency**, **higher velocity** gives a **longer wavelength** (so **faster sound** has a longer wavelength); in the **same medium**, velocity is **constant** and **wavelength** is **inversely proportional** to **frequency**
- **Sound** travels **faster** in **solids** than in the **other listed media**
# 2. Energy
- Waves **transfer energy** **without transferring matter**
- **Wave energy** ∝ the **square of amplitude** **multiplied** by the **square of frequency**
- **Intensity:** **Power per unit area** — ∝ **square of amplitude**
- **Mechanical waves:** Energy from **particle oscillation**; **electromagnetic waves:** from **oscillating electric and magnetic fields**
# 3. Uses
| Wave | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Sound** | **Communication**, **sonar**, **medical ultrasound** |
| **Light** | **Vision**, **optical fibres**, **lasers** |
| **Radio waves** | **Broadcasting**; **mobile communication** |
| **Microwaves** | **Cooking**, **radar**, **satellite communication** |
| **X-rays** | **Medical imaging**; **security scanning** |
| **Ultrasonic waves** | **Cleaning**, **echocardiography**, **ranging** |

## Interference, diffraction, polarization and Doppler effect | from: 20, 19
# 1. Interference
- **Young's double-slit experiment:** **Constructive** and **destructive** interference of **light**
- **Thin-film interference:** **Colours** in **soap bubbles** and **oil films**
- **Beats:** **Interference** of **two close frequencies** of sound
- **Destructive interference:** **Anti-reflective coatings**; **noise-cancelling headphones**
# 2. Diffraction
- **Bending of light** around an **obstacle** — light **enters** the **geometrical shadow**
- **Sound** is heard **around corners** — sound waves **diffract easily** because of their **long wavelengths**; ⚠ **light** **cannot** be seen clearly around corners — **very small wavelength**
- **Single-slit pattern:** A **central bright fringe** and **secondary fringes**
- **Diffraction grating:** Produces **spectra**
- **Fresnel diffraction:** **Near-field** patterns; **Fraunhofer diffraction:** **far-field** patterns
# 3. Polarization
- **Polaroid sunglasses:** **Reduce glare** — **cut reflected light**; **photography** uses **glare-reduction filters**
- **Three-dimensional (3D) movies:** Use **separate images** for **each eye**
- **LCD screens:** **Control light transmission**
- **Stress analysis:** **Photoelasticity** shows **stress patterns**
- **Optical activity:** **Measures** the **concentration** of **solutions**
- **Communication:** **Polarization multiplexing** in **optical fibres**
# 4. Doppler effect
- The **apparent change** in **frequency** due to **relative motion** between a **source** and an **observer**
  - **Approaching** source: **Higher** apparent **frequency** and **pitch**
  - **Receding** source: **Lower** apparent frequency and pitch
- **Applications:**
  - **Radar speed guns** (**police radar**): **Vehicle** / **traffic speed**
  - **Medical Doppler ultrasound:** **Blood-flow velocity**
  - **Astronomy:** **Red shift** and **blue shift** — the **expanding universe** and **star motion** (Doppler effect for **light**)
  - **SONAR:** **Speed** of **submarines** and **underwater objects**
  - **Weather radar:** **Storms** and **wind speed**
  - **Bats:** **Echolocation** with **frequency shifts**
  - **Sirens** demonstrate it

## Sound — nature, speed and characteristics | from: 19
# 1. Nature
- A **mechanical**, **longitudinal** wave — **compressions** and **rarefactions**
- Requires a **material medium** — **solids, liquids and gases**; **cannot travel** through a **vacuum** (speed **zero** — **no medium**)
# 2. Speed of sound
| Medium (20–25°C) | Approximate speed |
|---|---|
| **Air** | **340–343 m/s** |
| **Water** | **1480–1500 m/s** |
| **Steel** / **iron** | **5000–6000 m/s** |
| **Glass** | **5000–6000 m/s** |
| **Vacuum** | **Zero** |
- **Order:** **Solid > liquid > gas** in general
  - **Solids:** **Fastest** — **high elasticity** / **bulk modulus**; molecules **closely packed** — a **train** sound is **heard better** through **rails** than **air**
  - **Liquids:** **Intermediate** — **less elastic** than solids
  - **Gases:** **Slowest** — **low elasticity**; molecules **far apart**
- **Temperature:** Speed **increases** with temperature — about **0.61 m/s per 1°C**; about **331 m/s** at **0°C** and **343 m/s** at **20°C**
# 3. Frequency, wavelength and amplitude
- **Frequency:** The **number of vibrations** (**compressions**) **per second** — unit **hertz (Hz)**; **determines pitch**
- **Audible range:** **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz** (**20 kHz**)
- **Wavelength:** The **distance** between **two successive compressions or rarefactions** — **higher frequency**, **smaller wavelength**
  - At **340 m/s** in air: **20 Hz** → **17 m**; **20 kHz** → **1.7 cm**
- **Amplitude:** The **maximum displacement** of particles from their **mean position** — unit **metre (m)**; **determines loudness** and **intensity**; **energy** ∝ **square of amplitude**; ⚠ **no effect** on **speed**, **frequency** or **pitch**
# 4. Pitch, loudness and quality
| Characteristic | Depends on | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** | **Frequency** — **directly proportional** | The **sensation of frequency** in the **brain**; **independent** of **amplitude** and **loudness**; **low frequency** — **bass / deep** sound (**male voice**); **high frequency** — **shrill** (**female voice**, **whistle**) |
| **Loudness** | **Amplitude** — **directly related** | The **sensation** of **intensity** or **amplitude**; unit **decibel (dB)**; **independent** of **frequency** and **pitch** |
| **Quality** / **timbre** | The **waveform** — **overtones** and **harmonics** present; the **shape** of the **periodic waveform** | **Distinguishes** **two sounds** of the **same pitch and loudness** — the **same note** on a **flute, violin and piano** sounds **different**; **more overtones** → **richer quality** |

## Ultrasound, infrasound, SONAR and echo | from: 19
# 1. Ranges
- **Infrasonic:** **Below 20 Hz**; **ultrasonic:** **above 20 kHz**
- **Human hearing:** Most **sensitive** around **2000–4000 Hz**; the **limit decreases with age** — **age-related hearing loss** especially affects **higher frequencies**
# 2. Uses of ultrasound
- **Medical imaging** — **sonography**; **echocardiography** — **images** the **heart**
- **Cleaning** **delicate parts** and **jewellery**
- **Industry:** **Detects cracks** or **flaws** in **metals**; **welding and drilling** in **precision machining**
- **SONAR:** **Underwater detection**
- **Pest control:** **Drives away** **insects and rodents**
# 3. Infrasound
- **Elephants** and **whales:** **Communication**
- **Seismic waves** contain **infrasonic frequencies** — **earthquake detection**
- **Weather:** **Monitoring**; **detects storms and tornadoes**; study of the **atmosphere**
- **Military:** **Detects** **nuclear explosions** and **missiles**
- **Animals** can **sense** it **before natural disasters**; it **propagates** over **long distances**
# 4. SONAR — Sound Navigation And Ranging
- **Echo method** — **reflection** of **ultrasonic waves**: **sends ultrasonic pulses** toward an **object**, which **reflects** them; **measures** the **time interval** between **sending** and **receiving**
- **Why ultrasound:** **High frequency**, **less diffraction**, **greater directionality**
- **Uses:** **Detecting submarines** (a **military** use); **ocean depth**; **mapping the seabed**; **locating fish** (**fishing industry**); **underwater navigation** by **ships and submarines**; **locating wrecks** and **sunken ships or objects**; **limited** use in **underwater communication**
# 5. Echo
- **Uses:** **SONAR** — **distance and depth** measurement; **medical ultrasound** — **imaging internal organs**; measuring the **speed of sound** (**echo time**); **architectural acoustics** — **designing halls** for **reverberation control**
- **Echolocation:** **Bats** and **dolphins** — **navigation**; **radar** uses a **similar principle** with **electromagnetic waves**
- **Minimum distance** for an echo: **17.2 m** — based on the ear's **persistence of hearing** of **0.1 s**

## Musical instruments | from: 19
| Type | Examples | Sound from |
|---|---|---|
| **String** | **Guitar**, **sitar**, **violin** | **Vibration of strings** — **transverse standing waves** |
| **Wind** | **Flute**, **clarinet**, **shehnai** | **Vibration** of an **air column** — a **longitudinal standing wave** |
| **Membrane** | **Tabla**, **drum** | A **stretched membrane** |
| **Plate** | **Cymbals**, **bells** | **Plates** or **solids** |
| **Electronic** | **Electronic oscillators** and **speakers** | — |
- **Pitch:** Controlled by the **frequency of vibration**
  - **String:** **Length**, **tension** and **thickness** of the **string**
  - **Wind:** **Length** of its **air column**
- **Loudness:** The **amplitude of vibration** — depends on the **force** of **striking or blowing**
- **Quality / timbre:** **Overtones** or **harmonics** — depend on the **shape and material** of the instrument
- **Pipes:** A **closed pipe** produces **only odd harmonics**; an **open pipe** produces **all harmonics**
