=== TOPIC physics:4 | Heat and temperature

## Heat, temperature and scales | from: 21
# 1. Heat vs temperature
- **Heat:** A **form of energy** that **flows** due to a **temperature difference** — from **higher** to **lower temperature**
  - **Depends** on **mass**, **specific heat** and **temperature change**
- **Temperature:** Measures the **hotness or coldness** of a body — related to the **average kinetic energy** of **molecules**
- **Thermal equilibrium:** Bodies at the **same temperature** — **no net heat flow**
# 2. Units
- **SI unit** of **heat** or **energy:** The **joule (J)**
- **One calorie** = **4.186 joules** (about **4.2 joules**); **one kilocalorie** = **4186 joules**
- **Mechanical equivalent of heat:** **4.186 joules per calorie**
# 3. Temperature scales
| Scale | Symbol | Ice point | Steam point | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Celsius** | **°C** | **0°C** | **100°C** | — |
| **Kelvin** | **K** | **273.15 K** | — | An **absolute scale** — **starts** at **absolute zero** |
| **Fahrenheit** | **°F** | **32°F** | **212°F** | — |
| **Rankine** | **°R** | — | — | An **absolute Fahrenheit** scale |
- **Conversion:** **Kelvin** temperature **equals** the **Celsius** temperature **plus 273.15** — approximately **K = °C + 273**
  - **27°C** ≈ **300 K**; **0°C** ≈ **273 K**; **–273°C** ≈ **0 K**
# 4. Absolute zero
- **0 K** = **–273.15°C** = **–459.67°F** — the **lowest possible temperature**
- **Molecular motion** is **minimum**; **unattainable** according to the **Third Law of Thermodynamics**
- For an **ideal gas**, **volume** and **pressure** **theoretically** become **zero**
- The **starting point** of the **Kelvin scale**

## Transfer of heat | from: 21
# 1. Modes
| Mode | How | Medium |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduction** | **Direct contact** — **no bulk motion** | Requires a **material medium** — **best** in **solids** |
| **Convection** | **Actual movement** of **fluid particles** | Requires a **fluid medium** — **liquids and gases** |
| **Radiation** | **Electromagnetic waves** | **No medium** needed — occurs in a **vacuum** |
# 2. Examples
- **Conduction:**
  - A **metal spoon** in **hot tea** becomes **hot**
  - **Heating one end** of a **metal rod** makes the **other end** hot
  - **Metal bases** of **cooking utensils** conduct heat to food
  - **Wood** and **plastic** — **poor conductors**, used for **insulation**
- **Convection:**
  - **Boiling water** — **hot water rises**, **cold water sinks** — **convection currents**
  - **Sea breeze** and **land breeze** — **air movement** due to a **temperature difference**
  - **Room heaters** and **air conditioners** **circulate air**
  - **Hot air balloons** rise — **hot air rises**
- **Radiation:**
  - **Heat** from the **Sun** reaches **Earth** through a **vacuum**
  - Feeling **heat** from a **fire** or **heater** **without touching** it
  - A **black body** — a **perfect absorber** and **perfect emitter** of radiation
  - **Shiny walls** in a **thermos flask** **minimize radiation loss**

## Thermal expansion and the anomaly of water | from: 21
# 1. Applications of expansion
- **Railway tracks:** **Gaps** left for **thermal expansion**
- **Bridges:** **Rollers** or **expansion joints**
- **Thermometers:** **Liquid expansion** measures **temperature**
- **Bimetallic strips:** **Thermostats**, **electric irons**, **fire alarms**
- **Hot rivets:** **Contract** on **cooling** — a **tight joint**
- **Overflow pipes** in **tanks:** Allow for the **expansion of liquid**
# 2. Anomalous expansion of water
- Water **contracts** when **heated** from **0°C to 4°C**
- **Maximum density** — **minimum volume** — at **4°C**; density **decreases** **below** and **above** 4°C
- **Lakes and ponds** **freeze** from the **top** — **aquatic life** **survives**

## Specific heat and latent heat | from: 21
# 1. High specific heat of water
- **Coolant:** Water **absorbs large heat** with a **small temperature change**
- **Coastal areas:** A **moderate climate**
- **Hot water bottles:** **Retain heat** for a **longer time**
- **Land vs sea:** **Land** has a **lower specific heat** than **sea water** — **heats and cools faster**
- Substances with **high specific heat** **cook more slowly**
# 2. Latent heat
| Change | Latent heat | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Fusion of ice** | **3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg** — **80 cal/g** | **Melting point** **0°C** at **1 atmosphere**; **temperature constant** during **melting** |
| **Vaporisation of water** | **2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg** — **540 cal/g** | **Boiling point** **100°C** at **1 atmosphere**; **temperature constant** during **boiling**; boiling point **increases** with **pressure** |
- **Ice** cools: Its **high latent heat of fusion** **absorbs** a **large amount** of heat
- **Steam** burns **more severely** than **boiling water:** **High latent heat of vaporisation**
- **Refrigeration:** Uses **latent heat absorbed** during **evaporation**
- **Perspiration:** Its **evaporation** **cools** the body
- **Clouds and rain:** **Latent heat released** during **condensation**

## Evaporation, refrigeration and heat engines | from: 21
# 1. Evaporation
- **Faster molecules** **escape** from a liquid — the **average kinetic energy** of the **remaining molecules** **decreases** → **cooling**
- **Examples:** **Sweating**; water in an **earthen pot**; **desert coolers**
- **Increased by:** **Higher temperature**, **larger surface area**, **lower humidity**, **wind**
- A **surface phenomenon** occurring at **all temperatures** — this is how it **differs** from **boiling**
# 2. Refrigerator
- **Extracts heat** from the **cold body inside** and **rejects** it to the **hot surroundings** — based on the **Second Law of Thermodynamics**; **external work** is **required**
- **Cycle:** **Evaporation → compression → condensation → expansion**
- **Liquid refrigerant** **evaporates** at **low pressure** — **absorbs latent heat**; it **absorbs heat** during **evaporation** and **releases** heat during **condensation**
- **Refrigerants:**
  - **Earlier:** **Ammonia**; **Freon** — **Freons** are **CFCs**, now **phased out**
  - **Modern:** **HFCs**, **HCs**, **R-134a**, **R-600a**
  - **Desirable:** **Low boiling point**, **high latent heat**, **non-toxic**, **non-flammable**, **stable**
# 3. Heat engine
- **Converts** **heat energy** into **mechanical work** — **absorbs heat** from a **source**, **does work**, and **rejects heat** to a **sink**
- **Requires:** A **hot source**, a **cold sink** and a **working substance**
- Based on the **First Law** and **Second Law** of **Thermodynamics**
