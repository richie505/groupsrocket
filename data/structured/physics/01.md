=== TOPIC physics:1 | Force, energy and machines

## Force and its types | from: 14
# 1. Balanced and unbalanced force
- **Unbalanced force:** **Changes** an object's **state of motion** — can **accelerate**, **decelerate** or **change** its **direction**
- **Balanced force:** **No change** in the state of motion — the object **remains at rest** or moves with **uniform velocity**
# 2. Types of force
| Force | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Frictional** | **Opposes** **relative motion** or the **tendency of motion** |
| **Gravitational** | **Attracts** objects **towards each other** — **weight** is a gravitational force |
| **Centripetal** | Keeps an object in a **circular path** — acts **towards the centre** |
| **Tension** | **Pulls** along a **string or rope** |
| **Normal** | A **perpendicular push** by a **surface** |
| **Spring** | A **restoring force** — follows **Hooke's law** |
# 3. Units of force and weight
- **SI unit of force:** The **newton (N)** — **one newton** = **one kilogram metre per second squared** = **10⁵ dynes**; other units: **dyne**, **kilogram-force (kgf)**, **poundal**
- **SI unit of weight:** The **newton (N)**; also **kilogram-force (kgf)** — **one kgf** is about **9.8 newtons**

## Newton's laws and momentum | from: 14
# 1. Newton's laws
| Law | Applications / examples |
|---|---|
| **First law** | **Seat belts**; **sudden braking** effects; objects **at rest** **staying at rest** — a **passenger lurches forward** when a **bus stops suddenly**; a **book** remains on a **table** until **pushed** |
| **Second law** — **F = ma** | **Designing vehicles**; calculating the **acceleration** of **rockets and lifts**; a **heavier suitcase** needs **more force** to accelerate; pushing an **empty** vs a **loaded cart** |
| **Third law** | **Rocket propulsion**, **walking**, **swimming**, **gun recoil**, **jet engines** |
# 2. Action and reaction pairs
| Action | Reaction |
|---|---|
| A **walking foot** **pushes** the **ground backward** | The **ground** **pushes** the **foot forward** |
| A **rocket** **expels gases downward** (**rocket gases push down**) | The **gases** **push** the **rocket upward** — the rocket **moves up** |
| A **gun** **pushes** a **bullet forward** | The **bullet** **pushes** the **gun backward** — **gun recoil** (a gun **recoils** when a bullet is **fired**) |
| A **bird** **pushes air downward** | The **air** **pushes** the **bird upward** |
| A **swimmer** **pushes water backward** | The **water** **pushes** the **swimmer forward** |
| A **hammer** **hits** a **nail** | The **nail** **hits** the **hammer** with **equal force** |
| The **Earth** **pulls** an **apple downward** | The **apple** **pulls** the **Earth upward** — giving the Earth a **very small acceleration** |
# 3. Conservation of momentum
- The **total linear momentum** of an **isolated system** is **conserved** when **no external force** acts
- **Collision:** **Total momentum before** = **total momentum after**
- **Explosion:** Total momentum **remains zero** if the system was **initially at rest**
- **Gun recoil:** The **gun and bullet** system's momentum is **conserved** — the **bullet's forward momentum** = the **gun's backward momentum**
- **Rocket:** The **rocket and exhaust gases** momentum is **conserved** — **exhaust gases** gain **backward momentum**; the **rocket** gains **forward momentum**
- **Jet engines:** **High-speed exhaust** produces a **large momentum change**
- **Impulse in daily life:** **Vehicle safety design** uses **collision principles**; **airbags** **increase** the **time of impact** and **decrease force**; **catching a ball** by **drawing the hands back** **increases** the **time interval**

## Work, energy and power | from: 14
# 1. Units
- **Work:** SI unit the **joule (J)** — **one joule** = **one newton metre** = **one kilogram metre squared per second squared** = **10⁷ ergs**; each **another unit** of work: **erg**, **electronvolt (eV)**, **calorie**, **kilowatt-hour (kWh)**
- **One electronvolt** = **1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules**
- **Power:** SI unit the **watt (W)** — **one watt** = **one joule per second**; other units: **horsepower (hp)**, **kilowatt (kW)**, **megawatt (MW)**; **one horsepower** = **746 watts**
# 2. Forms of energy
- **Kinetic energy:** **Translational** or **rotational**
- **Potential energy:** **Gravitational**, **elastic** / **spring**, **electric**, **chemical**, **nuclear**
- **Mechanical energy** equals **kinetic energy** plus **potential energy**
- **Thermal** / **heat energy:** **Internal energy** due to **molecular motion**
- **Other types:** **Sound**, **light**, **electrical**, **magnetic**, **nuclear**
# 3. Energy conversion
| Device / process | Converts |
|---|---|
| **Generator** / **dynamo** | **Mechanical → electrical** |
| **Electric motor** | **Electrical → mechanical** |
| **Battery** / **cell** | **Chemical → electrical** |
| **LED** / **bulb** | **Electrical → light** |
| **Solar cell** | **Light → electrical** |
| **Nuclear power plant** | **Nuclear → heat → electrical** |
| **Free fall**, **pendulum**, **roller coaster** | **Potential → kinetic** |
| **Friction in brakes** | **Kinetic → heat** |
# 4. Conservation of energy
- Energy can **neither be created nor destroyed** — it can **only be transformed**
- **Mechanical energy conservation:** **KE + PE = constant** — when **only conservative forces** act
- **Applications:** **Pendulum**, **free fall**, **spring-mass system**, **ideal collisions**

## Friction | from: 14
# 1. Types
| Type | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Static friction** | **Opposes** the **tendency of motion** — **self-adjusting**; **maximum** = **μₛN** |
| **Kinetic** / **sliding friction** | **Opposes** **actual sliding motion** — **μₖN** |
| **Rolling friction** | **Opposes rolling** — **much smaller** than **sliding friction** |
| **Fluid friction** / **viscous drag** | **Opposes motion** in **fluids** |
# 2. Friction is useful
- **Walking and running:** The **forward push** — friction **enables walking**; **without friction**, people **slip**
- **Writing:** **Grip** between a **pen and paper** — friction helps in **writing and holding**
- **Vehicles:** **Move and stop** — **braking** converts **kinetic energy** to **heat**; **traction and braking** are **advantages**
- **Grip:** **Prevents** objects **slipping**
- **Useful heat:** **Ignites a matchstick**; **drilling**
- **Belts and pulleys:** **Power transmission** — **belt drives**
# 3. Friction is harmful
- **Wear and tear** in **machine parts** and **tyres**; surfaces become **rough** over **time**
- **Unwanted heat** — **overheating** of **engines and brakes**
- **Reduces efficiency** — **energy loss** in machines; **opposes motion**; **extra fuel consumption** in vehicles

## Circular motion and simple machines | from: 14
# 1. Centripetal vs centrifugal
| Basis | Centripetal force | Centrifugal force |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | A **real force** **towards the centre** — provides the **acceleration** for **circular motion** | A **pseudo force** in a **rotating frame** — acts **away from the centre** |
| Provided by / examples | **Tension**, **gravity**, **friction**, **electrostatic** and **magnetic** force | **Clothes** and **water** thrown **outward** in a **washing machine**; **mud** flies off a **rotating wheel**; **particles** move **outward** in a rotating frame |
# 2. What provides centripetal force
| Situation | Provided by |
|---|---|
| **Vehicle** on a **level circular road** | **Friction** |
| **Vehicle** on a **banked road** | The **component** of **normal force** and **friction** |
| **Satellite** and **planetary motion** | **Gravitational force** |
| **Stone** tied to a **string** | **Tension** |
| **Electron** around a **nucleus** | **Electrostatic attraction** |
| **Loop-the-loop** | **Gravity** and **normal force** |
# 3. Outward effects
- **Merry-go-round:** A **feeling** of being **pushed outward** — a **pseudo force**
- **Centrifuge:** **Separates** **cream** or **blood** — **denser particles** **move outward**
- **Planetary rings** and the **shape of Earth** — **Earth bulges** at the **equator**
# 4. Simple machines
| Machine | Used in |
|---|---|
| **Lever** | **Scissors**, **see-saw**, **crowbar**, **bottle opener**, the **human forearm** |
| **Inclined plane** | **Ramps**, **slopes**, **screws**, **wedges** |
| **Pulley** | **Lifting heavy loads** — **cranes**, **flagpoles**, **elevators** |
| **Wheel and axle** | **Steering wheel**, **door knobs**, **screwdrivers** |
| **Screw jack** | **Lifts cars** or **heavy vehicles** |
| **Gears** | **Bicycles**, **clocks**, **automobiles** — **convert** **speed and torque** |
