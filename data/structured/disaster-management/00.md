=== TOPIC disaster-management:0 | Disaster concepts, phases and vulnerability

## Crisis life cycle and disaster management phases | from: 2
# 1. Crisis management life cycle — three phases
| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Pre-crisis** | **Prevention and mitigation**; **preparedness**; **risk reduction** |
| **During crisis** | **Immediate response**; **rescue**; **relief**; **crisis handling** |
| **Post-crisis** | **Recovery** → **rehabilitation** → **reconstruction** |
# 2. The phases in detail
- Disaster management has **four phases**
| Phase | When | Aim | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prevention** | **Before** a disaster | **Stop** a **hazard** from becoming a **disaster** — **proactive and long-term** | **Land-use zoning**; **regulating deforestation**; **controlling industrial siting** |
| **Mitigation** | **Before** a disaster | **Reduce** **disaster risk** and the **severity** / **impact**; **minimise future losses** — the **most cost-effective** phase | **Earthquake-resistant buildings**, **embankments**, **building codes**, **afforestation**, **insurance**, **hazard mapping** |
| **Preparedness** / **preparation** | **Before** a disaster | **Readiness** — ensures a **ready** and **effective response**; **capacity building**; the **first and critical step** | **Preparedness planning**, **(Early) Warning Systems**, **evacuation arrangements**, **community preparedness**, **emergency resources**, **coordination**, **mock drills**, **stockpiling**, **training** through **Aapda Mitra**, **Emergency Operation Centres**, **contingency plans** |
| **Response** | **During** or **immediately** / **just after** | **Immediate action** — **save lives**; **meet basic needs**; **time-critical** | **Search and rescue**, **evacuation**, **first aid**, **temporary shelters**, **food and water**, **law and order** |
| **Recovery** | **After** a disaster | **Restore normal life** / **normalcy**; **reduce future vulnerability**; **Build Back Better** — **long-term**; **builds resilience** | **Rehabilitation**, **reconstruction**, **livelihood support**, **psycho-social care**, **resilient infrastructure** |
- **Risk assessment** leads to **mitigation**
- **Non-structural mitigation:** **Community awareness** and **education** programmes
- **Cyclone mitigation:** **Planting trees** along **coastal areas**; **earthquake mitigation:** **reframing** and **implementing** **building-construction guidelines**
# 3. Response
- The **community** is usually the **first responder**
- **Urban response:** **Civic authorities**, the **fire brigade**, **local police** — the **district administration** has the **basic responsibility**; the **Collector** plays a **pivotal role**
- **Search and Rescue:** A **major role** in the **post-disaster** scenario
# 4. Stages
| Stage | Covers | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-disaster** | **Prevention**, **mitigation**, **preparedness** | **Risk reduction** and **readiness** |
| **During-disaster** | **Response** | **Immediate life-saving action** |
| **Post-disaster** (**post-crisis**) | **Recovery**, **rehabilitation**, **reconstruction** | **Rebuild better** and **reduce future risk** |

## Shift in approach — 3Rs to 3Ps | from: 2
| Basis | Pre-2005 | Post-DM Act 2005 |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | The **three Rs** — **rescue**, **relief**, **rehabilitation** | The **three Ps** — **prevention**, **preparedness**, **proofing** / **mitigation** |
| Nature | **Relief-centric**; **reactive** | **Holistic**; **multi-phase**; a **continuous cycle**; **proactive** — **Build Back Better** |
- The **disaster management cycle** is **continuous** — **lessons** from **recovery** lead to **better prevention**
- **Sendai Framework (2015–2030):** **Global alignment** for disaster management

## Classification of hazards | from: 20
# 1. Broad classes
| Class | Hazards |
|---|---|
| **Geological** | **Earthquake**, **landslide**, **tsunami** |
| **Hydro-meteorological** / **atmospheric** | **Cyclone**, **flood**, **drought**, **thunderstorm-related** hazards, **hailstorm** |
| **Man-made** / **human-induced** | **Fire**, **industrial or chemical**, **nuclear**, **biological** disasters |
# 2. Natural hazard subtypes
| Subtype | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Geophysical** | **Earthquake**, **tsunami**, **landslide**, **volcanic eruption** |
| **Hydrological** | **Flood**, **flash flood**, **avalanche** |
| **Meteorological** | **Cyclone**, **hurricane**, **tornado**, **thunderstorm**, **hailstorm** |
| **Climatological** | **Drought**, **heat wave**, **cold wave**, **forest fire** |
| **Biological** | **Epidemics**, **pest attacks** |
# 3. Man-made / anthropogenic subtypes
| Subtype | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Technological** | **Industrial accidents**, **chemical leaks**, **nuclear accidents**, **dam failure** |
| **Transport** | **Air**, **rail**, **road** and **ship** accidents |
| **Social** / **complex** | **Terrorism**, **riots**, **war**, **civil strife** |
- **Socio-natural hazards:** A **combination** of **natural and human factors** — **floods** **worsened** by **deforestation**; **landslides** caused by **unscientific construction**
# 4. Institutional notes
- **Ministry of Home Affairs:** The **nodal ministry** for **overall coordination**
- **Key approach:** Shifted from **hazard-centric** management to **multi-hazard risk reduction**
- **Sendai's first priority:** **Understanding disaster risk**, including **hazards**
- **High Powered Committee (1999):** **Classified** disasters into **five broad categories**

## Vulnerability and vulnerable groups | from: 32
# 1. Types of vulnerability
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | **Susceptibility** of **buildings**, **infrastructure** and **physical assets** | **Weak houses** in **earthquake zones**; **settlements** in **floodplains** |
| **Social** | Based on **age**, **gender**, **caste**, **disability**, **social status** | **Women**, **children**, **elderly**, **SC/ST communities**, **persons with disabilities** |
| **Economic** | **Poverty**; **lack of livelihood options**; **low income** | **Daily wage workers**, **small farmers**, **informal sector workers** |
| **Environmental** | **Degradation** of **natural systems** that **increases risk** | **Deforestation** → **landslides**; **loss of mangroves** → greater **cyclone impact** |
| **Systemic** / **institutional** | **Weak governance**; **poor coordination**; **lack of preparedness** | **Inadequate early warning systems**; **delayed response** |
# 2. Vulnerable groups in India
| Basis | Groups |
|---|---|
| **Age** | **Children** — especially **unaccompanied** children; **elderly people** |
| **Gender** | **Women** — **pregnant**, **lactating**, **single women**, **widows**; **transgender persons** |
| **Disability** | **Persons with Disabilities (PwD)** |
| **Socio-economic** | **Poor people**, **SC/ST communities**, **informal sector workers**, **homeless people** |
| **Location** | People in **high-risk zones** — **coastal areas**, **Seismic Zone V**, **flood plains**, **hilly areas** |
| **Others** | **Migrants**, **refugees**, **remote tribal communities** |
# 3. NDMA focus areas
- **Disability-inclusive DRR:** The **NDMA Guidelines on Disability Inclusive Disaster Risk Reduction** (**2019**)
- **School safety:** A **special focus** on **children**
- **Gender mainstreaming:** **Women's leadership** and **participation** in DRR — part of the **PM's 10-Point Agenda**
- **Minimum standards of relief:** **Special provisions** for **vulnerable groups**
- **Community-based DRR:** **Including** the **most vulnerable** in **planning**
